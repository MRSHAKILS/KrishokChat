# Early Blight on Potato

Alternaria solani

|   | Prevention | Monitoring | Direct Control | Direct Control | Restrictions  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
|  Symptoms of early blight on potato. Photo: Howard F. Schwartz, Colorado State University, Bugwood.com | - Plant tolerant varieties when available - Select site with good drainage and avoid fields bordered by trees and dense vegetation - Remove weeds to reduce prolonged moisture and high humidity around the plant - Eradicate potential sources of inoculum by removing plant residues after harvesting and volunteer plants - Practice crop rotation for 3 to 4 years with non-susceptible crops, such as leguminous crops (beans, cowpeas) or cereals - Good sanitation practices - wash field equipment and machinery after use - Plant at recommended spacing to allow for airflow for optimal relative humidity - Respect planting time seasons | - Inspect the field once a week - The first symptoms are small light to dark green, circular to irregular-shaped water-soaked spots - These lesions usually appear first on the lower leaves. Lesions often begin to develop near the leaf tips or edges, where dew is retained the longest - White mycelium develop on the underside of the leaf and on stems throughout the canopy. - Act immediately when first symptoms are noticed | - Cut off stems three weeks before harvesting in order to reduce infection of tubers - Bury infected plants and plant parts below ground at a depth of about 60 cm or more away from the production site | - Wear protective clothing when handling pesticides - Consult recent list of registered pesticides (Agri-input Plant Health Division) - Follow label instructions carefully - Dispose pesticide containers responsibly. Do not allow product to enter the waterways or sewer - Apply Mancozeb which has a contact fungicidal action. Apply at 50-70 g/sprayer tank of 15 litres. You will a around 480 L/ha of mixture | - WHO Class U (Unlikely to give acute hazard in normal use). PHI 7 days.  |
