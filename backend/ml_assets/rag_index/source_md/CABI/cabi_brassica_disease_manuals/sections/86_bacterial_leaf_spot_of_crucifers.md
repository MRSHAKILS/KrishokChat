# Bacterial leaf spot of crucifers

*Pseudomonas syringae* pv. *maculicola*

|   | Prevention | Monitoring | Direct Control | Direct Control | Restrictions  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
|  Cabbage leaf showing later stages of infection (Francis Nduati, Kenya) | - Control of this bacterial disease relies on good cultural practices and other preventive methods - Avoid planting cabbage on land with continuous history of cabbage for two years - Remove and destroy by burning volunteer and leftover plants - Avoid causing injuries to plants when working on plants to reduce mechanical transmission - Avoid clustered areas where farmers plant the same crucifers - Practice rotations with corn and sorghum every two years - Set up nurseries away from major production fields and plant in well drained soils | - Monitor fields looking for small, water-soaked pinpoint lesions with yellow halos on the leaves - Look also for dark brown or purple lesions on leaves as spots merge together - Look for chlorotic and dying leaves as the disease progresses - Take action when the first symptoms are seen - Similar symptoms can be caused by fungi such as *Alternaria* spp. | - Rogue and destroy infected plants in nurseries and production fields when disease density is low | - No chemical for control of this disease has been reported |   |
