# Bacterial leaf spot on Brassica

Recognize the problem

Bacterial leaf spot disease rarely occurs in lowlands. It affects stems, leaves and petioles. Infections start as small, dark spots on leaves. As the disease develops, many water soaked leaf spots appear but these stay small. Older leaf spots turn brown and may or may not have a purple border around them. Symptoms on transplants may resemble downy mildew symptoms.

Background

The bacterium is found in seed and survives in the soil for over a year. It may be splashed from plant to plant by rain, wind and sprinkler irrigation. The disease is active during warm wet weather.

Management

Non-chemical control

- Plant cabbage seeds or seedlings that are free from diseases.
- Practice a crop rotation programme for 3 seasons.
- Plant resistant varieties.
- Soils should be mulched to avoid disease spread in rain and also wind.
- Farm equipment should be kept clean and disinfected to avoid disease spread.
- Changing from sprinkler to furrow irrigation limits the spread of the bacteria.
- Destroy all the infected crop material by ploughing it in to the soil, especially after harvest.

Chemical control

- Copper oxychloride can control the bacteria.
