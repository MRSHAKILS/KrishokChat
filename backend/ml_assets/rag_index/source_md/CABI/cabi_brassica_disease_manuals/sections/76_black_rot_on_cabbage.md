# Black rot on cabbage

*Xanthomonas campestris* pv. *campestris*

|   | Prevention | Monitoring | Direct Control  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|  Symptoms on cabbage (Florence Wakhungu) | - Use certified seed and seedlings free from disease as this disease can be seed-borne - Use resistant varieties where available - Do not plant crucifers in areas: that have a history of the disease; where crucifers have been grown within the previous 2 years; where cruciferous weeds are numerous - Deep plough fields to bury contaminated crop residues - Sterilise the seedbed by heaping and slowly burning dry grass or other plant thrash on the seedbed. - Plant on raised beds for better drainage. Avoid waterlogging. - Do not damage leaves of seedlings as this makes them prone to disease - Correct plant spacing: 50 cm between plants and 60 cm between rows. If plants are closer together this can encourage spread of disease between plants - Avoid flood/furrow irrigation because it will carry the disease from infected to uninfected areas. Also avoid irrigating early in the day when dew is present or late in the day. - Destroy alternative host weeds in the crucifer family e.g. birdsrape mustard, Indian mustard, black mustard - Avoid moving through fields when the plant are wet to prevent disease spread. Clean tools and boots. - Rotate crucifers for a minimum of 3 years with crops that are not susceptible such as maize, legumes, tomato | - Additional relevant crops: cauliflower, chinese cabbage, brussel sprouts, turnip, mustard, broccoli, rape, radish - Monitor plants weekly for symptoms: - Look for yellow V-shaped lesions with an open part of 'V' along the edge of the leaf - The diseased leaf area becomes brown and brittle - Affected leaf veins turn dark brown to black - The V-shaped lesions enlarge and the entire leaf eventually turns yellow or wilts and falls from the plant - Infected seedlings remain stunted and become unproductive - There is a ring of black discoloured tissue within the stem when cut - If 6-8 plants/ha show symptoms take action - The disease thrives in wet conditions with high temperatures | - Remove all infected young plants and debris from the field and destroy by: deep burying; composting well outside the field; feeding crop to livestock - When older plants become infected, harvest as soon as possible.  |
