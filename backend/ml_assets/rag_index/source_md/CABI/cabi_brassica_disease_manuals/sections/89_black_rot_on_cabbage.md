# Black rot on cabbage

*Xanthomonas campestris* pv *campestris* Black rot on cabbage

![img-119.jpeg](sections/images/89_p111_img-119.jpeg)

Initial infection begins as yellow "V" shaped spot on edge of leaf (David B. Langston, University of Georgia, Bugwood.org).

![img-120.jpeg](sections/images/89_p111_img-120.jpeg)

Cabbage seedlings showing bacterial blight lesions (Tom Creswell, Purdue University, Bugwood.org)

![img-121.jpeg](sections/images/89_p111_img-121.jpeg)

Blackened vascular tissue (Gerald Holmes, Strawberry Center, Cal Poly San Luis Obispo, Bugwood.org)

|  Prevention | Monitoring | Direct Control | Direct Control | Restrictions  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
|  - Always use certified disease-free seed and only plant healthy cabbage seedlings. - Use disease-resistant varieties such as Riana F1 and Carina F1. - Soil solarization may also help to reduce bacterial inoculum in the soil (refer to factsheet). - If seed is not treated, seed treatment with water at 50°C can be done for 20-25 minutes (refer to factsheet). - Use drip irrigation to avoid water splashing. Use of mulch to prevent splashing of irrigation water. - Avoid water logging in the cabbage field by placing your plants on ridges. - Increase spacing between plants (50-60 cm) and between rows (60-90 cm) to maximize air flow and to reduce leaf wetness. - Rotate cabbage with non-cruciferous crops such as cereals (maize) and legumes (beans or groundnuts) for a minimum of three years. - Avoid using compost from brassica crops in cabbage fields. | - Look for V-shaped lesions that start from leaf edge going inside the leaf. - Diseased areas start as yellow and become brown over time. - Affected leaf veins turn dark brown to black. - The V-shaped lesions enlarge to levels where they cover the entire leaf which wilts and dries. - Infected seedlings may be stunted. - Disease thrives in wet and humid conditions. - Check for symptoms every week. - Take action as soon as you notice an infected plant, as bacteria multiply quickly and may cover the whole field in a very short period of time. | - Remove all infected plants and debris from the field and destroy by deep burying (30cm or more) or composting far away from the field after harvest. - When older plants get infected, harvest them quickly. | - When using a pesticide or a botanical, always wear protective clothing (PPE) and follow the instructions on product labels such as dosage and timing of application, pre harvest interval, re-entry interval and number of sprays. - Do not eat, drink or smoke while handling (mixing or applying) pesticides. - WHO class II chemicals may not be allowed in local IPM schemes. - Always consult ZEMA for updated pesticide lists. - Copper based sprays are known to help against bacterial infections. Spray copper oxychloride at the dilution rate of 40g per 16L sprayer (250g/100L of water). | - WHO class II (Moderately acute hazard). Apply as soon as symptoms appear and repeat twice if needed. Pre harvest interval (PHI) is 14 days and Re-entry interval (REI) is 1 day.  |
