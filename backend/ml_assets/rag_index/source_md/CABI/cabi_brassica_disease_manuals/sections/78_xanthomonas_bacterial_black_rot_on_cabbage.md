# Xanthomonas bacterial black rot on cabbage

*Xanthomonas campestris* pv. *campestris*

|   | Prevention | Monitoring | Direct Control | Direct Control | Restrictions  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
|  Cabbage leaf affected by black rot showing the V-shape (photo M.T.McGrath) | - Use certified tolerant varieties like Riana F1, KK cross F1 and Gloria F1 - Plant on raised beds for better drainage - Sterilise the seedbed by heaping and slowly burning dry grass or other plant thrash on the seedbed. - Increase spacing in the hot season, such as up to more than 60 x 60 cm. - This bacterial disease is mainly spread by water. Therefore, do not flood irrigate from infected towards un-infected field areas. - Avoid water logging - Avoid working in the field when plants are wet as the disease can spread. - Rotate with non-brassicas like maize and legumes for at least 2 years after you had black rot disease in your field, and avoid susceptible crops like rape, Chinese cabbage, cauliflower and broccoli. | - This is a bacterial disease, common during wet conditions with high temperatures - Monitor for: Yellow, brown or black v-shaped patches at the edge of the leaves. If the yellow/black lesion is held against the light, black veins are visible. A cross section of infected stem reveals a black ring. In later stages, cabbage head turns black with an offensive foul smell - Take immediate action when first symptoms appear on young plants. - When symptoms appear at late crop stage or close to harvest, often no action is needed, because outer damaged cabbage leaves can be removed. | - Remove all infected young plants and debris from the field and destroy by deep burying or well-composting outside field. - When older plants become infected, harvest as soon as possible. | - There are no really effective pesticides against bacteria. - Only copper based products can help to prevent disease spread. - When using a pesticide or botanical, always wear protective clothing and follow the instructions on the product label, such as dosage, timing of application, pre-harvest interval, max number of sprays, restricted re-entry interval. Do not empty into drains and water sources. - WHO toxicity class II pesticides might not be allowed in local IPM schemes. - Always consult recent list of registered pesticides of ZEMA. - Spray the remaining healthy plants using a copper-based protective pesticide to suppress further spread and prevent new damage. For example copper-based products (copper salts, copper oxy chlorides). Multi-side action pesticide group. Usually applied at 40-80 mg /20 litres, but double-check product labels. Never spray more than 6 kg copper per ha per year! | - WHO toxicity class II (moderately acute hazardous); Pre-harvest interval p.h.i. about 2 weeks and restricted re-entry interval 1 day, but check product label. Use a maximum of 3 sprays. Spray interval of 14 days during wet weather. When used too much, copper(II)-ions can accumulate in soil and water. It can burn leaves. Livestock should be kept out of treated area for this period  |
