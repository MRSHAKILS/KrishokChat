# Downy mildew on Peas - Kenya

Peronospora viciae f. sp pisi

|   | Prevention | Monitoring | Direct Control | Direct Control | Restrictions  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
|  Typical downy mildew sporulation on the underside of pea stipules. (Source: Phil Taylor, CABI) | - • Employ crop rotation, avoid peas and faba beans for 3 years (all other crops are resistant). It is seed-borne so plant certified seeds. - • In the case of severe infection, remove crop trash after harvest. - • Seedlings are susceptible but plants become resistant with age. - • Use a slow-release organic fertilizer such as well-decomposed manure to avoid excess nitrogen as young leafy plant parts are susceptible. - • Where possible, attempt to keep leaves dry for as long as possible. - • Avoid sprinkler irrigation to reduce the level of spread. - • Staking peas to increase air circulation. - • Reduce humidity in greenhouses. | - • Look out for waxy pale yellow plants with thickened leaves. This indicates systemic infection. - • Look for cottony-white to tan fungal colonies and yellowed blotches on the upper and lower surfaces of the leaves. - • Take action when if you see one systemically infected plant. | - • If a systemically infected plant is seen, remove and destroy it to reduce the spread of disease to healthy plants. - • Plants will generally grow away from the infection because they become resistant with age. If severe, consider sprays. - • Spray plants using *Trichoderma*-based products. | - • When using a pesticide always wear appropriate personal protective equipment. - • Follow the instructions on the product label on dosage, maximum applications, PHI, Re-entry interval, and disposal of pesticide waste amongst others. - • Use list of registered pesticides (PCPB): www.pcpb.go.ke - • Try to apply a blend of fungicides to avoid the development of resistance. Spray just before petal fall especially if the weather is wet. - • Use azoxystrobin-based products. - • Use difenoconazole-based products mixed with other fungicides. | - • These restrictions apply to one active ingredient only. - • WHO Class U (unlikely to present an acute hazard) - Green band. Max. 2 applications per season. For 250g/litre (concentrate), max. dose is 0.4L per acre. PHI: 14 days. - • WHO Class II (moderately hazardous) - Yellow band. 200ml per acre (for a 250g stock). PHI: 14 days.  |
