# Bacterial Soft Rot on Carrot - Kenya

*Pectobacterium carotovorum* (previously called *Erwinia carotovora*)

|   | Prevention | Monitoring | Direct Control | Direct Control | Restrictions  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
|  Soft, watery, slimy rot on the taproot, which is grey to brown (Photo: Oregon State University) | - Plant carrots in well-drained soils, and water twice a week. - Rotate with legumes, soybeans and fodder grasses - Avoid injuring carrots when weeding or harvesting to prevent entry of pathogen - Control vectors that can wound carrots e.g. cabbage maggot - see yellow section. - Start working from the healthy field towards the infected area - Disinfect all farm tools in 10% jik solution (50 mls in 1 litre) of water for 5 minutes - Avoid harvesting carrots during wet conditions | - Look out for: - Soft, watery, slimy rot on the taproot, which is grey to brown with a foul smell - Rapid decay that consumes the core of the carrot, often leaving the epidermis intact - General yellowing, wilting and collapse of the foliage above ground - Severe pitting of carrot roots observed after the roots are harvested and washed - Take action as soon as yellowing and wilting symptoms are seen on one plant | - Remove and burn infected plants | - Bacterial diseases have no known cure hence avoid the introducing the pathogen. Use of copper-based fungicides only suppress the pathogen. - When using a pesticide always wear protective equipment. - Follow the instructions on the product label, such as dosage, timing of application, max. number of sprays, restricted re-entry interval. - Always consult recent list of registered pesticides (PCPB). - Spray with copper-based fungicide e.g. copper oxychloride (Amicop 50WP or Cobox 50WP at 60g per 20L water). Repeat spray after 2 weeks. Maximum 2 sprays/season. - For control of cabbage maggot, drench with Azadirachtin-based products e.g. Nimbecidine EC or Achook EC at 50mls/20L of water | - WHO Class II (Moderately Hazardous), PHI 7days, REI 12hrs. Toxic to aquatic organisms. WHO Class U (unlikely to cause any effects if used as required)  |
