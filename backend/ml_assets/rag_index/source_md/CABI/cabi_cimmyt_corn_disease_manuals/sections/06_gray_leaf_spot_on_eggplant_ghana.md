# Gray Leaf Spot on Eggplant - Ghana

*Stemphylium solani*; *S. lycopersici* (syn. *S. floridanum*) Gray leaf spot disease of eggplant and pepper

|   | Prevention | Monitoring | Direct Control | Direct Control | Restrictions  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
|  Spots on tomato leaf caused by *Stemphylium solani*. (Source: Clemson University - USDA Cooperative Extension Slide Series, Wikimedia Commons) | - Avoid planting crops near other susceptible crops (e.g. tomato, pepper, eggplant). - Orient rows parallel to prevailing wind direction for rapid drying of leaves. - Avoid working in the field while the vegetation is wet. - Practice 3-year crop rotation with non-susceptible crops (i.e. non-solanaceous crops). - Remove crop residue from year to year. Eliminate weeds that can serve as intermediate hosts. - Avoid overhead irrigation as splashing of water can spread the disease. - Provide adequate ventilation for seedling beds. - Ensure good aeration in field using appropriate planting distances. - Water during the day more than in the evening. | - Look out for the following symptoms: - Small, circular to elongated brown spots with a reddish border on leaves, petioles, stems, fruit stalks and calyxes. - Red to brown spots with gray to white centre (frog eye), sometimes with yellow margins. - Elongated lesions on petioles and stems. - Leaf drop. - Presence of fungal fruiting bodies on the lesions, giving them a slightly light grey tint. | - When infection is low, remove affected leaves and destroy. - Treat with fungicides if disease intensity is high. | - Always read and follow manufacturer's instructions. - Wear protective clothing. - Rotate fungicides to prevent development of resistance. - Use fungicides only when disease intensity is high. - Spray chlorothalonil (75%) at 7-10 day intervals. Apply 90mls/15L water. - Spray mancozeb (80%) at 7-14 day intervals. Apply 85-100g/15L water. - Spray copper-based (Dispers) at 7-10 day intervals. Apply 100g/15L water. | - WHO Class U (unlikely to present an acute hazard), FRAC M5. PHI 7 days, REI 24hrs. - WHO Class U (unlikely to present an acute hazard), FRAC M3. Apply 3 times in a season. PHI is 5 days, REI 24 hrs. - WHO Class II (moderately hazardous), FRAC M1. PHI 5 days.  |
