# Gray Leaf Spot of Maize - Nepal

Cercospora zeae-maydis मर्केको पातमा लाग्ने ध्वाँसे थेग्ले रोग

|   | Prevention | Monitoring | Direct Control | Direct Control | Restrictions  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
|  Gray leaf spot infected leaf of maize (Photo: Gyanu Manandhar) | - It is extremely important to remove plant debris from previous diseased crops. Rake up and burn the crop debris and to encourage your neighbours to do the same. The disease can easily spread from debris in your neighbours fields. The pathogen can survive on crop debris underground. - Use resistant/tolerant varieties like Ganesh-1, Manakamana-3 for high hills (1900 masl); and Manakamana-1, Deuti, Manakamana-6, ZM401 for mid hills (1300 masl). - Proper tillage practices as early planting as possible. - The pathogen can survive for long periods in the soil. Practice crop rotation for at least 3 years with non-host crops like millet, potato, vegetables, beans or pea. - Use recommended doses of chemical fertilizers (120:60:40 NPK kg/hectare). - #REF! | - Monitor regularly for the appearance of gray leaf spot lesions on leaves just before or near initiation of tassel and ear shoot stage of plants grown at about 1400 masl. - Look for smaller chlorotic spots or lesions, first on lower leaves, which turn into long narrow rectangular lesions 2-3 weeks later. Damage to the stalks may cause plants to fall over. - Take action as soon as disease symptom is seen. | - To delay the disease, you can remove all the lower infected leaves at early stage of infection. Bury them at least 50cm deep away from field or burn. Practice this together with other adjoining farmers. - Avoid overhead irrigation. | - Use personal protection equipment while using fungicides. - Do not use chemicals with the same mode of action year after year as this can lead to resistance. - Always consult the most recent list of registered pesticides (PCPB). - Spray whole plant covering leaf, leaf sheath, stalk, ear shoot, as a protective measure, with carbendazim 50% WP @ 2 g/litre of water during first week of July or at initiation of tassel in disease-prone areas where susceptible varieties are grown. At least 35L of aqueous suspension of fungicide are required for 500 m2 area. - Soon after disease appearance, spray whole plant covering leaf, leaf sheath, stalk, ear shoot, with propiconazole 25 EC @ 1 ml/L of water. A repeated spray at the interval of 15 days may be required depending on the disease severity. At least 35 L of aqueous suspension of fungicide is required for 500 m2 area | - WHO Class: U (unlikely to present acute hazard). There is widespread resistance to this chemical and it should not be used in isolation. If used mix with Propiconazole (below) or similar 'azole' fungicide. A repeated spray at an interval of 15 days may be required depending on the disease severity. - Propiconazole falls in WHO list II (moderately hazardous). Has waiting period (PHI) of at least 15 days.  |
