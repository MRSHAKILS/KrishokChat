# Leaf blight on Sorghum

*Setosphaeria turcica* Turcicum leaf blight, Northern leaf blight

|   | Prevention | Monitoring | Direct Control  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|  Large elliptical leaf blight lesions on sorghum (Courtesy EcoPort: David C. Nowell) Typical shape and colour of a *S. turcica* lesion on a sorghum leaf (Courtesy EcoPort: David C. Nowell) Early water-soaked lesion near the base of the leaf without a pigmented margin (Courtesy EcoPort: David C. Nowell) | - Use certified disease-free seeds. - Plant resistant or tolerant varieties. - To reduce initial inoculum, use tillage methods that promote the decomposition of crop debris, as the fungus survives in and on plant debris. - Practise crop rotation with non-grass crops and eliminate weedy grass hosts. - On sorghum, increased levels of nitrogen increase the development of the disease. Avoid high levels of nitrogen fertilization. - Crop rotation for 1-2 years with soybeans, beans and sunflower. Do not practise crop rotation with maize. | - Monitor regularly for symptoms during optimal conditions of high humidity and moderate temperatures (18-27 °C). - Leaves:     - Initially slightly oval, small, water-soaked spots appear. The spots grow into elliptical (with pointed ends) brown lesions until nearly as wide as the leaf.     - The margin is red-purple and during warm, humid weather, greenish-black fungal sporulation is produced in the centre of the lesions.     - In the final stages, lesions are tan to grey, may merge and lead to complete burning of the foliage.     - Lesions develop first on lower leaves and progress upwards.     - Seedlings: small reddish-purple or yellowish-brown spots usually develop on the leaves.     - Under warm, humid conditions the disease may cause serious damage by killing all leaves before plants have matured.     - Symptoms on resistant cultivars are reduced to small chlorotic or necrotic spots.     - *S. turcica* on sorghum forms larger and fewer lesions than *Cochliobolus heterostrophus* (southern leaf spot), mostly on the leaves but also on the stalks. *Cercospora*, *Colletorichum*, *Ascochyta* can also cause similar leaf disease symptoms.     - Act when the first lesions appear on the leaves. | - There are no direct control measures for this disease.  |

Note: Pesticides may be available to control this pest. Please check with the Ministry of Agriculture in your country to find out which pesticides are registered in your country and the local restrictions for their use.
