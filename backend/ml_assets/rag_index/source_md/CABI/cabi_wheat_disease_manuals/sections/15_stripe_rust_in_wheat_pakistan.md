# Stripe rust in wheat - Pakistan

*Puccinia striiformis* Yellow Rust

|   | Prevention | Monitoring | Direct Control | Direct Control | Restrictions  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
|  Close-up view of a spring wheat leaf showing signs and symptoms of stripe rust. (Source: Howard F. Schwartz, Colorado State University, Bugwood.org, CC BY 3.0) | - Cultural Practices: Avoid early planting of winter wheat because it will get affected by stripe rust. Reduce volunteer grasses because they act as hosts to carry stripe rust season to season. Use cultivar mixtures to reduce fungal attack. Avoid excessive irrigation to reduce humidity in environment. - Mechanical practices: Destroy previous residues if infected. Remove Berberis plants because it's an alternative host for rust. - Use partially resistant varieties like Akbar-19 or Ujala-16. | - What to look for?: Tiny yellow-orange fungus spots/pustules. Small, circular spots that later develop into yellow strips. - Where to look?: Leaf, leaf sheaths, awns and inside glumes. Mostly upper leaves. - When to look and how often?: From seedling to maturation. Through-out the season, mostly in rainy season. Temp. around 15-20°C and humid atmosphere. Thrice per week when disease attack is expected. - When to consider taking action?: 10-20 leaves with symptoms at few spots in the field, or when more than 5% of plants in a field have more than 5% of their flag leaf surface infected. - Too late to take action if 40-50 leaves per many spots in a field have symptoms, most of your plants are likely infected, and action is too late. | - Preventively spray *Sophora flavescens* extracts at 10mg/ml, 30L/660 square metres, once a week from jointing to grouting stage. - Preventively spray Neem oil products at 1 fluid ounce per gallon of water. - Note that these botanicals cannot cure a plant, they can only reduce the spread. | - When using a pesticide or botanical, always wear protective clothing and follow the instructions on the product label, such as dosage, timing of application, pre-harvest interval (PHI), max. number of sprays, and restricted re-entry interval (REI). Do not empty into drains and water sources. - Apply propiconazole (e.g. Tilt™ 25% EC, Azostar™ 18.7% SC) at 200ml/acre, 120L/acre of water. Systemic action. Foliar spraying. - Apply flutriafol + azoxystrobin (e.g. TOPGUARD EQ™ FMC at 100ml/acre, 120L/acre of water). Systemic action. Ground 10 gallon/acre. - Apply bixafen + flutriafol (e.g. Lucento™ FMC at 150ml/acre, 120L/acre of water). Systemic, contact action. Apply as ground spraying. | - WHO class II (moderately hazardous). Harmful to parasitoids. Best results when disease is in early stages. PHI: 45 days. REI: 1 day. - WHO Class II. Moderately harmful to bees and pollinators. PHI: 30 days. REI: 12 hours. - Bixafen is not classified by WHO, but is highly toxic to fish, and moderately toxic to birds, aquatic invertebrates and algae. Flutriafol: WHO Class II. PHI: 30 days. REI: 12 hours. Use maximum twice per year.  |
