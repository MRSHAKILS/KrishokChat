# Stem Rust on Wheat

*Puccinia graminis f. sp.tritici*

|   | Prevention | Monitoring | Direct Control | Direct Control | Restrictions  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
|  Stem rust (B. Steffenson) | - Sow resistant varieties because they provide the best protection against stem rust. - Balanced use of fertilizer during the crop season to ensure good health of plant. - Use disease free seeds. - Ensure field sanitation from barberry alternate host plants. - Spacing and field orientation away from winds coming from infected areas may protect from wind borne sources of inoculum. - Practice no tillage or minimum tillage to reduce over wintering spore inoculum. | - Inspect wheat crops every 7 to 10 days from flag leaf emergence to early dough grain development. - Carefully inspect different plant parts, especially the lower stems, for symptoms. Spend at least 15 minutes walking through each wheat crop. - If stem rust is detected within a region, increase inspection to every 4 to 7 days. Look for reddish-brown, powdery, oblong pustules on the stems, leaves, glumes, awns and kernels. - If stem rust is detected, then collect 10 stems from 10 random locations (total 100) to determine the percentage of stem rust infection. - Whenever the disease appears it is above ETL, so inspect the field regularly to protect crop. | - Remove the green bridge, including volunteer cereals e.g. barley, common barberry (and some additional Berberis, Mahoberberis, and *Mahonia* spp.). - Also ask your neighbours to monitor their crop to check the disease. - Use earlier maturing wheat varieties in cooler areas downwind from warmer wheat growing areas. | - Check that the product is registered, and use the maximum recommended label rate for stem rust control in wheat. Most pesticides have protective, curative, and systemic activity. - It is important to consider withholding periods when choosing an appropriate fungicide. - It is always important to read the chemical label before use and ensure protective clothing while spraying. - Avoid repeated use of fungicides with the same active ingredient in the same season. - Tebuconazole 430 g/L (eg. Folicur®430 SC), propiconazole 230 g/L (eg. Tilt250 EC, Bumper250 EC). - Triadimefon (e.g. Bayleton 25 WP) @ 400 gm/100L water per acre. | - Both Tebuconazole & Propiconazole: WHO Class II (Moderately hazardous). - WHO Class II (Moderately hazardous).  |
