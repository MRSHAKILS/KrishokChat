# Rice blast - Kenya

Magnaporthe grisea (Pyricularia grisea) Mpunga/Mchele-Swahili

|   | Prevention | Monitoring | Direct Control | Direct Control | Restrictions  |   |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
|  Symptoms of rice blast (Photo: R.K. Webster, ARS-USDA) Rice leaves infected by blast. (Photo: IRRI) | - Plant resistant varieties such as Nerica 1, 4, 10 and 11 for upland areas; SC 213, NIBAM 108, NIBAM 109, NIBAM 110, IR-025, and N221. - Plant early to avoid late-season blast pressure. - Flood the field as often as possible. - Avoid excessive use of nitrogenous fertilizers. - Destroy infested crop residue before planting. - Practise proper water management. | - Examine plants in several locations throughout the field for the presence of leaf lesions. - Scout for diamond-shaped elongated lesions with a gray or white center and brown or reddish brown border on the leaves, nodes, panicles and collar of the flag leaves. - Look out for a bleaching whitish colour on the head on the lower portion of the panicle. - Look out for a rotten panicle neck caused by a broken neck condition. | - Burn or compostdiseased tissues. | - When using a pesticide, always wear protective clothing and follow the instructions on the product label - Do not use chemicals with the same mode of action year after year as this can lead to resistance - Always consult the most recent list of registered pesticides (PCPB) | - Trifloxystrobin 100 g/L + and Tebuconazole 200g/L. NATIVO SC300. Rate: 5-10ml/L - Difenoconazole (Score 250 EC). Rate: 20ml in 20L | - Trifloxistrobin WHO class U (unlikely to present acute hazard), Tebuconazole WHO II (moderately hazardous). NATIVO SC 300: Blue colourband. MRL 2.0mg/Kg trifloxystrobin 0.2mg/Kg, PHI = 40 days. Tebuconazole, 2.0 g/Kg. MRL = 2.0mg/Kg - PHI 14 days. WHO class II (moderately hazardous)  |
