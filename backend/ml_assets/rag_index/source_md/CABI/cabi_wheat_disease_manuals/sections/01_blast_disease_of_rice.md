# Blast disease of Rice

Magnaporthe grisea Blast

|   | Prevention | Monitoring | Direct Control | Direct Control | Restrictions  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
|  Rice field with blast symptoms on the leaves (E. Moses, Crops Research Institute) | - Use certified disease free seeds for planting or consider using tolerant varieties (eg. Amankwatia, Jasmine and AGRA rice) - Avoid excessive use of nitrogen fertilizer as this can increase the severity of the disease - Avoid drought stress to plants and extended drainage period as this makes the plants more susceptible to the disease - Practice continuous flooding to limit blast development | - Monitor plants by observing leaves for appearance of major symptom - diamond shaped lesions with grey centre and brown or reddish borders and start post planting control measures - Presence of blast lesions just before booting often dictates decisions for chemical treatment | - Remove leaves with lesions or symptoms if they are not many during monitoring | - When using a pesticide or botanical, always wear protective clothing and follow the instructions on the product label - Do not use chemicals with the same mode of action year after year as this can lead to resistance - Always consult the most recent list of registered pesticides of MOFA, Ghana |   |
|  Blast lesions on leaves (Donald Groth, Louisiana State University AgCenter, Bugwood.org) |  |  |  | - Apply Maneb (80%) (Trimangol 80WP) Apply 70g/knapsack; Broad spectrum protectant fungicide with a multi-site mode of action (FRAC group M3) - Apply Kingstar WG (Azoxystrobin (60%)+Cyproconazole (24%)) Apply at a rate of 75ml/15l knapsack; FRAC groups: azoxystrobin C3 & cyproconazole G1 - WHO Class U (Unlikely to present acute hazard in normal use) Start application when lesions first appear and just before booting to prevent neck blast. Repeat application at 10days interval. Observe PHI of 14 days and : DO NOT enter into treated areas until the spray has dried unless wearing cotton overall. - WHO Class II (Moderately hazardous), Apply at first sign of disease attack, usually from tillering to early flowering. Ensure thorough coverage of application. Repeat application upon monitoring at early booting stage. PHI is 15 days. DO NOT enter into treated areas until the spray has dried unless wearing cotton overalls |   |
