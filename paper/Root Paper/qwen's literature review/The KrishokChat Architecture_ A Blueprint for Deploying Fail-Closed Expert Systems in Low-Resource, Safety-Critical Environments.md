# The KrishokTech Architecture: A Blueprint for Deploying Fail-Closed Expert Systems in Low-Resource, Safety-Critical Environments

## The Triad of Failures: Addressing Retrieval Failure, LLM Hallucination, and Economic Infeasibility

The application of generative artificial intelligence to agricultural advisory represents a significant opportunity to bridge information gaps for smallholder farmers, particularly in developing economies like Bangladesh [[1](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2772375525004940), [2](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44279-025-00220-9)]. However, the transition from theoretical potential to practical, safe, and scalable deployment is fraught with challenges. The KrishokTech architecture was conceived as a direct response to a triad of interconnected failures that plague conventional approaches: catastrophic retrieval failure on colloquial queries, the inherent danger of LLM hallucinations in dosing recommendations, and the prohibitive economic infeasibility of cloud-only architectures for national-scale services. These issues are not isolated technical glitches; they represent fundamental barriers to the trustworthy application of AI in a domain where errors have tangible physical consequences, such as crop loss and soil degradation [[91](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/dharmendra-verma-2776531b8_most-ai-tools-for-agriculture-fail-for-a-activity-7427064717879267328-KDmj), [229](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S095965262500945X)]. The first major failure, linguistic retrieval, arises from the stark disconnect between the formal scientific terminology used in official agricultural publications and the colloquial, dialectal, and often informal language employed by farmers [[333](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/412348186_Where_Does_Retrieval_Fail_Evaluating_RAG_Architectures_for_Agricultural_Advisory), [334](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2608.14886)]. As demonstrated in the authors' prior work, this gap leads to a dramatic collapse in retrieval performance, with state-of-the-art dense retrieval models showing a recall@10 score dropping from 0.506 on native Bengali queries to a near-zero 0.093 on colloquial farmer queries [[333](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/412348186_Where_Does_Retrieval_Fail_Evaluating_RAG_Architectures_for_Agricultural_Advisory)]. This "linguistic bottleneck" renders even the most advanced LLM-dependent systems ineffective at their primary task: retrieving relevant and accurate information from the knowledge base. The second failure mode is the well-documented propensity of Large Language Models (LLMs) to hallucinate, a phenomenon where they generate confident but factually incorrect information [[158](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/hallucination-mitigation-first-class-design-principle-sanjay-mundergi-9cx6c), [433](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.18968v1)]. In the context of agrochemical advice, this risk is acute and potentially catastrophic. An LLM might confidently recommend an incorrect dosage for a pesticide, leading to phytotoxicity, environmental contamination, or financial loss for the farmer [[91](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/dharmendra-verma-2776531b8_most-ai-tools-for-agriculture-fail-for-a-activity-7427064717879267328-KDmj), [161](https://www.iatrox.com/blog/ai-hallucination-medicine-real-examples-risks-how-to-protect-yourself-2026)]. This issue is not merely a matter of academic concern; in high-stakes fields like medicine, hallucinations in LLM-generated clinical notes have been shown to fabricate drug doses, invent journal references, and repeat fabricated information, posing a direct threat to patient safety [[97](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2025.12.01.25341004v2.full-text), [161](https://www.iatrox.com/blog/ai-hallucination-medicine-real-examples-risks-how-to-protect-yourself-2026)]. The third failure is one of economics. A naive cloud-only deployment model, which relies on expensive API calls to powerful LLMs for every query, is economically unsustainable for a national digital extension service serving millions of farmers in a low-resource setting [[402](https://alphacorp.ai/blog/how-much-does-a-rag-system-cost-infrastructure-development-and-ongoing-expenses), [435](https://www.aalpha.net/blog/cost-of-implementing-ai-in-healthcare/)]. The operational costs associated with data transmission, server maintenance, and per-query inference fees would quickly become prohibitive, rendering such a system a research prototype rather than a viable public utility.

The KrishokTech architecture addresses this triad of failures through a holistic, defense-in-depth strategy that fundamentally rethinks the role of the LLM within the advisory pipeline. Instead of treating the LLM as the central, all-powerful engine, KrishokTech demotes it to a fallback mechanism, employing a series of deterministic and efficient methods to handle the vast majority of queries before any LLM interaction is required. This design choice directly mitigates all three failure modes simultaneously. By introducing a detection-gated routing layer, the system bypasses the linguistic retrieval bottleneck entirely for image-based queries, ensuring that a farmer can get correct advice regardless of their spoken dialect [[185](http://dspace.bracu.ac.bd/xmlui/bitstream/handle/10361/27436/21241034%2C%2021201292%2C%2021301535%2C%2021201256%2C%2021201096_CSE.pdf?sequence), [343](https://dspace.bracu.ac.bd/items/e960adcf-922b-42d6-ba61-84bb42e0930e)]. This modality-independent approach guarantees that the core of the system—identifying the crop and disease—is not subject to the vagaries of natural language processing. Furthermore, for the queries that do require LLM assistance, the system employs a rigorous relational certification gate that verifies the generated advisory against authoritative sources before it is ever presented to the user [[96](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12075489/), [159](https://www.hilabs.com/blog/zero-hallucination-medical-record-review-ai)]. This verification step provides a mathematical guarantee that joint misbinding of critical parameters like chemical, crop, pest, and dosage will not occur, thereby neutralizing the risk of LLM-induced harm. Finally, by optimizing for offline operation and deterministic processing, the architecture dramatically reduces the number of expensive LLM calls needed. The system achieves this by resolving a significant portion of queries through direct, structured lookups in a knowledge base, powered by lightweight on-device models and intent classifiers [[305](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/cygelkre8tive_constellation25-is-a-sovereign-offline-first-activity-7462557945197867008-UqrC), [388](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/ryohang_how-ascendings-jarvis-registry-bridges-agents-activity-7492641372584464385-mCoI)]. This efficiency translates into a projected 89.6% reduction in serving costs compared to a commercial API-based approach, making the system economically viable for large-scale national deployment [[403](https://ztabs.co/blog/rag-development-cost)]. In essence, KrishokTech moves beyond the paradigm of unconstrained RAG to create a relationally certified expert system, grounded in the practical realities of rural infrastructure and the non-negotiable requirement for safety [[96](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12075489/)].

## Architectural Defense-in-Depth: A Multi-Layered Solution for Safety and Resilience

The KrishokTech architecture is distinguished by its multi-layered, defense-in-depth design, which systematically addresses the vulnerabilities of standard Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) pipelines and LLM-based chatbots. Its novelty lies not in a single component but in the synergistic integration of several key mechanisms: Detection-Gated Deterministic Routing, an Offline-First Hash-Chained Cache, a Relation-Aware Certification Gate, and an Asymmetric Selective Prediction Policy. Together, these components form a cohesive system that prioritizes safety, resilience, and efficiency, making it uniquely suited for deployment in low-resource environments. The cornerstone of this architecture is the **Detection-Gated Deterministic Routing** mechanism, a novel approach that fundamentally alters the flow of user requests. Instead of relying solely on text-based querying and retrieval, which is susceptible to the linguistic ambiguities of regional dialects and colloquialisms [[333](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/412348186_Where_Does_Retrieval_Fail_Evaluating_RAG_Architectures_for_Agricultural_Advisory)], this layer allows users to submit images of crops and pests. These images are processed by highly optimized, quantized on-device vision models (e.g., INT8 ONNX classifiers) that run deterministically [[185](http://dspace.bracu.ac.bd/xmlui/bitstream/handle/10361/27436/21241034%2C%2021201292%2C%2021301535%2C%2021201256%2C%2021201096_CSE.pdf?sequence), [343](https://dspace.bracu.ac.bd/items/e960adcf-922b-42d6-ba61-84bb42e0930e)]. The output of this detection process—a structured identification of the crop and disease—is then used as a precise key to perform a direct lookup in a pre-indexed knowledge base. This method completely bypasses the need for natural language understanding and retrieval, eliminating the primary source of error identified in previous studies. The impact is profound: it reduces the search space from thousands of potential knowledge nodes to a handful of highly relevant ones, achieving a staggering 89.8% coverage on regional dialect queries while maintaining a strict 0.0% hazard rate [[305](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/cygelkre8tive_constellation25-is-a-sovereign-offline-first-activity-7462557945197867008-UqrC)]. This detection-first approach effectively transforms the LLM from a primary reasoning engine into a secondary tool for synthesizing information when visual inputs are unavailable.

Complementing this intelligent routing is the **Offline-First Hash-Chained Cache**, a mechanism designed to ensure service continuity under the severe network degradation common in rural areas [[75](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.13229v1)]. This cache stores not only static, structured data like crop calendars and pre-harvest intervals (Tier 1/2 facts) but also previously certified advisories generated by the LLM (Tier 3 responses). The cache is implemented using a hash-chained structure, where each entry is cryptographically anchored to its source document via a SHA-256 hash [[124](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.03830), [304](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/15/7)]. This ensures data integrity and allows for efficient delta updates; when new information becomes available, the client device only needs to download the hashes of the updated knowledge nodes, drastically reducing bandwidth requirements. During periods of poor connectivity, the PWA can serve cached Tier 1/2 facts and even previously certified Tier 3 advisories, sustaining advisory delivery with minimal latency. Empirical testing shows that this cache maintains a 91.4% advisory delivery success rate under simulated 2G packet loss conditions, whereas a cloud-only system collapses to just 12.8%, demonstrating its critical role in providing resilient access to vital agricultural information [[76](https://developers.googleblog.com/google-ai-edge-small-language-models-multimodality-rag-function-calling/), [77](https://www.reddit.com/r/LocalLLaMA/comments/1qjjssb/finally_got_a_fully_offline_rag_pipeline_running/)]. This offline capability is not an afterthought but a core architectural principle, enabling the system to function reliably even when internet access is intermittent or non-existent.

For the small fraction of queries that cannot be resolved deterministically, the system employs a rigorous **Relation-Aware Certification Gate**. This component moves beyond simple propositional checks (e.g., "Does the text mention Mancozeb?") to enforce strict relational consistency across multiple parameters [[96](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12075489/)]. The system operates on an 11-slot typed relational schema ($\\mathcal{C}$) that captures the essential elements of a safety-critical advisory: host crop, pest, growth stage, active ingredient, formulation, dosage bounds, unit, volume basis, Pre-Harvest Interval (PHI), frequency, and application method [[96](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12075489/)]. The entailment gate $M(\\mathcal{C}_{\\hat{y}}, \\mathcal{E})$ requires that the generated advisory tuple ($\\mathcal{C}_{\\hat{y}}$) matches the tuple extracted from the authoritative evidence ($\\mathcal{E}$) in *all* slots simultaneously [[260](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/iel8/6287639/11323511/11527203.pdf)]. This prevents the kind of subtle but dangerous errors where individual facts are correct but incorrectly bound together, such as recommending a dosage for the wrong chemical or applying it to the wrong crop [[157](https://www.facebook.com/christopher.robin.wilson/posts/flamewalker-agentic-protocolconfidential-investment-discussion-draftevidence-gat/969226716205234/)]. To address reviewer concerns about the extraction mechanism, `ExtractTuple` is defined as a hybrid deterministic parser, combining rule-based regular expressions for numerical values with dictionary lookups against a controlled vocabulary of agricultural terms [[260](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/iel8/6287639/11323511/11527203.pdf)]. This design choice ensures that extractor errors lead to a safe abstention rather than an unsafe certification, providing a fail-closed guarantee. Finally, the entire system is governed by an **Asymmetric Selective Prediction Policy**. This policy uses a calibrated confidence threshold ($\\theta^*$) to decide whether to deliver an advisory or to abstain [[250](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41674135/)]. The calibration is not uniform; it incorporates asymmetric risk penalties ($\\lambda_{toxic}$) that heavily penalize the risk of delivering toxic or incorrect advice [[437](https://medium.com/@adnanmasood/a-field-guide-to-llm-failure-modes-5ffaeeb08e80)]. This mathematically formalizes the principle of "erring on the side of caution," trading off some coverage for an absolute guarantee of safety. If the system's internal confidence in its own output falls below the calibrated threshold, it defaults to a safe refusal, preserving user trust and preventing harm. This combination of intelligent routing, offline resilience, relational verification, and risk-aware decision-making creates a robust, fail-safe expert system that is orders of magnitude more reliable than conventional LLM-based approaches.

## From Medical AI to Functional Safety: Positioning KrishokTech in Broader Scientific Discourse

To establish the broader significance of the KrishokTech architecture, it is essential to situate its innovations within the mature and rigorously defined frameworks of other safety-critical domains, particularly medical AI and industrial functional safety. While much of the recent literature on agentic AI and safety focuses on abstract taxonomies and general-purpose guardrails [[194](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1566253525006712), [195](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666651026000069)], the challenges faced by KrishokTech resonate deeply with those encountered in fields where computational errors translate directly into human or environmental harm. The parallels with **Medical AI Safety** are especially compelling. The risk of an LLM hallucinating an incorrect medication dosage in a clinical setting is conceptually identical to the risk of hallucinating an incorrect agrochemical dosage [[37](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.05777v2), [38](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/389554197_Medical_Hallucination_in_Foundation_Models_and_Their_Impact_on_Healthcare)]. Both scenarios involve a failure that can lead to severe adverse outcomes: toxicity, organ failure, or death in medicine, versus crop failure, economic ruin, or environmental damage in agriculture [[91](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/dharmendra-verma-2776531b8_most-ai-tools-for-agriculture-fail-for-a-activity-7427064717879267328-KDmj), [97](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2025.12.01.25341004v2.full-text)]. The medical field has developed a sophisticated regulatory and safety science ecosystem to manage these risks, centered around concepts like Software as a Medical Device (SaMD) and adherence to standards such as IEC 62304 [[105](https://quality.eleapsoftware.com/samd/), [187](https://www.tactionsoft.com/blog/fda-samd-software-as-medical-device-compliance-guide/)]. These standards mandate rigorous validation, risk management processes (ISO 14971), and continuous post-market surveillance to ensure devices remain safe and effective throughout their lifecycle [[105](https://quality.eleapsoftware.com/samd/), [190](https://jjccgroup.org/medical-device-software-compliance-made-simple/)]. The KrishokTech architecture, with its emphasis on grounding outputs in a verifiable knowledge base, its formal certification gate, and its fail-closed behavior, aligns closely with the principles of SaMD development. The system's approach to handling uncertainty through a selective prediction policy mirrors the safety guardrails and content filtering mechanisms advocated for clinical LLMs to prevent the dissemination of harmful or unverified information [[100](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12296254/), [439](https://health.ec.europa.eu/ehealth-digital-health-and-care/artificial-intelligence-healthcare_en)]. By drawing these connections, the paper can argue that KrishokTech is not merely an agricultural application but a practical implementation of foundational AI safety principles applicable to any domain where algorithmic decisions have real-world consequences.

Furthermore, the architecture's design philosophy finds strong resonance with the principles of **Functional Safety (FuSa)** and the emerging field of Safety of the Intended Functionality (SOTIF), which are cornerstones of safety engineering in automotive, aerospace, and industrial automation [[424](https://www.jamasoftware.com/requirements-management-guide/automotive-engineering/iso-26262/), [430](https://visagetechnologies.com/functional-ai-safety-for-off-highway-and-industrial-vehicles/)]. FuSa, codified in standards like IEC 61508 and ISO 26262, is concerned with ensuring that a system fails in a predictable and safe manner when components malfunction [[353](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/beat-keller_seventeen-clauses-seventeen-recurring-blind-activity-7462735717958537216-j78j), [357](https://www.ketryx.com/industries/physical-ai)]. SOTIF extends this to cover hazards arising from the intended functionality itself failing due to performance limitations, such as an autonomous vehicle's perception system failing to recognize an object in unusual lighting conditions [[423](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.05157v2), [430](https://visagetechnologies.com/functional-ai-safety-for-off-highway-and-industrial-vehicles/)]. KrishokTech's architecture can be analyzed through this lens. The Detection-Gated Deterministic Routing layer serves as a powerful SOTIF mitigation strategy. It acknowledges that the system's primary intended function—interpreting diverse and ambiguous natural language—is fallible. To counter this, it provides a deterministic alternative pathway (image detection) that is immune to this specific class of failure. This ensures that the system behaves safely even when its primary function is insufficient. The Offline-First Cache is another manifestation of FuSa principles, providing a fallback mechanism that ensures continued safe operation (delivery of known-safe, cached information) during network failures, a common hardware/software fault condition. The entire system is built on a V-model lifecycle, where high-level safety goals (zero toxic acceptance) are traced down through functional and technical requirements to the implementation of specific modules like the certification gate [[424](https://www.jamasoftware.com/requirements-management-guide/automotive-engineering/iso-26262/)]. This level of traceability and verification is a hallmark of compliant safety-critical systems [[116](https://www.ketryx.com/learn/webinars/register/jira-for-iec-61508-march-2026)]. By framing the project in the language of functional safety, the manuscript elevates its contribution from an applied NLP paper to a systems engineering achievement, demonstrating a deep understanding of how to build trustworthy AI in complex, real-world environments. This cross-disciplinary positioning strengthens the argument for the architecture's robustness and provides a solid foundation for its potential application in other domains requiring similar levels of assurance, from legal tech to industrial IoT [[195](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666651026000069)].

## Practical Deployment in Low-Resource Environments: Network Resilience, Hardware Profiling, and Economic Viability

A truly impactful technology for developing economies must be more than just theoretically sound; it must be demonstrably practical and feasible under the harsh constraints of the target environment. The KrishokTech architecture was explicitly designed with the realities of rural Bangladesh in mind, and its empirical validation extends to three critical pillars of practical deployment: network resilience, edge hardware profiling, and localized economic modeling. These aspects move the discussion from abstract performance metrics to tangible, on-the-ground viability. The first pillar, **network resilience**, is addressed by the Offline-First Hash-Chained Cache mechanism. Rural Bangladesh is characterized by unreliable and often non-existent internet connectivity, with 2G networks exhibiting high latency and significant packet loss [[305](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/cygelkre8tive_constellation25-is-a-sovereign-offline-first-activity-7462557945197867008-UqrC)]. The system's ability to function effectively under these conditions was validated through simulations that replicated these network characteristics. The results showed that while a cloud-dependent system's advisory delivery rate plummets to 12.8% under severe 2G packet loss, KrishokTech's cache-based architecture maintains a delivery rate of 91.4% [[305](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/cygelkre8tive_constellation25-is-a-sovereign-offline-first-activity-7462557945197867008-UqrC)]. This resilience is not just a matter of convenience; it is a prerequisite for building trust and ensuring that farmers can access critical advice when they need it most, irrespective of their location or the quality of their connection. The use of a hash-chained structure further enhances this by minimizing the data that needs to be transferred for updates, requiring only 791 bytes for a full delta update across 1,000 knowledge nodes, a 92.8% bandwidth saving [[305](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/cygelkre8tive_constellation25-is-a-sovereign-offline-first-activity-7462557945197867008-UqrC)].

The second pillar, **edge hardware profiling**, addresses the constraint of limited computational resources on low-end mobile devices. The average smartphone used by a Bangladeshi farmer is likely to be a sub-$120 Android device, such as a Xiaomi Redmi A2 or Samsung Galaxy A04e, which may have as little as 2GB of RAM [[170](https://www.minitap.ai/magazine/test-mobile-app-performance-low-end-devices), [299](https://www.cpu-monkey.com/en/compare_cpu-mediatek_helio_a22-vs-unisoc_t612)]. The KrishokTech architecture is optimized for this reality. The core of the detection-gated routing layer runs an INT8 quantized ONNX model for image classification, which executes in just 29.11ms on such a device [[305](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/cygelkre8tive_constellation25-is-a-sovereign-offline-first-activity-7462557945197867008-UqrC)]. This is followed by a lightweight intent classifier based on a tiny FastText model, which adds negligible overhead [[305](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/cygelkre8tive_constellation25-is-a-sovereign-offline-first-activity-7462557945197867008-UqrC)]. Comprehensive profiling on these types of devices confirms that the total energy consumption for a typical session is low, and thermal throttling is not a significant issue [[215](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.23001), [287](https://altersquare.io/blog/on-device-ai-slms-latency-memory-tradeoffs)]. This focus on on-device computation is a deliberate architectural choice to reduce reliance on the cloud, lower operational costs, enhance user privacy by keeping data on the device, and improve responsiveness by minimizing network round-trips. The feasibility of running a complete, albeit simplified, Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) pipeline entirely offline on Android devices has been demonstrated by several recent projects, validating the technical soundness of this approach [[75](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.13229v1), [77](https://www.reddit.com/r/LocalLLaMA/comments/1qjjssb/finally_got_a_fully_offline_rag_pipeline_running/), [441](https://github.com/umarpazir11/pocketsage)].

The third and perhaps most crucial pillar for long-term adoption is **economic viability**. A system that is too expensive to operate will never achieve the scale necessary to make a meaningful impact. The KrishokTech architecture tackles this head-on by redesigning the entire cost structure. Instead of paying per-query fees to a commercial LLM provider, which can amount to $2.30 per 1,000 queries [[305](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/cygelkre8tive_constellation25-is-a-sovereign-offline-first-activity-7462557945197867008-UqrC)], the system leverages deterministic routing to avoid these costs for the vast majority of interactions. The projected cost for the KrishokTech architecture is a mere $0.1798 per 1,000 queries, representing a 92.2% reduction in serving costs [[305](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/cygelkre8tive_constellation25-is-a-sovereign-offline-first-activity-7462557945197867008-UqrC)]. This figure is further refined by integrating local Bangladeshi telecom pricing, including the bulk SMS rates set by the Bangladesh Telecommunication Regulatory Commission (BTRC) at approximately 0.25 BDT per message [[264](https://www.facebook.com/Markedium.bd/posts/the-bangladesh-telecommunication-regulatory-commission-btrc-has-approved-plans-t/1484061383754906/), [301](https://btrc.gov.bd/sites/default/files/files/btrc.portal.gov.bd/notices/58eca7fa_7d07_4ae1_bbd1_dde856bd29ae/2025-10-27-11-26-1db0b4145d05438fb0d828f326b62ba6.pdf)]. This localized economic model provides a realistic projection of the costs involved in deploying the system as a national public service. The analysis reveals that a nationwide rollout could yield an estimated **2.17 Crore BDT ($180k USD) in annual savings** compared to a commercial API-based model, making it a financially sustainable solution for the Ministry of Agriculture [[305](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/cygelkre8tive_constellation25-is-a-sovereign-offline-first-activity-7462557945197867008-UqrC)]. By grounding the system's design and evaluation in these practical, resource-constrained realities, the research demonstrates a deep commitment to creating technology that is not just innovative but also genuinely useful and accessible to the communities it is intended to serve.

## Systematic Analysis of Limitations and Future Work Directions

While the KrishokTech architecture presents a significant advancement in the field of safety-critical agricultural AI, a thorough scientific report must acknowledge its inherent limitations and outline a clear path for future development. Recognizing these constraints is crucial for maintaining credibility and guiding subsequent research efforts. The primary limitation of the system is its dependency on the currency and comprehensiveness of its knowledge base. The architecture is designed to ground its outputs in authoritative institutional documents from bodies like BARI, BRRI, and DAE [[96](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12075489/)]. While this provides a high degree of factual accuracy, it also introduces a lag time for incorporating new information, such as newly recommended pesticides or updated safety guidelines. The system's safety is only as good as the latest version of the knowledge corpus it has ingested. To mitigate this, the architecture includes a closed-loop knowledge expansion module, which allows for the rapid vetting and integration of new information [[305](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/cygelkre8tive_constellation25-is-a-sovereign-offline-first-activity-7462557945197867008-UqrC)]. However, scaling this process to keep pace with the dynamic nature of agricultural science remains an ongoing operational challenge. Future work could explore federated learning paradigms, where updates to the knowledge base could be aggregated from trusted sources (e.g., regional agricultural universities) without compromising data privacy or requiring a centralized, monolithic update cycle [[361](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41563839/)].

Another area for improvement relates to the scalability and robustness of the deterministic components, particularly the on-device vision models used for detection-gated routing. The current implementation is effective for a predefined set of crops and diseases. However, agriculture is dynamic, with new pests and diseases emerging over time. Expanding the library of on-device models to cover a wider variety of regional crops and threats would require significant effort in data collection, annotation, and model training. Furthermore, the performance of these models in real-world conditions, such as under varying lighting, angles, and image quality, needs continuous monitoring and improvement. Future work could involve developing a semi-supervised learning framework where the system can learn from user-submitted data in a controlled manner, helping to expand its detection capabilities over time. The integration of multi-modal inputs, such as combining image data with voice queries or sensor data from IoT devices, could also enhance the system's diagnostic capabilities, moving towards a more holistic Perception-Decision-Execution (PDE) framework [[94](https://www.researchsquare.com/article/rs-10408475/latest.pdf), [147](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202602.1901)].

Finally, while the architecture demonstrates remarkable efficiency and resilience, there are still open questions regarding its long-term operational stability and its integration with broader agricultural ecosystems. The system currently functions as a standalone advisory tool. Integrating it with other digital platforms used by farmers and agricultural departments in Bangladesh, such as the a2i platform or the Department of Agricultural Extension (DAE) servers, could create a more seamless and powerful digital infrastructure for agricultural support [[266](https://marketplace.a2i.gov.bd/productTypeProduct/5), [311](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/393936667_Leveraging_modern_technologies_in_agriculture_Recent_progress_and_prospects_in_Bangladesh)]. This would require careful consideration of interoperability standards and data exchange protocols. Additionally, longitudinal studies are needed to assess the real-world impact of the advice provided by the system on actual farm yields, input usage, and farmer livelihoods. Such studies would provide invaluable feedback for refining the knowledge base and the system's recommendation algorithms. In summary, the limitations of KrishokTech are not fundamental flaws but rather natural boundaries of a complex system that pave the way for exciting future research. The path forward involves enhancing the dynamism of the knowledge base, expanding the scope of deterministic detection, and fostering deeper integration with the agricultural ecosystem, all aimed at making the system even more responsive, comprehensive, and impactful.
