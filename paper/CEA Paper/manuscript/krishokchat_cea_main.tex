\begin{abstract}
Large Language Models (LLMs) and standard Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) pipelines demonstrate significant fluency in multilingual agricultural extension; however, generative models lack inherent bounds on factual authority. In safety-critical agricultural domains, fluent text generation can silently misbind pesticides to unauthorized pests, hallucinate chemical dosages, or truncate vital Pre-Harvest Intervals (PHI), posing direct ecological, economic, and human health hazards. This paper presents the \textbf{Bounded-Authority Advisory Architecture (BAA)}, a domain-specialized agricultural decision-support system that separates factual authority from linguistic realization. BAA enforces a fail-closed mathematical certification contract over structured, provenance-carrying evidence records and selectively routes farmer queries among deterministic fact resolution, grounded generation, conversational clarification, and expert escalation. 

Across an empirical battery comprising \Nmisbind{} adversarial relational misbinding attacks, 4,000 multi-register Bengali queries, and triple-blind evaluations by practicing agronomists, BAA achieves a \MisbindDetRate{} rejection rate on corrupted chemical claims, compared to an 80.0\% dangerous acceptance rate in standard lexical RAG and 38.65\% in LLM-as-a-judge verifiers. The five-tier selective resolution ladder resolves \ZeroLLMRate{} of advisory traffic deterministically without invoking generative LLMs, achieving a \LatencySpeedup{} latency reduction and lowering per-query serving costs to \$0.0768/1k queries. In low-resource field deployments, deterministic 11-slot semantic compression eliminates the \SMSHazardLLM{} parameter-omission hazard observed in LLM-generated SMS, while offline-first cryptographic caching sustains 80.3\% advisory delivery under severe 30\% cellular packet loss. These findings demonstrate that subordinating language generation to explicit, bounded factual authority provides a scalable and dependable foundation for agricultural decision support in low-resource environments.
\end{abstract}
