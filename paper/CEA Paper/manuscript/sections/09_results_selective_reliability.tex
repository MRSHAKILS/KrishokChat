\section{Results: Selective Resolution, Calibration, and Robustness (RQ3, RQ4)}
\label{sec:results_selective_reliability}

Calibrated abstention sustains \textbf{84.56\% advisory coverage at a 1.26\% selective-risk level} across 20,112 held-out cases, achieving a better coverage-risk trade-off than all four alternatives. Across linguistic registers, progressive knowledge-base deletion, and varying regulatory risk budgets, the safety boundary holds monotonically.

\subsection{Risk--Coverage Calibration (E04)}
Layer E04 evaluated five selective policies on a 20{,}112-case corpus (12{,}068 train / 4{,}022 dev / 4{,}022 test). The threshold $\theta^*$ was frozen on the development split and transferred to the held-out test split. Table~\ref{tab:risk_coverage_calibration} reports test-split results.

\input{tables/tab7_risk_coverage_calibration.tex}

The calibrated relational policy achieves the lowest test Area Under the Risk--Coverage curve (AURC, \textbf{0.0153}) and Expected Calibration Error (ECE, \textbf{0.0785}) of all five policies. At the frozen threshold $\theta^* = 0.2375$, it sustains \textbf{84.56\% test coverage at a selective risk of 1.26\%}, achieving higher coverage at comparable risk than the conformal baseline (77.62\% at 1.06\%).

\subsection{Safety--Coverage Frontier for Regulatory Policy (E45)}
To evaluate deployment flexibility for policymakers and agricultural extension services with varying risk tolerances, Layer~E45 computes the Pareto frontier of achievable advisory coverage across five regulatory CUAR budgets ($N=20{,}112$, held-out test $n=4{,}022$, Table~\ref{tab:safety_coverage_frontier}).

\input{tables/tab_e45_safety_frontier.tex}

Under an ultra-strict safety budget ($\text{CUAR} \le 0.1\%$), unconstrained LLM coverage collapses to 5.7\% and the LLM-as-a-judge guardrail to 20.8\%. In contrast, BAA sustains \textbf{83.5\% coverage} (95\% CI [82.4, 84.7]) at $\text{CUAR} \le 0.1\%$ and \textbf{84.6\% coverage} at $\text{CUAR} \le 1.0\%$, strictly dominating all heuristic and judge baselines across all five operating points.

\subsection{Knowledge-Base Incompleteness and Monotone Safe Degradation (E44)}
In practice, agricultural knowledge bases are rarely complete. Layer~E44 evaluates system behavior under progressive evidence deletion across 35{,}000 cases (0\% to 50\% of authoritative facts deleted across 5 random seeds $\times$ 1{,}000 queries, Table~\ref{tab:kb_incompleteness_degradation}).

\input{tables/tab_e44_kb_degradation.tex}

As official records are progressively deleted, BAA coverage degrades strictly monotonically from \textbf{85.0\% down to 43.4\%}, while Critical Unsafe Acceptance Rate remains bounded at \textbf{0.00\% across all deletion levels} (95\% CI [0.0, 0.07]). In contrast, unconstrained LLMs escalate in CUAR from 16.1\% up to 36.8\% as missing evidence induces parametric hallucination. This quantitatively confirms Section~\ref{sec:limitations}'s theoretical principle: \textbf{knowledge incompleteness converts strictly into safe abstentions, never dangerous hallucinations.}

\subsection{The Lightweight Intent Router (E25)}
The character $n$-gram router achieves a joint exact match of 78.4\% (95\% CI [72.89, 83.05]) with per-field crop accuracy 96.4\% and intent-type accuracy 100.0\%, at p50 latency 0.3855~ms and a memory footprint of 1.25~MB. The 78.4\% joint-EM figure motivates the design, where the architecture absorbs routing uncertainty in the certification predicate rather than trusting metadata.

\subsection{Root-Cause Failure Taxonomy (E10)}
A manual audit of 100 residual failure/abstention cases assigned each to one of eight categories (Table~\ref{tab:failure_mitigation}): retrieval evidence omission 28\%, generative relational misbinding 22\%, numerical/volumetric parsing ambiguity 14\%, dialectal lexical ambiguity 12\%, regulatory status discrepancy 8\%, temporal/source expiry 6\%, conservative verifier over-refusal 6\%, and calibration underconfidence 4\%. Critically, both dominant failure families were converted into fail-closed abstention rather than dangerous advice: the verifier caught all 22 misbinding attempts (0.0\% dangerous leak) and the coverage threshold converted the 28 omissions into abstentions.

\subsection{Input Robustness: Bengali Register Coverage (E17, E21)}
Bengali input spans at least four registers: standard formal, authentic farmer language, regional dialects (Sylheti/Chittagonian), and romanized Banglish. Detection-gated routing reduces the candidate search space by 75.64\% (2{,}135~$\rightarrow$~516.4 nodes) and lifts Hit@1 on dialects by $+$36.6~pp and on Banglish by $+$39.7~pp. Layer E21 confirms that end-to-end coverage rises from 42.9\% to 89.8\% on dialects, and from 41.6\% to 90.0\% on Banglish, while holding hazard at \textbf{0.0\% in all eight register~$\times$~routing cells} (0/4{,}000; 95\% CI [0.0, 0.09], Table~\ref{tab:multimodal_robustness}).

\subsection{Cost-Gated Semantic Disambiguation and Cross-Crop Misbinding Prevention (E09)}
When farmers pose symptom or treatment questions without specifying the crop ($crop = \emptyset$, e.g., \textit{``patay dag, ki spray korbo?''}), unconstrained RAG suffers a \textbf{38.5\% cross-crop misbinding hazard rate} by retrieving mismatched agronomic manuals and generating toxic dosage recommendations for the wrong plant. We evaluated the cost-gated semantic disambiguation rail across a 400-instance stratified suite ($n=100$ ambiguous symptoms, $n=100$ specified crops, $n=70$ safety-critical banned chemical/crisis probes, $n=50$ dialects, $n=40$ fact-base queries, $n=40$ out-of-scope). The cost-gated pipeline (\texttt{gemini-2.5-flash-lite} / deterministic alias matcher) achieves \textbf{100.0\% gating recall on underspecified queries} (100/100 halted before retrieval) and \textbf{100.0\% pass-through on specified crops} (140/140), driving cross-crop misbinding hazard down to \textbf{0.0\%} while slashing generation token expenditure by \textbf{88.0\%} on ambiguous conversational turns. High-risk safety probes achieve \textbf{100.0\% refusal} (70/70 intercepted, 0.0\% toxic advice leak).

\subsection{Multimodal Perception Safety and Field Replay (E02)}
To evaluate visual perception under realistic field conditions, we executed an end-to-end replay across 436 held-out agricultural images spanning 45 pathological classes across 9 crop groups. In unconstrained multimodal pipelines, monolithic vision models frequently induce cross-family misbinding---such as confusing Solanaceae foliage and prescribing potato fungicides to tomato crops. KrishokChat prevents these dangerous failure modes through a two-tier bounded-authority perception architecture. First, an on-device 10-class YOLO26s edge classifier ($20.82$\,MB ONNX, $99.55\%$ test top-1) isolates crop identity before candidate treatment manuals are retrieved. Downstream diagnosis is then delegated to crop-specific disease specialists operating under calibrated confidence and margin thresholds ($C_d \ge 0.85$, $M_d \ge 0.18$), including an 8-class Chilli specialist ($97.50\%$ accuracy on held-out samples, $n=40$, 5.91\,MB ONNX) and a 10-class Rice specialist covering Tungro virus ($96.25\%$ accuracy on $n=80$). As detailed in Table~\ref{tab:multimodal_field_replay}, this tiered containment completely eliminates cross-crop pesticide hazards ($0.00\%$, 0/436, 95\% CI [0.00, 0.87]) while sustaining a \textbf{97.94\% safe containment rate} through verified direct automation ($48.62\%$), zero-token interactive chip disambiguation ($20.87\%$), and conservative fail-closed abstention ($28.21\%$). Actionable residual errors are strictly confined to 9 intra-crop foliar spot ambiguities ($2.06\%$), completely preventing hazardous cross-crop chemical exposure.

\subsection{Knowledge Integrity and Tamper Detection (E23, E24)}
On tamper detection (E23), 1{,}000 SHA-256 chain mutations were all detected (100.0\%; 95\% CI [99.62, 100.0]) at p95 0.1919~ms verification latency. Knowledge maintenance remains a data operation, not a code operation: 18~minutes closed a real cluster gap (E24, single-cluster case study).
