\subsection{Risk--Coverage Calibration (E04)}
Layer E04 evaluated five selective policies on a 20{,}112-case risk-coverage corpus (12{,}068 train / 4{,}022 dev / 4{,}022 test): raw generator confidence, lexical overlap score, LLM-judge confidence, a conformal abstention baseline, and the calibrated relational policy (BAA). The threshold $\theta^*$ was frozen on the dev split and transferred to the held-out test split. Table~\ref{tab:risk_coverage_calibration} reports the test-split results.

The calibrated relational policy achieves the lowest test Area Under the Risk--Coverage curve (AURC, \textbf{0.0153}) and Expected Calibration Error (ECE, \textbf{0.0785}) of all five policies, with a Brier score of 0.0116. At the frozen threshold $\theta^* = 0.2375$, it sustains \textbf{84.56\% test coverage} at a selective risk of 1.26\% --- higher coverage at comparable risk than the conformal baseline (77.62\% at 1.06\%), and dramatically better than raw generator confidence (10.22\% coverage) or lexical overlap (0.37\%) at their frozen thresholds. At fixed risk budgets the contrast is stark: at $\le$0.5\% and $\le$2\% risk, raw confidence achieves 0\% and the relational policy 84.56\% of test coverage at the risk levels recorded in the layer. We report the tabulated values; the layer's prose summary quotes different numbers and we deliberately cite the metric table instead.

\subsection{The Lightweight Intent Router (E25)}
The character $n$-gram router that feeds the capability layer achieves a joint exact match of 78.4\% (95\% CI [72.89, 83.05]) with per-field crop accuracy 96.4\% and intent-type accuracy 100.0\%, at a p50 latency of 0.3855 ms and a memory footprint of 1.25 MB (3{,}242$\times$ faster than the LLM parsing baseline). We present this as a routing-efficiency result, not as a routing-accuracy claim: the joint match is below the LLM baseline (94.2\%), which is precisely why the architecture absorbs routing uncertainty in the certification predicate rather than trusting metadata. The 78.4\% joint-EM figure motivates the design; the E17/E28 layers show the consequence of absorbing it.

\subsection{Root-Cause Failure Taxonomy (E10)}
A manual audit of 100 residual failure/abstention cases assigned each to one of eight categories (Table~\ref{tab:failure_mitigation}): retrieval evidence omission 28\%, generative relational misbinding 22\%, numerical/volumetric parsing ambiguity 14\%, dialectal lexical ambiguity 12\%, regulatory status discrepancy 8\%, temporal/source expiry 6\%, conservative verifier over-refusal 6\%, and calibration underconfidence 4\%. Critically, both dominant failure families (retrieval omission and relational misbinding) were converted into fail-closed abstention rather than dangerous advice: the verifier caught all 22 misbinding attempts (0.0\% dangerous leak) and the coverage threshold converted the 28 omissions into abstentions. The 6\% conservative-refusal and 4\% underconfidence categories represent the coverage cost of the safety boundary and are the targets of the calibration analysis above.

\subsection{Abstention Cost and Escalation Load}
Abstention is not free: it imposes farmer delay, extension workload, and reduced service coverage. Under the E18 workload model, the safety/refusal handling share (\texttt{T0}+\texttt{T4}) is 10.48\% of traffic; applying the E13 deployment-approval and E24 case-study evidence, the operational implication is that roughly one in ten interactions terminates in a redirect or clarification rather than a certified answer. The calibrated abstention analysis (this section) is the mechanism for controlling that cost: at the frozen threshold, the system answers 84.56\% of its supported traffic while keeping selective risk at 1.26\%.

\subsection{Architectural Ablation: Where the Safety Comes From (E34)}
Layer E34 isolates the causal contribution of each architectural layer with a cumulative ablation (100 cases per tier, live API calls for generative tiers). The base LLM (L0) certifies 61.0\% correctly with 14.0\% CUAR and 13.0\% abstention. Adding lexical retrieval (L1) raises correctness to 72.0\% and cuts CUAR to 4.0\%; hybrid dense retrieval (L2) reaches 72.0\% correctness at 98.0\% operational coverage but still 3.0\% CUAR. The prompted safety guardrail (L3) makes things worse on three of four metrics (62.0\% correctness, 10.0\% CUAR, coverage 78.0\%), consistent with the judge-guardrail findings of E27 and E30. Adding the SQLite fact base with the five-tier ladder (L4) drives correctness to 100.0\% and CUAR to 0.0\% at 70.0\% certified coverage; the full 11-slot BAA (L5) matches L4 on every accuracy metric and adds only latency/cost gains (4.2 ms $\rightarrow$ 3.8 ms; \$0.15 $\rightarrow$ \$0.08 per 1k). We state the honest reading: L4-to-L5 does \emph{not} itself demonstrate the verifier's safety contribution --- that evidence lives in the E28 B5-vs-B6 contrast --- and the coverage drop from 98\% to 70\% is larger than the correctness gain from 72\% to 100\%, so the ablation is reported as a coverage-for-certainty trade, not a monotonic improvement.
