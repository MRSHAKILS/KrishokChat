\section{Results: Temporal Validity and Knowledge Integrity (RQ4, RQ5)}
\label{sec:results_temporal_governance}

\subsection{Temporal Invalidation and Gazette Adherence (E30)}
The temporal conflict benchmark (100 cases per system, live API calls) pitted an obsolete pre-2024 record against the current authorized gazette. BAA achieved \textbf{100.0\% current-gazette adherence} (95\% CI [96.30, 100.0]) with 0.0\% obsolete leakage (CI [0.0, 3.7]); the unconstrained LLM leaked the obsolete record in 39.0\% of cases, lexical mixed RAG in 39.0\%, and the LLM-judge guardrail in 75.0\%. The mechanism is structural: the certification predicate (Eq.~\ref{eq:certification_rule}) requires $\operatorname{Current}(e)$, which is evaluated against the authority manifest at resolution time. No model temperature or knowledge-cutoff randomness can override a deterministic authority comparison. We emphasize that adherence and leakage are reported as separate metrics because a third (unrecorded) outcome existed for baselines.

\subsection{Source Authority Hierarchy and Conflict Resolution}
The authority stratification of Section~\ref{sec:knowledge_governance} (MoA Regulatory $\succ$ BARI/BRRI $\succ$ Legacy $\succ$ Secondary) is enforced at resolution time. When overlapping records disagree, the resolver selects by authority rank then by publication timestamp, and the certification predicate requires both authority and currency. This directly implements the distinction between \emph{authoritative but obsolete} and \emph{recent but secondary}: only records passing both predicates can satisfy Eq.~\eqref{eq:certification_rule}. The E30 result above is the empirical demonstration for the obsolete-but-retrievable case; source-conflict cases that cannot be resolved by the hierarchy fail closed to abstention.

\subsection{Cache Integrity and Tamper Detection (E23)}
Layer E23 evaluated the offline fact pack's tamper-evidence and differential-update behavior: 1{,}000 single-bit and swap mutations of the SHA-256 chain were all detected (\textbf{100.0\%}, CI [99.62, 100.0]); an invalidation delta carried 791 bytes versus a 10{,}985-byte full pack (92.8\% bandwidth reduction); and chain verification on the client completed at p95 0.1919 ms. We report this as a deployment-integrity result: the offline evidence base cannot be silently altered, so a disconnected client's certified outputs remain traceable to the record that certified them. (The layer records an internal disagreement on the propagation-completeness percentage; we therefore cite only tamper detection, delta size, and verification latency, which are unambiguous.)

\subsection{Knowledge Maintenance and Coverage Growth (E24)}
The maintenance loop closes knowledge gaps without model retraining. Layer E24's single-cluster case study (chili anthracnose) added two validated facts in 18 minutes, closing 100.0\% of the cluster's 55 recurring gap queries (95\% CI [93.47, 100.0]) and lifting system coverage from 58.7\% to 64.2\% (+5.5 pp; CIs [55.62, 61.71] / [61.18, 67.11]), at a marginal return of 27.5 newly resolvable queries per fact and 3.06 per authoring minute. We report this as a single-cluster case study, not a productivity distribution, and note the authoring contract in Section~\ref{sec:knowledge_governance} (no LLM-to-production fact insertion).
