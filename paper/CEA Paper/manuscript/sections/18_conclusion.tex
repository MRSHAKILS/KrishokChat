\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We introduced and evaluated the Bounded-Authority Advisory Architecture (BAA), an agricultural decision-support architecture in which factual authority is explicitly separated from generative language realization. BAA certifies every recommendation through an 11-slot, single-record, fail-closed contract, and selectively resolves each farmer query among deterministic resolution, grounded generation with verification, clarification, abstention, and escalation. The architecture treats the LLM as a language realization component, not as the authority for safety-critical agricultural facts.

The evidence shows the mechanism works across the causal chain. Typed single-record binding rejects 100.0\% of 10{,}000 relational misbindings and 100.0\% of 11{,}000 metamorphic mutations, where lexical, dense, citation, and judge baselines certify 42--82\% of corrupted claims. On live end-to-end evaluation, BAA certifies 97.0\% correct advice with 0.0\% observed critical unsafe acceptance (and a stated CI), at a coverage cost of 33\% safe abstention; three agronomists rated its outputs 4.82/5 with a 100\% chemical-safety pass. Calibrated abstention delivers 84.56\% coverage at 1.26\% risk. The deterministic share of the resolution ladder cuts modeled latency 2.28$\times$ and cost to \$0.0767 per 1{,}000 queries, deterministic SMS preserves 100\% of safety slots where LLM SMS loses 64.4\%, and offline-first caching sustains 91.4\% delivery at 15\% loss.

What remains unproven is equally clear. There is no farmer-outcome data, no national-scale deployment, and no same-model replication of the escalation layer. Several newer layers lack full registry verification blocks; two exploratory layers show an unresolved CUAR inconsistency; and the small-$n$ layers cannot support the effect sizes they hint at. These are honest boundaries of the current evidence, and they define the next experimental cycle: larger independent annotation, a same-model escalation study, hardened verification blocks for the live-API layers, and --- eventually --- field pilots with real extension officers. Within those boundaries, the paper demonstrates a dependable and auditable pattern for applying generative language to high-consequence agricultural decision support.
