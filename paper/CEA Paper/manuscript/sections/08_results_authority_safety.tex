\section{Results: Safety Verification and Authority Enforcement (RQ2)}
\label{sec:results_authority_safety}

BAA rejected \textbf{100.0\% of 10,000 adversarial relational-misbinding attacks} (95\% CI [99.96, 100.0]) and 100.0\% of 11,000 metamorphic mutations. The safety boundary holds across every attack family evaluated. This section traces the structural mechanism behind that result and stress-tests it from every angle.

Two exploratory layers (E32, oracle-vs-predicted routing; E33, high-confidence wrong metadata) recorded non-zero CUAR values for BAA (11.0\% and 10.0\%). In these exploratory layers, the certification predicate (Eq.~\ref{eq:certification_rule}) was deliberately bypassed to test whether routing metadata perturbation alone could induce unsafe outputs; the non-zero CUAR reflects the absence of the certification gate, not a failure of the gate when present. Both are reported as exploratory and discussed in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.

\subsection{Relational Misbinding Defence (E02)}
Layer E02 evaluated 10{,}000 adversarial cases (10 corruption families $\times$ 1{,}000 cases), each presenting juxtaposed valid facts from different records designed to trigger relational misbinding. BAA rejected \textbf{100.0\%} (0/10{,}000 false certifications; 95\% CI [99.96, 100.0]). A lexical substring matcher certified 80.0\% of corrupted claims as safe (CI [79.20, 80.77]); dense retrieval 37.56\%; citation-alignment 43.14\%; LLM-as-judge 38.65\%; vanilla RAG 72.65\%; the partial 8-slot matcher (B5) 24.5\%. Single-record joint binding makes cross-record misbinding impossible by construction: a claim whose 11 slots cannot all be satisfied from a single accredited record hash fails certification regardless of how the slots individually appear in the retrieved pool. \subsection{Metamorphic Authority Testing (E28)}
Layer E28 generalizes the attack: 11~mutation operators (crop, pest, active ingredient, formulation, dosage overdose, dosage unit, water volume, interval shortening, PHI shortening, regulatory polarity flip, provenance-hash corruption) $\times$ 1{,}000 cases each~$=$~11{,}000 adversarial mutations, run against seven verification systems. BAA rejected \textbf{100.0\%} (95\% CI [99.97, 100.0]) with 0.0\% false certification. B5 is the isolation baseline: it shares all architecture with BAA except the four missing slots. Its failure on exactly those four families (and only those: 0.0\% rejection on water volume, spray interval, PHI, and provenance hash) demonstrates that the safety gain is attributable to slot completeness, not to confounding architectural differences. The LLM-as-judge rejected only 72.25\%, the citation-alignment baseline 57.33\%, dense matcher 37.05\%, BM25 36.36\%, and unconstrained LLM 18.41\% (false-certifying 81.59\%).

\subsection{Counterfactual Evidence Binding (E05)}
Layer E05 tested certification under counterfactual mutations (dose scaled 5$\times$, PHI shortened to 3~days): 2{,}000 evidence pairs per system. BAA certified 0.0\% of mutated claims (95\% CI [0.0, 0.19]) while certifying 100.0\% of true evidence, giving a Counterfactual Binding Consistency (CBC) of 1.0000. Vanilla RAG certified 72.65\% of mutated claims (CI [70.65, 74.56]; CBC~0.2735), a lexical matcher 59.55\% (CBC~0.4045), and an LLM-as-judge 38.65\% (CBC~0.5705). The verifier is evidence-bound rather than prior-bound.

\subsection{Parametric-vs-Evidence Conflict (E29)}
In Layer E29, each of five LLMs was run in four modes: direct generation, standard RAG, prompted judge, and BAA. When retrieved evidence conflicted with the model's parametric prior, direct generation showed parametric intrusion rates of 30--56\%, standard RAG still leaked 8--30\%, and a prompted judge leaked 2--16\%. The BAA mode achieved \textbf{100.0\% evidence adherence and 0.0\% parametric intrusion} in every model/mode cell, because the deterministic 11-slot resolver makes parametric memory irrelevant: it certifies the record when the contract holds and abstains otherwise. Note the effective sample granularity caveat reported in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.

\subsection{Temporal Source Authority (E30)}
BAA achieved \textbf{100.0\% current-gazette adherence} (95\% CI [96.30, 100.0]) with 0.0\% obsolete leakage (CI [0.0, 3.7]) in the temporal conflict benchmark (100 cases per system). The unconstrained LLM cited the obsolete record in 39.0\% of cases; lexical mixed RAG in 39.0\%; and the LLM-judge guardrail in \textbf{75.0\%}. The mechanism is structural: the $\operatorname{Current}(e)$ predicate is evaluated against the authority manifest at resolution time, so no model temperature or knowledge-cutoff randomness can override a deterministic authority comparison.

\subsection{Cross-Modal Perception Uncertainty (E31)}
When text and visual crop identity conflicted, BAA triggered a safe clarification request in \textbf{100.0\%} of conflicted inputs (95\% CI [98.77, 100.0]) and delivered 0.0\% wrong chemical recommendations. The unconstrained multimodal LLM delivered the wrong recommendation in 54.0\% of cases. On the harm axis, BAA's advantage over the LLM-judge is not in unsafe output (both 0.0\%) but in the clarification rate (100.0 vs 26.0\%). Per the project's artifact constraints, perception inputs are classification labels, not detection bounding boxes.

\subsection{Slot Ablation: What Each Contract Field Buys (E03)}
Layer E03 ablated each of the 11 contract slots individually (10{,}000 cases per configuration, 13 configurations). Removing each slot raises dangerous acceptance as follows: dosage bounds $+$31.6~pp, regulatory polarity $+$17.8~pp, active ingredient $+$14.2~pp, host crop $+$11.4~pp, target pathogen $+$8.2~pp, solvent volume $+$7.1~pp, formulation $+$5.8~pp, unit $+$5.4~pp, growth stage $+$4.9~pp, PHI $+$2.7~pp, spray interval $+$1.9~pp. Removing all typed constraints collapses the system to the 80.0\% hazard of a lexical substring matcher. The dosage-bound and regulatory-polarity slots are individually load-bearing: their ablation alone creates the two largest hazard surges in the battery.
