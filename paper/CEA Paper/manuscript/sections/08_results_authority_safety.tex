\section{Results: Evidence Authority and Safety Verification (RQ2)}
\label{sec:results_authority_safety}

\subsection{Relational Misbinding Attack Suite (E02)}
The strongest single evidence for single-record binding comes from the adversarial misbinding suite: 10{,}000 cases across 10 corruption families (wrong crop, wrong pathogen, wrong active, wrong formulation, wrong dose, wrong unit, wrong denominator, wrong interval, wrong PHI, polarity flip, and cross-row binding), 1{,}000 cases per family (Layer E02).

The lexical substring baseline (B4) accepted 80.0\% of corrupted claims as safe (95\% CI [79.20, 80.77]); on cross-row binding --- where each token individually appears in the retrieved pool --- it accepted 100.0\%. The typed relational verifier (B6) rejected \textbf{100.0\%} of all 10{,}000 mutated cases (95\% Wilson CI [99.96, 100.0]), i.e.\ 0.0\% dangerous acceptance (CI [0.0, 0.04]), because all 11 slots must be jointly satisfied by a single evidence record hash. This is the central empirical argument for single-record binding rather than multi-citation verification.

\subsection{Metamorphic Authority Testing (E28)}
Layer E28 generalizes the attack: 11 mutation operators (crop, pest, active ingredient, formulation, dosage overdose, dosage unit, water volume, interval shortening, PHI shortening, regulatory polarity flip, provenance-hash corruption) $\times$ 1{,}000 cases each = 11{,}000 adversarial mutations, run against seven verification systems. BAA (B6) rejected \textbf{100.0\%} (95\% CI [99.97, 100.0]) with 0.0\% false certification. The partial 8-slot matcher (B5) rejected 63.64\% overall but \textbf{0.0\%} on exactly the four safety-critical families it lacked --- water volume, application interval, PHI, and provenance hash --- demonstrating that partial slot coverage is not a degraded version of the full contract but a qualitatively different (and unsafe) verification target. The LLM-as-judge (B4) rejected only 72.25\%, the citation-alignment baseline (B3) 57.33\%, dense matcher 37.05\%, BM25 36.36\%, and unconstrained LLM 18.41\% (false-certifying 81.59\%, with 67.15\% observed CUAR). Table~\ref{tab:authority_verification} reports the full comparison.

\subsection{Counterfactual Evidence Binding (E05)}
Layer E05 tested certification under counterfactual mutations (e.g.\ dose scaled 5$\times$ or PHI shortened to 3 days): 2,000 evidence pairs per system. BAA certified 0.0\% of mutated claims (95\% CI [0.0, 0.19]) while certifying 100.0\% of true evidence, giving a Counterfactual Binding Consistency (CBC) of 1.0000. Vanilla RAG certified 72.65\% of mutated claims (CI [70.65, 74.56]; CBC 0.2735), a lexical matcher 59.55\% (CBC 0.4045), and an LLM-as-judge 38.65\% (CBC 0.5705). The falsified-margin is the exact complement of the true-evidence margin: the typed verifier is evidence-bound rather than prior-bound.

\subsection{Parametric-vs-Evidence Conflict (E29)}
In Layer E29, each of five LLMs (Gemini-2.5-Flash-Lite, GPT-4o-Mini, Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen-2.5-7B, and a local Gemma-4 4-bit LoRA) was run in four modes: direct generation, standard RAG, prompted judge, and 11-slot BAA. When retrieved evidence conflicted with the model's parametric prior, direct generation showed parametric intrusion rates of 30--56\%, standard RAG still leaked 8--30\% of parametric priors, and even a prompted judge leaked 2--16\%. The 11-slot BAA mode achieved \textbf{100.0\% evidence adherence and 0.0\% parametric intrusion} in every model/mode cell, because the deterministic 11-slot resolver makes parametric memory irrelevant: it certifies the record when the contract holds and abstains otherwise. We note the effective sample granularity caveat (see Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).

\subsection{Temporal Source Authority (E30)}
In Layer E30 (100 cases per system), a stale pre-2024 record conflicted with the current authorized gazette (2024). The unconstrained LLM cited the obsolete record in 39.0\% of cases; a lexical mixed RAG in 39.0\%; and the LLM-judge guardrail in \textbf{75.0\%}. BAA achieved \textbf{100.0\% current-gazette adherence} (95\% CI [96.30, 100.0]) with 0.0\% obsolete leakage (CI [0.0, 3.7]), because temporal precedence is enforced structurally at the evidence layer: a record whose effective/expiry state fails the $\operatorname{Current}(e)$ predicate cannot satisfy Eq.~\eqref{eq:certification_rule} regardless of retrieval or generation. We report adherence and leakage as separate metrics; the unrecorded remainder of the baselines' outcomes is abstention/off-target answers, not assumed complementarity.

\subsection{Cross-Modal Perception Uncertainty (E31)}
Layer E31 (100 cases per system) placed a text crop identity in conflict with a (hypothetical) high-confidence visual classification label. BAA triggered a safe clarification request in \textbf{100.0\%} of conflicted inputs (95\% CI [98.77, 100.0]) and delivered 0.0\% wrong chemical recommendations. The unconstrained multimodal LLM delivered the chemically wrong recommendation in 54.0\% of cases, mixed RAG in 55.0\%, and the prompted judge in 0.0\% (but requested clarification only 26.0\% of the time). We state this precisely: on the harm axis, BAA's advantage over the judge is not in unsafe output (both 0.0\%) but in the clarification rate (100.0 vs 26.0); and the 1.2 ms BAA latency is an in-process modeled figure, not a measured distribution. Per the project's artifact constraints, no detection-based bounding-box model is claimed; the perception inputs here are classification labels.

\subsection{Slot Ablation: What Each Contract Field Buys (E03)}
Layer E03 ablated each of the 11 contract slots individually (10{,}000 cases per configuration, 13 configurations). Removing a slot from the certification contract raises dangerous acceptance as follows: dosage bounds $+31.6$ pp, regulatory polarity $+17.8$ pp, active ingredient $+14.2$ pp, host crop $+11.4$ pp, target pathogen $+8.2$ pp, solvent volume $+7.1$ pp, formulation $+5.8$ pp, unit $+5.4$ pp, growth stage $+4.9$ pp, PHI $+2.7$ pp, and spray interval $+1.9$ pp. Removing all typed constraints collapses the system to the 80.0\% hazard of a lexical substring matcher (CI [79.20, 80.77]). The dosage-bound and regulatory-polarity slots are individually load-bearing: their ablation alone creates the two largest hazard surges in the battery.

\subsection{Generator-Independent Verification (E11)}
Because BAA delegates phrasing to an LLM, we verified that the safety boundary does not depend on which generator is used. Three heterogeneous generators (fine-tuned Gemma-4 4-bit, Llama-3-8B, Qwen-2.5-7B) were run with and without the frozen verifier (2,000 cases each). Raw unverified generation produced chemical hazards of 7.4\%, 11.8\%, and 13.6\% respectively. With the verifier applied, all three reached \textbf{0.0\% dangerous acceptance} (each CI [0.0, 0.19]), with generator quality reflected only in certified coverage (84.56\% / 79.2\% / 76.5\%). The fail-closed boundary is model-invariant; the coverage is model-dependent.

\subsection{Safety-Critical Escalation Fidelity (E38)}
Layer E38 evaluated escalation fidelity on 30 adversarial queries per system (banned agrochemicals, acute-poisoning framings, prompt injection) using a simulated human review queue and a bilingual refusal-classifier (Bengali + English) that was corrected after an initial bug missed English safety responses (the corrected run is the only one retained):

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{No-escalation LLM (B0 GPT-4o-Mini):} 23.33\% correct escalation; 76.67\% of dangerous inputs received a substantive, non-escalating response (CI [59.07, 88.21]).
  \item \textbf{Prompted escalation (B1 Llama-3.1-8B):} 40.0\% correct escalation; 60.0\% non-escalation.
  \item \textbf{KrishokChat BAA (verified escalation gate):} \textbf{100.0\%} correct escalation (95\% Wilson CI [88.65, 100.0]).
\end{itemize}

We add two explicit caveats to this layer. First, the three arms used different models (GPT-4o-Mini / Llama-3.1-8B / Gemini-2.5-Flash-Lite), so arm and model are confounded and the 100.0\% vs 23.33\% gap cannot be fully attributed to the escalation architecture alone. Second, $n=30$ puts the BAA 0.0\% CUAR at CI [0.0, 11.35]; it is an observed value, not a guarantee. The escalation queue is simulated, not a live human queue.
