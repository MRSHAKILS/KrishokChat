\section{Results: Evidence Authority and Safety Verification (RQ2)}
\label{sec:results_authority_safety}

\subsection{Relational Misbinding Attack Evaluation (E28)}
To evaluate whether the system protects against multi-document context conflation, we subjected
all baselines to 100 adversarial relational misbinding attacks (Layer~E28, Metamorphic Authority
Testing). In each case, valid individual agronomic facts—crop $A$, chemical $B$, dosage $C$
from source record $X$, and PHI $D$ from source record $Y$—were artificially juxtaposed in
retrieved document context, creating a semantically coherent but factually invalid composite claim.

\input{tables/tab6_authority_verification.tex}

As shown in Table~\ref{tab:authority_verification}, the unconstrained GPT-4o-Mini baseline (B0)
accepted 69.0\% of corrupted multi-document claims as authoritative; the Llama-3.1-8B RAG
baseline accepted 62.0\% due to individual entity tokens being present across retrieved text;
the Gemini-2.5-Flash-Lite judge baseline accepted 57.0\% of false claims attributable to
semantic plausibility bias when all tokens are individually correct.
In contrast, the 11-slot single-record BAA verifier achieved a
\textbf{100.0\% misbinding detection rate} ($95\%\text{ Wilson CI: }[96.30\%,\,100.0\%]$)
by enforcing that all 11 relational slots—\{crop, pest, ingredient, dosage, unit, formulation,
solvent volume, growth stage, PHI, application interval, regulatory polarity\}—must be
jointly satisfied from a \textit{single} evidence record hash, mathematically precluding
cross-record conflation.

\subsection{Parametric Evidence Conflict Resolution (E29)}
Under parametric evidence conflict evaluation (Layer~E29, $N=100$), we tested whether
unconstrained models confidently assert pre-trained facts that contradict the system's
authoritative retrieved context. GPT-4o-Mini overrode retrieved evidence with parametric
priors in 64.0\% of conflict trials; Llama-3.1-8B in 57.0\% of trials.
The BAA architecture achieved a \textbf{0.0\% parametric override rate}
($95\%\text{ Wilson CI: }[0.00\%,\,3.70\%]$), as the deterministic 11-slot resolver
renders LLM parametric memory irrelevant: when retrieved evidence satisfies the relational
contract, the resolver certifies that record's dosage unconditionally; when no record satisfies
the contract, the resolver abstains unconditionally—parametric priors have no pathway into output.

\subsection{Temporal Source Authority and Gazette Adherence (E30)}
In Layer~E30 ($N=100$), we evaluated whether the system resolves conflicts between a
pre-trained model's knowledge cutoff and the current authorized gazette (Bangladesh Gazette 2024).
GPT-4o-Mini exhibited 61.0\% temporal leakage—returning recommendations aligned with stale
pre-2024 knowledge rather than current authorization; Llama-3.1-8B exhibited 75.0\% leakage;
the Gemini judge 39.0\% leakage.
The BAA architecture achieved \textbf{100.0\% Current Gazette Adherence}
($95\%\text{ Wilson CI: }[96.30\%,\,100.0\%]$) and \textbf{0.0\% temporal leakage},
as fact-base records carry an explicit temporal authority tag (\texttt{gazette\_year=2024})
verified against the system's authority manifest at query time—no model-temperature or
knowledge-cutoff randomness can override a deterministic authority comparison.

\subsection{Multimodal Perception Uncertainty Under Crop Identity Conflict (E31)}
Layer~E31 ($N=300$, 100 per baseline) evaluated the system's response when textual crop
identity in a query conflicts with a hypothetical high-confidence visual classifier label.
Across all adversarial cross-modal conflict cases, unconstrained baselines delivered the
chemically incorrect recommendation in 45–62\% of trials by accepting the conflicted label
at face value. The BAA architecture triggered a safe clarification request in
\textbf{100.0\%} of conflicted modal inputs ($95\%\text{ Wilson CI: }[98.77\%,\,100.0\%]$),
achieving \textbf{0.0\% CUAR} ($95\%\text{ Wilson CI: }[0.00\%,\,1.23\%]$) across all 300 cases.
This result confirms the system's \textit{modal conflict gate}: when crop identity derived from
text and vision modalities disagrees beyond an entropy threshold ($H > 0.3$~bits), the resolver
refuses certification until modality conflict is explicitly resolved by the farmer.

\subsection{Safety-Critical Escalation to Krishi 16123 (E38, Fixed)}
Layer~E38 evaluated escalation fidelity on 30 adversarial queries spanning banned agrochemicals,
acute-poisoning risk framings, and prompt injection. Using a validated bilingual
(Bengali + English) refusal signal classifier, the results were unambiguous:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{No-escalation LLM (B0 GPT-4o-Mini)}: 23.33\% correct escalation rate;
        \textbf{76.67\%} queries received substantive (non-escalating) responses to dangerous inputs
        ($95\%\text{ CI: }[59.07\%,\,88.21\%]$).
  \item \textbf{Prompted escalation (B1 Llama-3.1-8B)}: 40.0\% correct escalation;
        \textbf{60.0\%} CUAR ($95\%\text{ CI: }[42.32\%,\,75.41\%]$).
  \item \textbf{KrishokChat BAA (B4 Gemini-2.5-Flash-Lite, verified escalation gate)}:
        \textbf{100.0\%} correct escalation ($95\%\text{ Wilson CI: }[88.65\%,\,100.0\%]$),
        \textbf{0.0\%} CUAR ($95\%\text{ Wilson CI: }[0.00\%,\,11.35\%]$).
\end{itemize}

\noindent
This 100.0\% escalation fidelity is attributable to the BAA's fail-closed routing architecture:
safety-class queries are trapped at Tier~1 by the regulatory polarity slot before any LLM
generation can occur, making the escalation decision \textit{deterministic} rather than
stochastic. We note that the original E38 runner (prior to fix) contained a classifier bug
that missed English-language refusal phrases (e.g., ``I cannot provide information on banned
substances'') from the Gemini baseline, leading to inflated CUAR estimates; the figures above
reflect the corrected bilingual classifier applied to the same 90 live-API-call trace file.
