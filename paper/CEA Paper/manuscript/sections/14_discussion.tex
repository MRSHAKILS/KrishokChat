\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What Bounded Factual Authority Buys}
The experimental evidence across evaluation layers converges on a coherent systems principle: agricultural evidence is fundamentally relational. A recommendation is only as valid as its \emph{jointly bound} slots. Simple retrieval or citation can misbind those slots (E02: 80.0\% dangerous acceptance for a lexical baseline; E05: 72.65\% for vanilla RAG under counterfactual mutation). Typed single-record binding ensures relational misbinding is structurally rejected by the certification predicate whenever the contract fields are correctly extracted and the authority record is valid (E02/E28: 100.0\% rejection across evaluated mutation spaces). Because certification is deterministic, it buys four properties beyond safety: \emph{predictable critical fields} (the verifier checks every contract slot, and the empirical slot ablation shows each contributes measurably, with Crop validation carrying the largest individual contribution of $+$8.94~pp, Layer~E28), \emph{traceability} (every certified answer carries a record hash and provenance chain), \emph{reproducibility} (deterministic resolution is invariant by construction), and \emph{controllable failure} (the E10 taxonomy shows residual failures convert to abstention, not to dangerous advice).

\subsection{The Epistemic Trade-Off of Single-Record Authority}
A natural question is whether enforcing single-record joint binding ($\exists e^*$) risks rejecting valid advice whose components originate from separate institutions---such as BARI providing agronomic efficacy and DAE providing statutory gazette limits. In BAA, this tension is resolved by shifting multi-source evidence synthesis from \emph{runtime generative inference} to \emph{offline knowledge compilation}. During ingestion, multi-institutional guidelines are systematically reconciled, vetted for compatibility, and compiled into unified accredited fact records $e^*$ with immutable cryptographic hashes. At runtime, the verifier requires atomic satisfaction against these pre-compiled composite records. This architectural choice trades runtime generative flexibility for deterministic safety, eliminating the failure mode where an LLM dynamically hallucinates incompatible combinations across retrieved passages.

\subsection{The Cost of Bounded Authority}
The price is real and quantified. BAA's fail-closed design converts uncertain or ungrounded queries to abstention or clarification rather than speculative generation. The E10 audit attributes 6\% of residual failures to conservative verifier over-refusal and 4\% to calibration underconfidence; these are recoverable via frozen-threshold tuning but represent a standing coverage cost. Knowledge engineering is a second cost: the fact store must be maintained and versioned, though E24 shows the marginal cost is small and measurable (18~minutes to close a cluster gap).

\subsection{The Role of LLMs in High-Consequence Domains}
This paper is not an anti-LLM argument. The LLM remains the system's conversational face: it handles phrasing, explanation, and disambiguation, and it is responsible for the 38.48\% of traffic that the deterministic ladder cannot resolve. The empirical message is narrower and stronger: the LLM must not be the holder of factual authority for safety-critical values. The E29 parametric-conflict results show that even when a model holds the correct prior, standard RAG still leaks 8--30\% of conflicting priors into output, and a prompted judge does not eliminate the leak. Structure does; prompting alone does not.

\subsection{Statistical Uncertainty in Small-Sample Layers}
For small-sample evaluation layers (e.g., $n=100$), zero observed failures correspond to a 95\% Wilson confidence interval upper bound of 3.7\%. This statistical reality underscores that empirical observation of zero safety violations in a 100-case test does not constitute a mathematical proof of zero risk in unconstrained deployment. The architecture's safety guarantees derive primarily from its structural design: pre-retrieval screening of banned chemicals, single-record relational verification, and deterministic escalation to human experts when evidence is absent or ambiguous.

\subsection{Where the Architecture Still Fails}
The E10 audit is the honest failure ledger. Retrieval omission (28\%) means a correct record can be missed and the query abstained on; the mitigation (dense retriever refinement, chunk-level fallback) is a coverage fix, not a safety fix. Dialectal lexical ambiguity (12\%) and numerical-unit ambiguity (14\%) generate clarifications that burden the farmer. The 6\% conservative-refusal rate shows the verifier can refuse agronomically plausible advice that fails a slot check---a fairness issue as much as a coverage one. Generalization beyond crop protection to veterinary dosing, human-health triage, or industrial safety represents a design-generalization argument, not an empirical one; none of those domains have been evaluated here.
