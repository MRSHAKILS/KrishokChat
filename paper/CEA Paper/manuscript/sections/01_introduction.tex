\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

\subsection{Agricultural Advisory as a Safety-Critical Domain}
Agricultural crop protection is an inherently safety-critical domain. A pesticide recommendation is not a passage of prose but a tightly coupled tuple of decision variables: the host crop, the target pathogen, the registered active ingredient, its formulation code, a bounded dosage interval with explicit units, the solvent volume, the spray interval, and the mandatory pre-harvest interval (PHI). Consider late blight management in potato: the correct advisory specifies mancozeb 80~WP at 2.0--2.5~g per litre of water, applied at seven-day intervals with a 14-day PHI. Altering any single field---substituting the crop, inflating the dose, or omitting the PHI---produces a superficially valid recommendation that can cause phytotoxicity, dangerous chemical residues on harvested produce, environmental contamination, or acute poisoning of the applicator. The consequence of an error is not a degraded user experience; it is a measurable harm to a farmer, a field, or a food supply chain.

Large Language Models (LLMs) generate fluent, plausible-sounding text across all of these fields, and Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) pipelines can anchor that fluency to retrieved documents \citep{ragchecker2024, farmerchat2024}. Yet neither fluency nor the presence of relevant text in a retrieval window determines the correctness of a safety-critical agronomic value. In unconstrained generative systems and in standard RAG deployments, factual authority rests implicitly with the model's parametric memory and with whatever passages happen to be retrieved. When retrieved documents individually contain valid facts drawn from different records, the generator assembles a semantically coherent composite---the correct crop paired with a dosage taken from an unrelated pathogen's entry, or the correct active ingredient coupled with a PHI lifted from a different formulation.

\subsection{Retrieval Failure Modes in Agricultural RAG}
Retrieval quality in agricultural domains varies sharply with query type and language condition. Text-first retrieval Hit@1 on standard formal Bengali reaches approximately 72--73\%, but falls to 44.7--45.7\% on regional dialects and 41.5--41.6\% on romanized Banglish input (Layer E17, threshold sweep at $\theta{=}0.7$), leaving the generator to operate on degraded or absent evidence. These failures are the dominant failure mode, not rare edge cases. In a 100-case root-cause audit conducted as part of this work (Layer E10), retrieval evidence omission accounted for 28\% of advisory failures and generative relational misbinding for 22\%; both failure modes produced outputs that remained fluent and confident, which is precisely what makes them dangerous.

\subsection{The Missing Authority Boundary}
Existing agricultural advisory systems can be categorized into two paradigms. Generative conversational systems such as Farmer.Chat~\citep{farmerchat2024} and KrishokBondhu~\citep{krishokbondhu2025} treat the LLM as the end-to-end reasoning engine, prioritising multilingual fluency and conversational usability for smallholder farmers. Structured knowledge systems---decision tables, knowledge graphs, and crop simulation wrappers~\citep{goatrag2026,dssatlm2026}---hold curated facts but cannot converse with farmers in natural language. A growing body of work addresses individual reliability properties: temporal validity~\citep{tarag2026}, multimodal disease diagnosis~\citep{smart2026}, post-generation faithfulness assessment~\citep{generateverify2025}, and adversarial robustness of retrieval pipelines~\citep{saferag2025}. What remains absent is an evaluated architecture that makes the authority relationship between deterministic agricultural facts and generative language explicit, and that quantifies the safety--coverage--latency frontier of that relationship under adversarial, linguistic, and operational stress.

\subsection{Proposed Solution: Bounded-Authority Advisory Architecture}
We propose the \textbf{Bounded-Authority Advisory Architecture (BAA)}, a decision-support framework for safety-critical crop protection in which factual authority is structurally separated from linguistic realization. The generative model functions as a phrasing and realization engine without authority to alter, synthesize, or recombine safety-critical agronomic parameters. Every advisory is certified against an 11-slot, single-record, fail-closed contract: all contract fields must be jointly satisfied by one authoritative evidence record, and any failure triggers deterministic routing to clarification, safe abstention, or escalation to the national Krishi Call Center (dial 16123).

\begin{center}
\setlength{\fboxsep}{6pt}
\fbox{\parbox{0.84\columnwidth}{%
\textbf{Design Rule.} \emph{In safety-critical agricultural advisory, the component that generates fluent language must not be the component that holds factual authority.}
}}
\end{center}

\noindent This rule has a concrete operational consequence: a failed safety-critical evidence condition is never repaired by generating additional ungrounded text. If the 11-slot contract cannot be jointly satisfied from a single accredited record, the system does not attempt a speculative completion; it deterministically clarifies, abstains, or escalates. Throughout this paper, \textbf{BAA} refers to the architecture; \textbf{KrishokChat} refers to the deployed system artifact.

The contributions of this work are threefold:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Bounded-authority advisory architecture.} A decision-support architecture that separates factual authority from generative language through an 11-slot, fail-closed, single-record certification contract (Sections~\ref{sec:problem_formulation} and~\ref{sec:system_architecture}).
    \item \textbf{Risk- and evidence-aware selective resolution policy.} A five-tier resolution ladder that routes each query among deterministic resolution, grounded generation, clarification, abstention, and escalation, with a frozen threshold validated on held-out data (Sections~\ref{sec:problem_formulation} and~\ref{sec:results_selective_reliability}).
    \item \textbf{Multi-axis empirical evaluation.} An extensive evaluation covering adversarial safety, empirical advisory correctness, risk--coverage calibration, linguistic and temporal robustness, prompt injection security, and constrained-deployment economics, spanning 36 experimental layers and over 140{,}000 evaluated cases and systematic perturbations (Sections~\ref{sec:experimental_methodology}--\ref{sec:results_deployment}).
\end{enumerate}
