\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

\subsection{Agricultural Advisory as a Safety-Critical Computing Problem}
A wrong agricultural recommendation can remain fluent, grounded-looking, and dangerous. In crop protection, a recommendation is not a paragraph of prose; it is a tuple of decision-critical values---which crop, which pest or pathogen, which registered active ingredient, which formulation, which dosage interval, which unit and solvent volume, which spray interval, which pre-harvest interval (PHI), and whether the chemical is even permitted under current national regulation. An error in any one of these fields can cause phytotoxicity, crop loss, chemical residues on food, environmental contamination, or poisoning of the applicator.

Large Language Models (LLMs) are fluent in all of these fields. Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems \citep{ragchecker2024, farmerchat2024} can make them superficially grounded as well. Yet neither fluency nor retrieved text determines the correctness of a safety-critical agricultural value. The decisive question for a decision-support system is not \emph{can the model produce a plausible sentence?} but \emph{who holds factual authority over the recommendation?} In unconstrained and RAG-based systems, that authority implicitly rests with the generative model's parameters and with whatever text happens to be retrieved, in whatever order those snippets are assembled.

\subsection{Why Retrieval Alone Is Insufficient}
Retrieval quality in agricultural domains varies sharply with query type and language condition. Prior work in our research program established this empirically: text-first retrieval Hit@1 on standard formal Bengali is roughly 72--73\%, but drops to roughly 45--48\% on regional dialects and 41--42\% on romanized Banglish input (Layer E17, threshold sweep at $\theta{=}0.7$). The failure is not solved by better embeddings alone. When the retrieved pool is corrupted---mixed-source documents that individually contain valid facts---a generator will often assemble a semantically plausible composite: the correct crop paired with a dose taken from a different pathogen's record, or the correct active ingredient paired with an uncalibrated PHI from an unrelated row.

These failures are not rare edge cases in our evaluations; they are the dominant failure mode. In a 100-case root-cause audit of residual system failures (E10), retrieval evidence omission accounted for 28\% of cases and generative relational misbinding---the generator pairing a host crop with an active ingredient from an adjacent retrieved row---for 22\%. Both families are silent: the output remains fluent and confident, which is precisely why they are dangerous.

\subsection{The Missing Architectural Question}
Existing agricultural advisory systems fall into two camps. Generative RAG systems (Farmer.Chat~\citep{farmerchat2024}, KrishokBondhu~\citep{krishokbondhu2025}) treat the LLM as the end-to-end reasoning engine. Structured agricultural knowledge systems (decision tables, knowledge graphs, crop simulation wrappers) hold the facts but cannot converse with farmers in natural language~\citep{goatrag2026,dssatlm2026}. A small but growing body of work addresses individual reliability properties: temporal validity (TARAG~\citep{tarag2026}), human-in-the-loop multimodal diagnosis (SMART~\citep{smart2026}), post-generation faithfulness assessment (Generate-but-Verify~\citep{generateverify2025}), and RAG security~\citep{saferag2025}. What is missing is an architecture that makes the authority relationship between deterministic agricultural facts and generative language explicit, and that evaluates the safety--coverage--latency frontier of that relationship.

\subsection{Proposed Solution: Bounded-Authority Selective Resolution}
We propose the \textbf{Bounded-Authority Advisory Architecture (BAA)}. Its central principle is stated as an explicit design rule: \emph{in safety-critical agricultural advisory, the component that generates fluent language must not be the component that holds factual authority.} BAA assigns factual authority to structured, versioned, provenance-carrying evidence records. The LLM is retained as a \emph{linguistic realization} component: it may phrase, explain, disambiguate, and converse, but it may not invent or mutate the values of the safety-critical contract. Each advisory is certified by an 11-slot single-record binding predicate, and when certification cannot be established, the system selectively routes to deterministic resolution, grounded generation with verification, conversational clarification, safe abstention, or escalation to the national Krishi Call Center (16123).

\subsection{Research Questions}
We evaluate BAA along five research questions:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item[\textbf{RQ1}] \textbf{End-to-end advisory quality.} Does BAA certify correct, safe agricultural advice on naturalistic and adversarial farmer queries, as judged by expert agronomists?
    \item[\textbf{RQ2}] \textbf{Evidence authority and safety verification.} Does single-record joint binding protect against relational misbinding, counterfactual evidence corruption, and parametric-evidence conflict, where unconstrained LLM/RAG/judge pipelines fail?
    \item[\textbf{RQ3}] \textbf{Selective reliability and uncertainty.} Can calibrated abstention trade coverage for a user-specified risk bound, and what are the residual failure modes?
    \item[\textbf{RQ4}] \textbf{Linguistic, temporal, and multimodal robustness.} Does the architecture preserve safety when the input language register, the evidence timeliness, or the evidence modality becomes uncertain?
    \item[\textbf{RQ5}] \textbf{Constrained deployment and efficiency.} What are the latency, cost, connectivity-resilience, and channel-fidelity properties of the deterministic and local pathways?
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Bounded-authority advisory architecture.} A decision-support architecture that separates factual authority from generative language realization through an 11-slot, fail-closed, single-record certification contract (Section~\ref{sec:system_architecture}).
    \item \textbf{Risk-/evidence-aware selective resolution.} A five-tier resolution ladder that routes each query among deterministic resolution, grounded generation, clarification, abstention, and escalation, formalized as a decision policy over evidence and risk state (Section~\ref{sec:problem_formulation}).
    \item \textbf{Multi-axis empirical evaluation.} An evidence chain covering end-to-end agronomist-validated correctness, adversarial safety and authority testing, risk--coverage calibration, linguistic/temporal/multimodal robustness, injection security, and constrained-deployment economics.
\end{enumerate}
