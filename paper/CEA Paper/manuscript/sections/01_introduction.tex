\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

\subsection{Agricultural Advisory as a Safety-Critical Computing Problem}
A wrong agricultural recommendation can remain fluent, grounded-looking, and dangerous. In crop protection, a recommendation is not a paragraph of prose; it is a tuple of decision-critical values---which crop, which pest or pathogen, which registered active ingredient, which formulation, which dosage interval, which unit and solvent volume, which spray interval, and which pre-harvest interval (PHI). Consider a recommendation for potato late blight: the correct answer specifies mancozeb 80~WP at 2.0--2.5~g/L water, sprayed every 7~days, with a 14-day PHI. Changing any single field---the crop, the dose, the interval---creates a different recommendation with different consequences. An error in any of these fields can cause phytotoxicity, crop loss, chemical residues on food, environmental contamination, or poisoning of the applicator.

Large Language Models (LLMs) are fluent in all of these fields. Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems \citep{ragchecker2024, farmerchat2024} can make them superficially grounded as well. Yet neither fluency nor retrieved text determines the correctness of a safety-critical agricultural value. The decisive question is not \emph{can the model produce a plausible sentence?} but \emph{who holds factual authority over the recommendation?} In unconstrained and RAG-based systems, that authority implicitly rests with the generative model's parameters and with whatever text happens to be retrieved.

\subsection{Why Retrieval Alone Is Insufficient}
Retrieval quality in agricultural domains varies sharply with query type and language condition. Text-first retrieval Hit@1 on standard formal Bengali is roughly 72--73\%, but drops to roughly 45--48\% on regional dialects and 41--42\% on romanized Banglish input (Layer E17, threshold sweep at $\theta{=}0.7$). The failure is not solved by better embeddings alone: when retrieved documents individually contain valid facts from different records, a generator will assemble a semantically plausible composite---the correct crop paired with a dose taken from a different pathogen's record, or the correct active ingredient paired with an uncalibrated PHI from an unrelated row.

These failures are the dominant failure mode, not rare edge cases. In a 100-case root-cause audit (E10), retrieval evidence omission accounted for 28\% of cases and generative relational misbinding---the generator pairing a host crop with a chemical from an adjacent row---for 22\%. Both are silent: the output remains fluent and confident, which is precisely why they are dangerous.

\subsection{The Missing Architectural Question}
Existing agricultural advisory systems fall into two camps. Generative RAG systems (Farmer.Chat~\citep{farmerchat2024}, KrishokBondhu~\citep{krishokbondhu2025}) treat the LLM as the end-to-end reasoning engine. Structured knowledge systems (decision tables, knowledge graphs, crop simulation wrappers) hold facts but cannot converse with farmers in natural language~\citep{goatrag2026,dssatlm2026}. A growing body of work addresses individual reliability properties---temporal validity~\citep{tarag2026}, multimodal diagnosis~\citep{smart2026}, post-generation faithfulness~\citep{generateverify2025}, and RAG security~\citep{saferag2025}---but no evaluated system makes the authority relationship between deterministic agricultural facts and generative language explicit and evaluates the safety--coverage--latency frontier of that relationship.

\subsection{Proposed Solution: Bounded-Authority Selective Resolution}
We propose the \textbf{Bounded-Authority Advisory Architecture (BAA)}, a decision-support framework for safety-critical crop protection. Its central principle is an explicit design rule:

\begin{center}
\setlength{\fboxsep}{6pt}
\fbox{\parbox{0.82\columnwidth}{%
\textbf{Design Rule.} In safety-critical agricultural advisory, the component that generates fluent language must not be the component that holds factual authority.
}}
\end{center}

\noindent BAA assigns factual authority to structured, versioned, provenance-carrying evidence records. The LLM is retained as a \emph{linguistic realization} component: it may phrase, explain, and disambiguate, but it may not invent or mutate the values of the safety-critical contract. Throughout this paper, \textbf{BAA} refers to the architecture; \textbf{KrishokChat} refers to the deployed system artifact. Each advisory is certified by an 11-slot single-record binding predicate, and when certification cannot be established, the system selectively routes to deterministic resolution, grounded generation with verification, conversational clarification, safe abstention, or escalation to the national Krishi Call Center (16123).

\subsection{Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Bounded-authority advisory architecture.} A decision-support architecture that separates factual authority from generative language through an 11-slot, fail-closed, single-record certification contract (Section~\ref{sec:system_architecture}).
    \item \textbf{Risk-/evidence-aware selective resolution.} A five-tier resolution ladder that routes each query among deterministic resolution, grounded generation, clarification, abstention, and escalation, formalized as a decision policy over evidence and risk state (Section~\ref{sec:problem_formulation}).
    \item \textbf{Multi-axis empirical evaluation.} An evidence chain covering adversarial safety, agronomist-validated correctness, risk--coverage calibration, linguistic and temporal robustness, injection security, and constrained-deployment economics, across 36 experimental layers and~$\approx$60,000 evaluation cases (Section~\ref{sec:experimental_methodology} and Table~\ref{tab:rq_mapping}).
\end{enumerate}
