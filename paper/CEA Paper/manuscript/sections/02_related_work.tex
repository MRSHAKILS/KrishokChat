\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}

\subsection{Agricultural Conversational AI and RAG Advisory}
The deployment of LLM-based agricultural extension has grown rapidly. Farmer.Chat~\citep{farmerchat2024} demonstrated scalable multilingual conversational advisory for smallholder farmers, engaging over 15{,}000 farmers across four countries. KrishokBondhu~\citep{krishokbondhu2025} showed a Bengali voice-enabled retrieval pipeline. These systems establish that conversational interfaces are usable in low-resource agricultural settings, but they treat the LLM as the end-to-end reasoning engine. Under dialectal variation and multi-document retrieval, they remain exposed to the silent factual corruption documented in Section~\ref{sec:introduction}. Our positioning is complementary: we do not claim a new conversation interface; we evaluate whether an explicit authority layer can make such interfaces safe for safety-critical crop-protection advice.

\subsection{Structured Agricultural Knowledge and Domain-First RAG}
A parallel line of work embeds structured agricultural knowledge into decision support. The goat-farming knowledge assistant of Han et al.~\citep{goatrag2026} textualizes decision trees and tables to improve answer consistency over heterogeneous livestock knowledge. TARAG~\citep{tarag2026} contributes a time-aware knowledge base and time-sensitive re-ranking so that crop-pest recommendations respect phenology and pest life stages. SMART~\citep{smart2026} integrates structured multimodal embeddings with human-in-the-loop verification for plant disease diagnosis. DSSAT-LM~\citep{dssatlm2026} couples LLMs with mechanistic crop simulation by parsing free text into structured simulation parameters. These works establish that structured representations and time awareness improve agricultural advisory quality. BAA incorporates the same mechanisms---structured typed facts, temporal validity tags, and multimodal conditioning---but makes them \emph{enforced constraints} of a certification contract rather than retrieval features.

\subsection{RAG Faithfulness, Verification, and Security}
Faithfulness of generated answers to retrieved evidence has become a first-class evaluation target. RAGChecker~\citep{ragchecker2024} argues that retrieval and generation must be diagnosed separately. Generate-but-Verify~\citep{generateverify2025} formalizes post-generation faithfulness prediction as an explicit task with precision/recall variants and shows that better faithfulness assessment improves downstream RAG performance. FaithfulRAG~\citep{faithfulrag2025} models fact-level conflict between retrieved knowledge and parametric memory, and claim-level provenance tracing is demonstrated in scientific verification by SciTrue~\citep{scitrue2026}. On the security side, SafeRAG~\citep{saferag2025} demonstrates that manipulated or poisoned retrieved knowledge can defeat otherwise strong RAG pipelines. These works motivate our architecture: they show that post-hoc textual verification and retrieval-side fixes are necessary but insufficient for safety-critical fields, because they verify \emph{language} rather than \emph{relational authority}. BAA differs by certifying an 11-slot tuple against a single evidence record, so a faithful-sounding sentence whose fields come from different records fails certification by construction.

\subsection{Selective Answering, Abstention, and Risk--Coverage}
Selective prediction and abstention are established tools in classification. Risk--coverage curves and calibrated abstention allow a system to trade coverage for a bounded error rate. In conversational systems, abstention is increasingly treated as a first-class output rather than a failure~\citep{generateverify2025}. BAA applies these tools to agricultural advisory: abstention is certified \emph{appropriate} when the 11-slot contract cannot be jointly satisfied, and the abstention threshold is frozen on a development split and validated on a held-out test split (Section~\ref{sec:results_selective_reliability}).

\subsection{Gap and Positioning}
Table~\ref{tab:literature_positioning} summarizes the positioning. Existing systems exhibit strong individual properties---multilingual fluency, structured facts, temporal awareness, multimodality, offline operation---but no evaluated system binds a safety-critical advisory contract to a single authoritative record and then selectively resolves across deterministic, generative, and abstention paths. The novelty we claim is not any individual component; it is the \emph{intersection}: a reliability-oriented agricultural computing architecture in which factual authority is explicitly bounded, uncertainty can terminate safely, and generative language is subordinate to validated evidence.
