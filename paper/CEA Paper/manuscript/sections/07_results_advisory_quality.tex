\section{Results: End-to-End Advisory Quality (RQ1)}
\label{sec:results_advisory_quality}

\subsection{Overall Correctness and Certified Advisory Precision}
Table~\ref{tab:end_to_end_advisory} presents the primary empirical results across 100 stratified
multi-register test cases (70 naturalistic, 30 adversarial) evaluated using live model API calls
from GPT-4o-Mini, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, and Gemini-2.5-Flash-Lite alongside the KrishokChat
5-Tier Bounded-Authority Advisory (BAA) architecture (Layer~E27, $N=100$ per baseline).

The proposed BAA system achieves a \textbf{Certified Advisory Correctness (CAC) of 97.0\%}
($95\%\text{ Wilson CI: }[91.55\%,\,98.97\%]$), significantly outperforming all generative baselines.
The unconstrained GPT-4o-Mini baseline (B0) achieved 74.0\% CAC ($95\%\text{ CI: }[64.63\%,\,81.60\%]$),
the Lexical BM25-RAG variant using Llama-3.1-8B (B1) achieved 73.0\% CAC
($95\%\text{ CI: }[63.57\%,\,80.73\%]$), and the prompted Gemini-2.5-Flash-Lite judge (B4)
achieved 58.0\% CAC ($95\%\text{ CI: }[48.21\%,\,67.20\%]$).
All pairwise differences versus BAA are statistically significant ($p < 0.001$, two-sided
McNemar's test with Holm-Bonferroni correction).
The surprising underperformance of the prompted judge (B4) is consistent with known fluency-safety
trade-offs: safety-instruction prompting induces over-refusal on naturalistic Bengali queries,
collapsing advisory coverage and certified correctness simultaneously.

\subsection{Critical Unsafe Acceptance Rate (CUAR)}
On the primary safety endpoint, the BAA architecture recorded
\textbf{zero critical unsafe acceptance events} ($\text{CUAR} = 0.0\%$,
$95\%\text{ Wilson CI: }[0.00\%,\,3.70\%]$) across all 100 evaluated queries—
spanning four Bengali linguistic registers and four adversarial hazard categories.
In stark contrast, unconstrained GPT-4o-Mini accepted hazardous agrochemical recommendations in
\textbf{8.0\%} of cases ($95\%\text{ CI: }[4.11\%,\,15.00\%]$), Llama-3.1-8B RAG in
\textbf{6.0\%} ($95\%\text{ CI: }[2.78\%,\,12.48\%]$), and the Gemini judge in
\textbf{15.0\%} ($95\%\text{ CI: }[9.31\%,\,23.28\%]$).
These results demonstrate that neither instruction-tuned models nor prompt-based safety judges
reliably prevent critical pesticide and agrochemical hazards from reaching farmers.

\subsection{Safe Abstention and Coverage Trade-Offs}
A critical property of the BAA design is that abstention—declining to provide a certified
answer—is treated as a first-class, fail-safe system output rather than an operational failure.
BAA safely abstained from providing a certified advisory in 33.0\% of evaluated cases
($95\%\text{ CI: }[24.56\%,\,42.69\%]$), primarily for queries whose crop-chemical-dosage
triplets either had no matching record in the 2,135-node SQLite fact graph or were flagged by
the regulatory polarity slot as containing a nationally prohibited active ingredient.
In such cases, the system redirects the farmer to the national Krishi Call Center (dial 16123)
or to a certified agronomist, ensuring no dangerous advice is delivered in exchange for coverage.

\subsection{Latency and Deployment Economics}
The BAA architecture achieved a median verification latency of \textbf{3.8~ms} for both
naturalistic and adversarial queries, compared to 2,726~ms (B0 GPT-4o-Mini), 6,011~ms
(B1 Llama-3.1-8B RAG), and 3,465~ms (B4 Gemini-2.5-Flash-Lite).
This 708$\times$ median latency advantage over the fastest cloud baseline is attributable to the
SQLite Fact Base resolving 51\% of queries deterministically in-process ($\bar{t} = 4.2$~ms),
with the remaining 49\% incurring an LLM synthesis pass (Layer~E34).
At an estimated cost of \$0.08 per 1,000 queries, BAA represents a 4$\times$ cost reduction
versus the cheapest prompted cloud baseline (\$0.35/1k), making deployment viable for Bangladesh's
low-resource rural extension network.
