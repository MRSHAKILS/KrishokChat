BAA achieves \textbf{97.0\% Certified Advisory Correctness (CAC)} (95\% Wilson CI [91.55, 98.97]) versus 74.0\% (B0), 73.0\% (B1), and 58.0\% (B4). On the primary safety endpoint, BAA records \textbf{0.0\% critical unsafe acceptance} (CUAR, CI [0.00, 3.70]) across all 100 cases, whereas B0 accepted unsafe recommendations in 8.0\% of cases, B1 in 6.0\%, and the LLM-judge guardrail (B4) in \textbf{15.0\%} --- \emph{worse than the unguarded baseline}. We report these values as observed at $n=100$ rather than as a general statement about judge guardrails.

Two framing caveats are explicit. First, the 33.0\% abstention of BAA (CI [24.56, 42.69]) is more than double any baseline (B1 abstains only 5.0\%); the recorded 97.0\% CAC is therefore computed over the answered/certified set, and the comparison with baselines is not a like-for-like raw accuracy comparison --- it is a certified-precision comparison with a coverage penalty. Second, the BAA latency entry (3.8 ms) is a modeled in-process figure, not a measured wall-clock percentile; we report the measured decomposition in Section~\ref{sec:results_efficiency}.

\subsection{Agronomist Validation (E13)}
In the expert study, three certified agronomists rated 200 outputs. BAA scored a mean correctness of \textbf{4.82/5} (SD 0.28), a \textbf{100.0\% chemical-safety pass rate} (95\% CI [98.12, 100.0]), 98.5\% evidence traceability, and 96.5\% deployment approval. RAG+LLM judge (B5) scored 4.10/5 and 86.0\% safety pass; vanilla RAG (B2) 3.65/5 and 71.5\%; unconstrained LLM (B1) 3.12/5 and 64.5\%. LLM-direct and vanilla RAG were approved for deployment in only 38.0\% and 52.5\% of cases, due to undetected dosage discrepancies and unsubstantiated treatment claims. The raters' agreement on safety was Gwet's AC1 = 0.862 (0.814 on deployment approval; 0.785 on correctness).

\subsection{Multi-Register Correctness (E06)}
Across 4{,}000 multi-register queries (1{,}000 per register), the BAA system certified 65.8\% of cases correctly and abstained safely in 34.2\%, with 0.0\% dangerous acceptance (95\% CI [0.0, 0.1]). Per register: standard formal 76.2\% correct / 23.8\% abstained; authentic farmer 68.3\% / 31.7\%; regional dialects 59.5\% / 40.5\%; romanized Banglish 59.2\% / 40.8\%. The strongest baselines in the same suite, vanilla RAG, reached only 56.07\% overall correct with 13.45\% dangerous acceptance (CI [12.43, 14.54]) and direct LLM 58.73\% with 1.85\%. The register invariance of the BAA safety boundary (0.0\% dangerous in all four registers) is the central robustness result of Section~\ref{sec:results_robustness}.

\subsection{Per-Contract-Slot Correctness}
The 11-slot contract of Section~\ref{sec:problem_formulation} is the unit of correctness. In the E03 hazard-ablation suite, removing a single slot from the certification contract raises dangerous acceptance from 0.0\% to between 1.9 pp (interval) and 31.6 pp (dosage bounds); removing all typed constraints collapses the system to the 80.0\% hazard rate of a lexical substring matcher (Section~\ref{sec:results_authority_safety}). The per-slot contribution is the mechanism behind the end-to-end CAC and is reported in Section~\ref{sec:results_authority_safety}.
