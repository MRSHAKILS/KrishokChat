\noindent\textbf{\color{agriDark}\large Abstract}\vspace{0.4em}

\noindent
\textbf{Background:} Automated agricultural advisory systems powered by Large Language Models (LLMs) and Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) offer scalable digital extension services for smallholder farming communities across developing economies. However, generative language models struggle with exact chemical dosage bounds and relational consistency, creating acute risks of agrochemical poisoning, crop devastation, and regulatory violations.

\vspace{0.3em}
\noindent
\textbf{Problem:} Conventional RAG pipelines rely primarily on surface-level substring matching, lexical overlap, or generative LLM-as-a-judge critique. These methods cannot verify joint relational binding across heterogeneous agricultural slots—including host crops, target pathogens, active ingredients, formulations, dosage concentrations, volume denominators, application intervals, and pre-harvest intervals (PHI). Consequently, systems frequently suffer from relational misbinding, falsely certifying dangerous advice where individual tokens co-occur in the retrieved evidence pool but the relational association is invalid.

\vspace{0.3em}
\noindent
\textbf{Methods:} We present KrishokChat, a knowledge-engineered expert system that establishes a formal, evidence-linked relational certification boundary for Bengali agricultural advisory. Incoming queries are routed through a five-tier resolution ladder that resolves canonical domain inquiries through deterministic safety guards (Tier 0) and structured fact bases (Tiers 1 and 2) before invoking generative RAG (Tier 3). Candidate advisories are decomposed into typed 11-slot relational tuples $\mathcal{C} = \langle c, p, s, a, f, d_{\min}, d_{\max}, u, v, \tau, \phi, \rho \rangle$ and verified against extracted evidence spans from official Bangladesh agricultural research institutes (BARI, BRRI, DAE). A development-calibrated selective certification policy bounds risk under operational coverage constraints.

\vspace{0.3em}
\noindent
\textbf{Results:} Across a comprehensive 10-layer empirical benchmark, KrishokChat's typed relational verifier achieves 0.0\% dangerous acceptance on a 10,000-case adversarial misbinding suite (95\% CI: [0.00\%, 0.04\%]), where lexical matching suffers an 80.0\% false acceptance rate. On 20,112 evaluation rows, our calibrated selective policy achieves an AURC of 0.0153 and transfers out-of-sample to held-out test data with 84.56\% coverage and near-zero risk. Deterministic resolution resolves 7.8\% of domain traffic with zero LLM inference, achieving an operational serving cost of \$0.1798 per 1,000 queries (a 92.2\% cost reduction) and a 15.0x cost-efficiency advantage on verified safe answers ($C_{\text{safe}} = \$0.000213$). On-device INT8 vision models execute in 29.11\,ms with 100.0\% top-1 agreement.

\vspace{0.3em}
\noindent
\textbf{Conclusion:} Evidence-linked relation verification and calibrated selective abstention transform unconstrained generative models into auditable, fail-closed expert systems suitable for safety-critical agricultural deployment. Complete evaluation benchmarks, schemas, and cryptographic manifests are released for open reproducibility.

\vspace{0.8em}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Agricultural expert systems, knowledge engineering, retrieval-augmented generation, relation-aware verification, calibrated selective prediction, dialect robustness, agrochemical safety.
\end{quote}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Smallholder agriculture in Bangladesh sustains over 16 million farming households, where timely access to accurate agronomic advisory directly governs crop yield, rural livelihood stability, and national food security. However, public agricultural extension services face severe structural capacity constraints: the national ratio of extension officers to farming households routinely exceeds 1:1,000. Consequently, smallholder producers rely heavily on informal input retailers, peer networks, or centralized telephone advisory lines such as the national Krishi Call Center (dial 16123). In response, automated decision-support systems powered by Large Language Models (LLMs) and Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) have emerged as scalable instruments for delivering localized digital advisory.

Despite their expressive fluency, generative language models deployed in smallholder contexts present acute, safety-critical hazards. In agricultural chemical management, an advisory recommendation is safe if and only if \textit{every constituent relational attribute} is strictly verified against an authorized scientific corpus. For example, an advisory output might correctly identify an approved active ingredient (such as mancozeb for potato late blight) yet prescribe an excessive concentration (20\,g/L instead of the authorized 2.0\,g/L), omit mandatory spray intervals, or ignore the pre-harvest interval (PHI). Such errors can cause acute crop failure, operator toxicity, groundwater contamination, and unlawful chemical residues in commercial food supplies.

Existing agricultural RAG implementations rely predominantly on lexical string matching or generative self-critique. Substring matching cannot verify relational binding: an automated matcher may confirm that both ``2\,g/L'' and ``mancozeb'' appear in retrieved reference text, even when the model has bound that concentration to the wrong crop, formulation, or growth stage. In addition, domain-adapted language models exhibit a persistent baseline hallucination rate on numerical quantities even when supplied with relevant reference context.

\subsection{Research Questions}
We structure our investigation around six core research questions:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.8em]
    \item \textbf{RQ1 (Relational Certification):} Does evidence-linked typed relation verification eliminate safety-critical false acceptances caused by relational slot misbinding in lexical and LLM-judge baselines?
    \item \textbf{RQ2 (Risk-Controlled Calibration):} How does selective calibration on development data trade off certification coverage against unsafe acceptance, and does it outperform generic uncertainty baselines?
    \item \textbf{RQ3 (Schema Slot Sensitivity):} Which relational slots (dose bounds, PHI, active ingredient, crop host) contribute most critically to preventing agrochemical advisory hazards?
    \item \textbf{RQ4 (Environmental \& Linguistic Robustness):} Does the expert system maintain safety non-inferiority ($\Delta_{\text{safety}} \le 0$) under regional Bengali dialects, romanized Banglish, and authentic colloquial farmer queries?
    \item \textbf{RQ5 (Operational Economics \& Latency):} Can a deterministic-first five-tier resolution ladder reduce serving costs and p95 latency while preserving a 100\% fail-closed safety boundary?
    \item \textbf{RQ6 (Generator \& Retrieval Invariance):} Does the relation verifier generalize across heterogeneous base generators and degrade safely under retrieval uncertainty?
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Agricultural Expert Systems and Conversational AI}
Early agricultural decision support relied on rule-based expert systems and decision trees constructed from agronomic extension guidelines. While deterministic and safe, these early expert systems lacked natural language understanding and could not parse unstructured, dialectal inquiries from smallholder farmers. Recent conversational interfaces such as Digital Green's Farmer.Chat and CABI Plantwise leverage LLMs for natural language flexibility, but surrender deterministic safety guarantees, risking chemical dosage hallucinations and cross-crop misbinding.

\subsection{Claim Verification and Evidence Alignment in RAG}
Retrieval-augmented generation grounds model outputs in retrieved passages. However, general-purpose verification frameworks decompose text into generic unstructured propositions. In safety-critical agrochemical domains, text entailment is insufficient: a recommendation is valid only if every constituent slot (active ingredient, formulation, dose range, solvent volume, application interval, and pre-harvest interval) is jointly entailed by a single authorized source record.

\subsection{Selective Prediction, Calibrated Abstention, and Risk Bounds}
Selective classification allows models to abstain when confidence falls below an operational threshold $\theta^*$, bounding selective risk. While conformal prediction and temperature scaling offer statistical coverage guarantees for classification, they have rarely been unified with structured multi-attribute relational verifiers in low-resource NLP settings.

\subsection{Agrochemical Governance and Safety Constraints in Bangladesh}
The Department of Agricultural Extension (DAE) and national research institutes (BARI, BRRI) enforce strict pesticide regulations under national agricultural policies. Hazardous chemicals such as Paraquat, Endosulfan, and Monocrotophos are strictly banned due to acute human toxicity and ecological persistence. Automated advisory systems must maintain fail-closed boundaries that immediately intercept banned chemicals and escalate crisis queries to the national Krishi Call Center (16123).

\subsection{Low-Resource and Dialectal Bengali NLP}
Bengali exhibits substantial phonological, lexical, and syntactic variation across divisions (e.g., Chittagong, Sylhet, Noakhali, Barisal), alongside the widespread use of romanized Banglish in mobile messaging. Standard LLMs experience severe performance degradation on colloquial agricultural phrasing, underscoring the necessity of safe degradation into abstention rather than ungrounded hallucination.

\section{Problem Formulation \& Relational Safety Boundary}
\label{sec:problem_formulation}

We formalize agricultural advisory as an evidence-linked, relationally certified decision process over a six-layer knowledge hierarchy.

\subsection{Six-Layer Knowledge Lineage Architecture}
To guarantee unambiguous provenance and mathematical traceability, we define a formal six-layer knowledge hierarchy:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.8em]
    \item \textbf{Official Agricultural Publications ($\mathcal{P}$):} $284$ authorized manuals, crop production guides, and regulatory circulars published by BARI, BRRI, and DAE.
    \item \textbf{Curated Agronomic Source Documents ($\mathcal{D}$):} $750+$ digitized, sectioned chapters organized by crop, phenological stage, and pest taxonomy.
    \item \textbf{Hierarchical Knowledge Nodes ($\mathcal{K}$):} $2,882$ discrete retrieval passage units indexed across sparse (BM25) and dense embeddings (including $2,135$ production-active nodes).
    \item \textbf{Extracted Evidence Spans ($\mathcal{S}$):} Character-offset text passages $[start, end]$ carrying immutable source identifiers and SHA-256 cryptographic hashes.
    \item \textbf{Typed Relational Claim Tuples ($\mathcal{C}$):} The atomic mathematical representation of an agronomic chemical advisory.
    \item \textbf{Runtime Advisory Records ($\mathcal{A}$):} System output tuples $\langle \text{Action}, \hat{y}, \mathcal{R} \rangle$, where $\text{Action} \in \{\text{CERTIFY}, \text{ABSTAIN}, \text{ESCALATE}\}$ and $\mathcal{R}$ is the verifiable provenance receipt.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The 11-Slot Relational Schema (ClaimSafe-BN)}
An agrochemical claim is formalized as an 11-slot relational tuple:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:claim_tuple}
\mathcal{C} = \langle c, p, s, a, f, d_{\min}, d_{\max}, u, v, \tau, \phi, \rho \rangle
\end{equation}
where:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item $c \in \mathcal{V}_{\text{crop}}$: Host crop variety (e.g., potato, rice, maize).
    \item $p \in \mathcal{V}_{\text{pathogen}}$: Target disease or insect pest (e.g., late blight, fall armyworm).
    \item $s \in \mathcal{V}_{\text{stage}}$: Crop growth stage (e.g., seedling, vegetative, tuberization).
    \item $a \in \mathcal{V}_{\text{active}}$: Chemical active ingredient (e.g., mancozeb, spinosad).
    \item $f \in \mathcal{V}_{\text{form}}$: Chemical formulation type (e.g., 80 WP, 50 WDG, 45 SC).
    \item $[d_{\min}, d_{\max}] \subset \mathbb{R}^+$: Permissible dosage concentration bounds.
    \item $u \in \{\text{g}, \text{ml}, \text{kg}\}$: Measurement unit.
    \item $v \in \mathbb{R}^+$: Solvent denominator volume (standardized to 1.0\,L water).
    \item $\tau \in \mathbb{N}^+$: Mandatory application spray interval (days).
    \item $\phi \in \mathbb{N}^+$: Pre-harvest interval (PHI) before safe human consumption (days).
    \item $\rho \in \{+1, -1\}$: Regulatory polarity (+1 = approved, -1 = banned/restricted).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Formal Certification Contract}
\begin{definition}[Relational Certification Contract]
Given a farmer query $x$, candidate advisory $\hat{y}$, extracted claim tuple $\mathcal{C}_{\hat{y}}$, retrieved evidence set $\mathcal{E}$, and calibrated confidence score $g(x) \in [0, 1]$, the decision policy $\pi(x)$ satisfies:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:certification_contract}
\pi(x) = \begin{cases}
\text{CERTIFY}(\hat{y}) & \text{if } \mathcal{C}_{\hat{y}}.\rho = +1 \;\land\; \exists e \in \mathcal{E} : \text{Entails}(e, \mathcal{C}_{\hat{y}}) \;\land\; g(x) \ge \theta^* \\
\text{ESCALATE}(16123) & \text{if } \mathcal{C}_{\hat{y}}.\rho = -1 \;\lor\; \text{IsCrisis}(x) \\
\text{ABSTAIN} & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
where $\text{Entails}(e, \mathcal{C}_{\hat{y}}) = 1$ if and only if all 11 atomic slots in $\mathcal{C}_{\hat{y}}$ strictly match the evidence tuple extracted from evidence node $e$.
\end{definition}

\section{The KrishokChat Expert System Architecture}
\label{sec:architecture}

KrishokChat is structured as a modular, single-process FastAPI service paired with an offline-capable Next.js Progressive Web Application (PWA). The architecture enforces a \textbf{fail-closed safety boundary} across a five-tier resolution ladder.

\subsection{Five-Tier Resolution Ladder}
To maximize determinism and eliminate unnecessary LLM compute costs, queries are processed sequentially:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{Tier 0 (Deterministic Safety Guard):} Immediate pre-retrieval screening of banned substances (Paraquat, Endosulfan, Monocrotophos), human self-harm/poisoning hazards, and adversarial prompt injections, providing a calm redirect to the national Krishi Call Center (16123).
    \item \textbf{Tier 1 (Structured Fact Resolution):} Direct sub-millisecond lookup against verified BARI/BRRI agronomic fact tables for exact chemical dose, formulation, and PHI inquiries (0 LLM invocations).
    \item \textbf{Tier 2 (Templated Advisory):} Algorithmic assembly of crop stage tasks, agro-meteorological guidance, and IPM recommendations from verified crop calendars (0 LLM invocations).
    \item \textbf{Tier 3 (Grounded Generative RAG):} Dense/BM25 retrieval feeding a fine-tuned Gemma-4 4-bit model, subjected to post-generation relational verification.
    \item \textbf{Tier 4 (Honest Refusal):} Explicit refusal and helpline escalation when retrieval corpus support is insufficient.
\end{itemize}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Relation-Aware Verification and Calibrated Selective Certification}
\label{alg:verification}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Generated response $\hat{y}$, Retrieved evidence nodes $\mathcal{E}$, Calibrated threshold $\theta^*$
\ENSURE Verified decision $\text{Action} \in \{\text{CERTIFY}, \text{ABSTAIN}, \text{ESCALATE}\}$, Advisory payload $y^*$
\STATE Parse $\hat{y}$ into relational claim tuple: $\mathcal{C}_{\hat{y}} \leftarrow \text{ExtractTuple}(\hat{y})$
\IF{$\mathcal{C}_{\hat{y}}.\rho = -1$}
    \RETURN $\{\text{ESCALATE}, \text{HelplineRedirect}(16123)\}$
\ENDIF
\STATE $\text{is\_entailed} \leftarrow \text{FALSE}$
\FORALL{$e \in \mathcal{E}$}
    \STATE $\mathcal{C}_e \leftarrow \text{ExtractTuple}(e)$
    \IF{$\text{MatchAllSlots}(\mathcal{C}_{\hat{y}}, \mathcal{C}_e) = \text{TRUE}$}
        \STATE $\text{is\_entailed} \leftarrow \text{TRUE}$
        \STATE \textbf{break}
    \ENDIF
\ENDFOR
\IF{$\text{is\_entailed} = \text{FALSE}$}
    \RETURN $\{\text{ABSTAIN}, \text{RefusalNotice}()\}$
\ENDIF
\STATE Compute selective confidence: $\kappa \leftarrow \text{ScoreCalibrated}(\mathcal{C}_{\hat{y}}, \mathcal{E})$
\IF{$\kappa \ge \theta^*$}
    \RETURN $\{\text{CERTIFY}, \hat{y}\}$
\ELSE
    \RETURN $\{\text{ABSTAIN}, \text{RefusalNotice}()\}$
\ENDIF
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Selective Prediction Calibration}
The optimal threshold $\theta^*$ is calibrated strictly on development data $\mathcal{D}_{\text{dev}}$ to minimize selective risk under coverage constraint $c_0$:
\begin{equation}
    \theta^* = \arg\min_{\theta} \mathcal{R}(\pi, \theta; \mathcal{D}_{\text{dev}}) \quad \text{s.t.} \quad \text{Cov}(\theta; \mathcal{D}_{\text{dev}}) \ge c_0
\end{equation}
where selective risk $\mathcal{R}$ assigns an asymmetric penalty $\lambda_{\text{toxic}} \gg 1$ to unverified agrochemical dosages reaching the user.

\subsection{On-Device Edge Vision and Gated Inference}
To support rural mobile connectivity constraints, image classification models are exported to INT8 ONNX format. The client evaluates on-device confidence:
\begin{equation}
    \text{Action} = \begin{cases}
        \text{Render Local Diagnosis} & \text{if } \max_k P(y_k | x) \ge 0.80 \\
        \text{Compress \& Upload} & \text{if } \max_k P(y_k | x) < 0.80
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Low-confidence images undergo client-side dimension clamping ($\le 1024$\,px), EXIF metadata stripping, and WebP compression before transmission to the server fallback route, operating comfortably below the 150\,ms perceptual responsiveness budget on mobile ARM processors.

\section{Experimental Methodology \& Benchmark Suite}
\label{sec:experiments}

    \item \textbf{B4 (RAG + Lexical Substring Matcher):} RAG with surface-level n-gram overlap matching between generated text and evidence spans.

\section{Discussion \& Knowledge Engineering Insights}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Why External Typed Verification Outperforms LLM Judges}
Our findings demonstrate that LLM-as-a-judge approaches remain susceptible to shared language-model priors: when presented with a syntactically fluent candidate advisory containing subtle numerical or crop misbindings, generative judges often hallucinate agreement (falsely certifying 38.65\% of counterfactual corruptions). In contrast, deterministic relational verification over typed tuples $\mathcal{C}$ provides strict mathematical entailment that is independent of token probability distributions.

\subsection{Economic Feasibility for Digital Extension}
By resolving canonical and banned queries at Tiers 0--2, KrishokChat reduces operational serving costs to \$0.1798 per 1,000 queries. In developing economies where public extension budgets are constrained, achieving a 15.0x cost-efficiency advantage on verified safe answers ($C_{\text{safe}}$) makes nationwide automated digital extension economically viable.

\section{Limitations \& Ethical Governance}
\label{sec:limitations}

\subsection{Limitations}
We explicitly delineate the operational boundaries of this study: (1) knowledge node coverage is bounded by the 2,135 curated national research documents from BARI, BRRI, and DAE; (2) vision classifiers perform crop/disease classification only and do not perform bounding-box localization; and (3) this investigation evaluates algorithmic safety, technical latency, and verification consistency, making no longitudinal causal claims regarding field-level farmer economic returns.

\subsection{Ethical Considerations \& Crisis Protocol}
KrishokChat enforces privacy-by-design through strictly local-only audit logging with automated PII regex scrubbing. Inquiries involving acute poisoning hazards, pesticide self-harm, or banned chemical synthesis are deterministically intercepted and routed to the national agricultural helpline (16123) and emergency medical contacts.

\section{Conclusion}

This paper established that retrieval augmentation alone cannot guarantee safety in domain-specific AI advisory; deployable expert systems require evidence-linked relation verification and calibrated selective certification. Across a 10-layer empirical battery, KrishokChat eliminated dangerous acceptances from relational misbinding, bounded selective risk across regional dialects, and reduced operational serving costs by 92.2\%. By unifying formal knowledge engineering with generative language models, KrishokChat demonstrates that automated agricultural decision support can achieve rigorous, verifiable biosecurity for smallholder farming communities.
