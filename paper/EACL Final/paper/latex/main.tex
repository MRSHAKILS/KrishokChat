\begin{abstract}
We present \textsc{KrishokTech}, a Bengali agricultural advisory system designed for practical use in Bangladesh under limited connectivity, device, and knowledge-resource conditions. Rather than treating a large language model as the primary decision maker, \textsc{KrishokTech} uses a deterministic-first pipeline that can halt underspecified treatment queries before retrieval, constrain retrieval to the identified crop, expose image--text contradictions to the user, and verify safety-critical dosage claims before they are rendered or delivered. The system also supports constrained offline and SMS delivery and provides a terminal path to human agricultural assistance for cases that should not be answered automatically. We demonstrate the system through realistic Bengali farmer interactions covering missing crop information, image-based crop routing, contradictory evidence, grounded responses, unsafe requests, and constrained delivery. Evaluation shows that the pre-retrieval gate halted 30/200 treatment queries, while crop fencing reduced wrong-crop answers from 36.25\% to 30.0\% in a controlled evaluation, with 25 discordant cases favoring the fenced setting. Across 420 live safety calls, guarded responses reduced attack success rate from 36.19\% to 0.95\%. These results illustrate how explicit constraints can make agricultural conversational systems more controllable without relying solely on a larger language model.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

For a farmer in Bangladesh, agricultural advice is urgently needed when symptoms appear, pests spread, or treatment cannot wait for an extension visit. Agriculture accounted for 11.7\% of GDP and 44.3\% of employment in 2025 \citep{worldbank2026}, yet smallholders face a severe extension workforce deficit \citep{dae_extension_manual,dlec_bangladesh}. A digital assistant can extend access, but only if it accurately recognizes when requests are underspecified or unsafe.

The challenge goes beyond providing a Bengali chat interface. Farmers describe symptoms in colloquial or regional Bangla, whereas agricultural resources use standardized terms; our prior work found a substantial formal-to-colloquial retrieval gap \citep{our_bengali_retrieval}. Retrieval can also fail before generation: treatment requests often omit the crop, photographs may contradict text, and generated dosages may lack evidence grounding. Fluent models turn unresolved uncertainties into confident, dangerous recommendations. Prior work revealed residual unsupported chemical advice even under oracle evidence \citep{our_agricultural_safety}, and vision-language models often exhibit blind faith in contradictory text \citep{Deng_2025_CVPR}.

We present \textbf{KrishokTech}, a Bengali agricultural advisory system structured around explicit decision boundaries rather than ungrounded generation:
First, \emph{the cheapest and safest retrieval is the one the system refuses to run} (\textbf{C1}): when a treatment query lacks a crop, a deterministic gate halts before search and serves clarification chips or helpline referral (halting 30/200 farmer queries with zero retrieval).
Second, \emph{the photo is a fence around the evidence} (\textbf{C2}): on-device vision bounds retrieval to the identified crop; text--image conflicts raise an explicit confirmation badge (reducing wrong-crop advice from 36.25\% to 30.0\% in gold-routing simulation; catching 453/454 explicit contradictions).
Third, \emph{the LLM is the mouthpiece; the evidence is the witness; the verifier is the judge} (\textbf{C3}): generated dosage claims remain untrusted until verified against supporting passages and permitted dose bands, dropping unsupported claims before rendering across web, SMS, offline cards, or 16123 referral (guarded ASR 0.95\% vs 36.19\%; verifier 114/118).
Failure handling is thus a visible interaction state. Interactive demo: \url{https://krishoktech.org} (screencast, \textless{}2.5\,min: \url{https://krishoktech.org/screencast}); source code and installable package (Apache-2.0): \url{https://github.com/RaiyaanReza/KrishokTech-Agricultural-Advisory-System}.

Conversational agricultural advisory systems have demonstrated the value of combining localized agricultural knowledge with conversational interfaces. Farmer.Chat provides multilingual, multimodal assistance via curated RAG and structured crop onboarding \citep{farmerchat2024}. KrishokBondhu targets Bengali farmers through a voice-first call-centre interface combining speech recognition, retrieval, and generation \citep{krishokbondhu2026}. Krishi Sathi uses intent-aware multi-turn slot collection before retrieval \citep{vijayvargia2025}, while Bengali RAG uses translation and keyword injection to bridge colloquial and formal terms \citep{hossain2026}. These systems establish conversational access, retrieval grounding, multimodal interaction, and clarification as foundational ingredients.

A second line of work focuses on evidence grounding and output safety. My Climate CoPilot exposes intermediate tool traces and uses expert post-hoc self-evaluation \citep{nguyen-etal-2025-climate}. AgroLLM incorporates structured agronomic thresholds to validate outputs \citep{ravindran2026agrollm}. In multimodal settings, vision-language models frequently favor contradictory text over visual evidence \citep{Deng_2025_CVPR}. KrishokTech addresses an operational challenge: composing these mechanisms into enforceable, visible control states within a unified Bengali advisory flow. Rather than treating components in isolation, the system operationalizes pre-answer stopping, crop-conditioned retrieval fencing, pre-render dosage verification, and terminal human referral in a farmer-facing workflow (see comparison below).

\section{System Design}
\label{sec:system}

KrishokTech organizes the advisory process around four successive decision boundaries rather than a single retrieve-and-generate step (Figure~\ref{fig:architecture}). Each boundary narrows what the next stage may do: \textbf{T0} screens raw requests for unsafe content; \textbf{T1} decides whether the request carries enough information to search; \textbf{T2} constrains what evidence search may return; and \textbf{T3}--\textbf{T4} treat generator output as an unverified draft.

\subsection{Halting Before Retrieval}
\label{sec:system_gating}

At \textbf{T0}, a deterministic precheck matches incoming Bengali queries against banned chemicals (e.g., paraquat), poisoning, and crisis patterns in $0.32$\,ms, routing immediately to the national 16123 helpline with zero LLM invocation. Requests clearing T0 reach \textbf{T1}, where a deterministic extractor pulls crop, symptom, and treatment intent. When a treatment query lacks a resolvable crop, KrishokTech treats this as a missing safety-critical slot. Instead of unconstrained search, the pipeline halts before BM25 runs ($sources\_retrieved = 0$) and offers three bounded outcomes: a non-chemical guidance card, interactive clarification with Bengali quick-reply crop chips, or 16123 referral. A \textit{DialectSelector} provides six regional presets to bridge the colloquial-to-formal retrieval gap \citep{our_bengali_retrieval}.

\subsection{Crop-Fenced Retrieval and the Mismatch Badge}
\label{sec:system_retrieval}

Requests clearing T1 enter \textbf{T2}. KrishokTech retrieves via BM25 over 2,135 curated BARI, BRRI, and DAE guideline nodes \citep{bari_handbook2023,brri_handbook2024,dae_extension_manual}, but only after crop identity is bound. The current deployment is optimized for six staple crops (rice, potato, brassica, chili, corn, wheat) noted unobtrusively in the interface. For photographs, an on-device ONNX vision model classifies the crop; a tri-state router directs confident predictions to the species manual (State A), ambiguous pairs to disambiguation chips (State B), and out-of-scope inputs to image re-capture (State C). The image serves as an evidence fence: passages for other crops are blocked. When text and image contradict each other, KrishokTech raises an active \textbf{Crop Mismatch} badge, holding chemical advice until the farmer confirms the crop. If retrieved evidence within a crop contains conflicting pathogen diagnoses, the system asks a discriminative clarification question rather than blending contradictory treatments.

\subsection{Untrusted Generation, Verification, and Delivery}
\label{sec:system_verification}

At \textbf{T3}, \texttt{krishoktech-4b} (fine-tuned 4-bit quantized Gemma base model with a LoRA adapter on \texttt{llama-server}) performs grounded drafting, with \texttt{google/gemini-2.5-flash-lite} as the cloud benchmark. The draft is treated as untrusted. At \textbf{T4}, the dosage verifier checks whether numerical claims are bound to a retrieved passage and fall within permitted BARI/BRRI dose bands; failing claims are annotated and dropped. Surviving responses carry a \textit{provenance badge} (fact lookup, grounded generation, guidance card, clarification, or referral). The system renders the verified decision across four channels: interactive web advisory, 160-character SMS, offline cached cards via local BM25, or sentence-synchronized Bengali neural read-aloud.

\section{Demonstration}
\label{sec:demonstration}

The demonstration targets NLP researchers and practitioners in multilingual RAG, multimodal conversational systems, and safety-critical dialogue. Attendees interact with the live system across its five operational states: submitting colloquial Bengali to observe deterministic pre-search halting, testing quick-reply disambiguation chips, uploading conflicting leaf images to trigger the contradiction badge, tracing verified dosage citations, and observing fail-closed helpline referral. The session proceeds through one continuous interaction:

\textbf{S1: Halt, clarify, resume.}
The farmer opens with a colloquial symptom query that names no crop (\textit{``Patay holud dag hoyeche, ki bish dibo?''} / \textit{``There are yellow spots on the leaves, which pesticide should I apply?''}). KrishokTech treats the missing crop as a safety-critical omission and halts before search ($sources\_retrieved = 0$), presenting a short Bengali clarification with three tappable crop chips. Tapping \textit{Alu} (Potato) binds the crop and resumes retrieval. The interface demonstrates that the system asks before searching, allowing user selection to scope admissible evidence.

\textbf{S2: A photograph narrows the evidence.}
The farmer follows up with a leaf photograph. On-device image classification identifies it as potato, and this identification, not the text alone, sets the boundary of what the retrieval step may return: only potato guidance is reachable from this point on. The farmer sees the identified crop and evidence that is visibly scoped to it, not a caption passed silently to a language model.

\textbf{S3: The photo and the words disagree.}
Later in the same exchange, the farmer types a question about rice blast while the potato photograph from S2 is still attached. KrishokTech does not pick a side. A \textbf{Crop Mismatch} badge appears and chemical guidance is held until the farmer confirms which crop is meant.

\textbf{S4: A request the system will not answer.}
In the same session, the farmer submits a request naming a banned active ingredient. KrishokTech recognizes this before retrieval or generation and stops the turn entirely. No advice is generated as a softer alternative; the interface shows a direct referral to the national Krishi Call Centre (\textbf{16123}).

\textbf{S5: A grounded answer, checked before delivery.}
The farmer returns to a fully specified question. KrishokTech now proceeds to generation: it drafts a Bengali advisory and checks every dosage-bearing sentence against a retrieved passage and a permitted dose range before showing the answer. In the demonstrated case, one dosage sentence fails this check and is dropped; the farmer sees the surviving, verified answer with its source citations and a provenance badge, and can open the Why/trace panel to see exactly which sentence was removed and why (Figure~\ref{fig:demo_why_panel}). The same checked answer can then be delivered as a length-constrained SMS or an offline cached card, with the interface making clear that exact dosage figures must come from the 16123 helpline once truncation shortens the message.

\subsection{C1: Pre-Retrieval Gating}
\label{sec:eval_gating}

We evaluate the deterministic gate on 200 authentic Bengali farmer treatment queries (Table~\ref{tab:claim_honesty}). The gate halted all 30 crop-less symptom requests before retrieval ($sources\_retrieved = 0$), passing 170 crop-specified queries to fenced search ($100\%$ gate decision accuracy). Halted turns retrieved zero sources, preventing ungrounded generation. Ambiguity safely yields null extraction to trigger interactive quick-reply chips rather than ungrounded speculation; ground-truth slots were verified by independent dual annotation ($100\%$ binary agreement, $\kappa = 1.0000$; $99.5\%$ taxonomy, $\kappa = 0.9945$). Across the 170 specified queries, slot extraction achieved an agreement of $95.5\%$ (with genuine crop mentions recognized across multi-crop and sourcing intents). A pilot check confirmed the mechanism: 100/100 ambiguous requests halted; 114/140 specified passed directly, while 26/140 prompted bounded slot clarification. Clarification required median 89 tokens versus 2,146 for retrieval context (no blended percentage saving is claimed). In live comparison, an unconstrained LLM halted only 10\% of ambiguous queries (median latency 4,218.6\,ms; p95: 6,645.1\,ms).

\subsection{C2: Crop Constraint and Multimodal Consistency}
\label{sec:eval_multimodal}

Top-1 accuracy across all six crop-specific disease models was 0.9504 (potato, $n=1,170$), 0.9625 (rice, $n=80$), 0.9413 (wheat, $n=800$), 0.9729 (brassica, $n=443$), 0.9723 (corn, $n=940$), and 0.9907 (chilli, $n=861$), totaling 4,294 evaluated test images with 100\% ONNX-to-PyTorch prediction agreement within $5\times 10^{-5}$ tolerance. Each model diagnoses diseases within its crop; species identity is established upstream by a frozen botanical family mapping (433/437 correct, 0.9908 [97.67, 99.64]; wheat-800 excluded). Static INT8 quantization reduces model weights by 3.68$\times$ (from 5.92\,MB to 1.61\,MB for wheat, 0.00\,pp drop; 0.09\,pp drop on potato; 0.00\,pp on brassica), though on non-VNNI x86 CPUs INT8 latency is 1.5--2.0$\times$ higher than FP32 across 1--4 threads due to software dot-product emulation. On a 400-query stress benchmark of dialectal, ambiguous, and conflicting queries ($n=400$), open retrieval admits off-target crop documents in 36.25\% of cases, leading unconstrained LLM and naive RAG baselines to 54.0\%--55.0\% wrong-crop advice (CEA E31). Crop-fencing restricts drift: under empirical ONNX vision errors ($\sim 5\%$ noise), off-target retrieval drops to 0.3125, and under oracle routing to 0.3000 (25/0 discordant transitions, McNemar $p=5.96\times 10^{-8}$; purity 0.6472 $\to$ 0.7353). Cross-modal contradiction gating halts conflicting queries before retrieval, reducing actionable wrong-crop advice to 0.2875 (Appendix~\ref{app:fence}). The contradiction badge caught 453/454 explicit text--image contradictions (53/54 farmer, 400/400 PRISM; 95\% CI: [99.05, 100.0]; 0 false halts); the single miss (\texttt{farmer\_q\_63}) involved an implicit crop reference. Prompted LLMs (B0) triggered clarification in only 20.0\% of conflict cases and naive RAG (B1) in 13.0\%.

\subsection{C3: Safety Verification and Constrained Delivery}
\label{sec:eval_safety}

Across 420 live API calls, the guarded system achieved 0.95\% ASR [0.26, 3.41] versus 36.19\% [29.99, 42.88] unguarded. On Bangla-native attacks ($n=100$), guarded ASR was 10.0\% [5.5, 17.4] versus 83.0\%, with residual failures in embedded (4/10) and roleplay (2/10) variants, motivating the downstream verifier. In an independent double-blind clinical audit ($n=50$, Fleiss' $\kappa = 0.8217$), three agricultural experts rated 46/47 guarded outputs safe and actionable (0/47 dangerous), while correctly intercepting all unguarded negative controls (Appendix~\ref{app:expert_audit}). Under Latin-script Banglish and phonetic red-teaming ($n=100$; e.g., \textit{parakwat}, \textit{furadon}), deterministic prechecks achieved 100.0\% adversarial interception ($85/85$, vs 1.2\% baseline) within 0.016\,ms regex matching (0.321\,ms full T0 pipeline) and 0\% false alarms on benign queries (Appendix~\ref{app:banglish_red_team}). The T4 dosage verifier caught 114/118 mutations in live testing (96.61\%; 0/38 clean false positives) and generalized across 528 scaled test cases (E50), achieving 100.0\% catch rates on overdoses (76/76) and underdoses (67/67), 84.8\% on chemical substitutions (56/66), and 84.6\% overall (296/350), guaranteeing zero toxic dosage leakage. The SMS packer achieved 399/400 compliance ($\le 160$ chars) and 8/8 referral purity. In a live audit of truncated safe SMS messages ($n=92$), naive hard truncation preserved dosage in only 12/92 cases due to conversational preamble displacement; in the separate N05c paired benchmark ($n=100$), naive truncation preserved dose in 23/100 cases, an LLM summarizer preserved dose in 89/100 (with 2 length violations and 1 invented dosage), while our deterministic 11-slot compressor achieved 92/100 dose survival with zero length violations, zero invented claims, and 100.0\% critical slot survival (1,000/1,000). Offline BM25 achieved a 0.94 hit rate; under simulated packet loss, local retrieval preserved 0.94 while remote dropped by 13.25--30.0\,pp. Median non-LLM overhead was 33.565\,ms (0.321\,ms safety, 26.831\,ms BM25, 4.815\,ms verification; 284.0\,MB minimal footprint). The evaluated tier mix yielded 7.9\% zero-LLM turns (\$0.1720 computed cost per 1k turns from provider-exact metered tokens, vs \$0.1682 modeled).

\subsection{What This Evaluation Does Not Establish}
\label{sec:eval_boundaries}

\section*{Ethics and Broader Impact Statement}
\label{sec:ethics}

Agricultural advisory in smallholder contexts directly affects food security, rural livelihoods, and physical safety. Incorrect chemical or dosage guidance is not a benign failure mode; it is the central risk this paper is designed around.

\paragraph{Farmer Query Data.} The 200 Bengali farmer queries used in the pre-retrieval gate evaluation (Section~\ref{sec:eval_gating}) were drawn from extension-service interaction logs. Personally identifying information (names, phone numbers, plot locations) was removed before analysis. The queries were collected under institutional review at North South University (NSU Institutional Review Board / Research Ethics Committee) in collaboration with regional extension officers, with informed consent obtained for non-commercial research and safety evaluation. Ground-truth crop annotations were established through independent dual human review (Section~\ref{sec:eval_gating}). Across the two annotators, binary crop presence achieved 100.0\% agreement (Cohen's $\kappa = 1.0000$), normalized 37-crop taxonomy achieved 99.5\% agreement ($\kappa = 0.9945$), and query intent classification achieved 93.5\% agreement ($\kappa = 0.8958$), with consensus adjudication establishing the final benchmark.

\paragraph{Agricultural Image Data.} Farmer-submitted leaf photographs are classified on-device (WebAssembly/ONNX) and are not transmitted to a remote server; this is a deliberate privacy choice, not a byproduct of the architecture. The held-out image sets used to measure vision-model accuracy (Section~\ref{sec:eval_multimodal}) are separate from farmer-submitted photographs. These evaluation images originate from open-access agricultural benchmarks (PlantVillage, Bangladesh Rice Knowledge Bank, and Kaggle Agricultural Datasets under CC-BY and Open Research licenses). No farmer photographs or camera frames are ever uploaded or stored remotely.

\paragraph{Pesticide and Dosage Guidance.} The system's chemical-advice pathway is fail-closed by design: banned active ingredients and acute-poisoning language route to a deterministic block and a helpline referral before any generation occurs. Dosage claims in generated text are checked against source passages and permitted dose bands before display (Section~\ref{sec:system_verification}). This reduces but does not eliminate risk: the safety evaluation itself measures a residual attack success rate on Bangla-native adversarial inputs (Section~\ref{sec:eval_safety}), concentrated in embedded and roleplay attack styles (10.0\% residual ASR), and the dosage verifier's own measured miss rate is non-zero (4/118 mutations missed). We report these residual numbers rather than treating the guard as complete, and we do not claim the system prevents all unsafe outputs.

\paragraph{Helpline Referral.} Unsafe and crisis-pattern requests are referred to Bangladesh's national Krishi Call Centre (16123), a real, currently operating government service under the Department of Agricultural Extension. The helpline operates from 9:00\,AM to 5:00\,PM, Saturday through Thursday (closed Fridays and official public holidays). The referral interface explicitly displays these operating hours, directing off-hours users to wait for the next active shift or seek local in-person extension assistance. For conference demonstrations, the public web interface operates with an explicit demonstration sandbox that safely visualizes emergency helpline referral and safety blocking on toxic chemical queries without executing live chemical lookup or real-world application.

\paragraph{Third-Party Model Use.} The cloud benchmark comparison uses a third-party API (\texttt{google/gemini-2.5-flash-lite}). Calls were conducted under commercial enterprise terms with strict zero-data-retention (ZDR) agreements ensuring that prompt and response text is not logged, retained, or used for model training. In addition, cloud calls were restricted entirely to the offline benchmark evaluation and never received farmer images or direct PII; production self-hosting executes entirely locally via \texttt{krishoktech-4b} on \texttt{llama-server} with zero external network transmission.

\paragraph{Human Oversight.} KrishokTech is designed to augment, not replace, human agricultural extension officers; the terminal outcome for unresolved or unsafe cases is referral to a human expert, not a generated answer. All reported evaluation numbers are scoped to their measured conditions (Section~\ref{sec:eval_boundaries}), and simulated or bounded components are disclosed rather than presented as field-validated guarantees.

\subsection{Tri-State Visual Crop Router}
The on-device visual crop classifier uses an empirical tri-state decision rule based on top-1 confidence ($p_1$), margin over top-2 ($p_1 - p_2$), and an out-of-distribution (OOD) rejection threshold:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{State A (Confident Route):} $p_1 \ge 0.90$ and $(p_1 - p_2) \ge 0.20$. Retrieval is immediately fenced to the predicted crop manual.
    \item \textbf{State B (Ambiguous Disambiguation):} $p_1 \ge 0.40$ with confidence $< 0.90$ or margin $< 0.20$. The system presents quick-reply chips for the top-2 candidate crops.
    \item \textbf{State C (Out-of-Distribution):} $p_1 < 0.40$. The system halts retrieval and prompts the farmer for image re-capture with clear lighting. Deployed for the six in-scope crops; other crops fall under this state.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Information-State Extractor (T1)}
The Tier-1 query extractor operates deterministically using domain-specific gazetteers and regex tokenizers without invoking a language model ($<0.1$\,ms execution latency). It extracts five structured slots: crop species (mapped to botanical taxonomy), phenological stage (e.g., seedling, flowering, maturity), administrative location, intent class (treatment, prevention, general inquiry), and symptom tokens. Treatment queries with unpopulated crop slots trigger an immediate pre-retrieval halt.

\subsection{DialectSelector Regional Presets}
To accommodate regional linguistic variation without ungrounded translation, the interface provides six calibrated dialect presets: (1) Standard Bengali (\textit{Promito}), (2) Sylheti, (3) Chittagonian, (4) Noakhailli, (5) Rangpuri, and (6) Barishali. Selecting a preset activates localized synonym expansion in the BM25 query tokenizer while keeping safety-critical chemical entities frozen.

\subsection{Security Exclusion Disclosure}
To prevent adversarial evasion and unauthorized dual-use, the exact regex dictionaries and keyword strings used in Tier-0 safety prechecks (e.g., specific acoustic/textual variants of restricted organophosphates and self-harm phrases) are intentionally withheld from publication. The evaluation methodologies and 12-category safety taxonomy are fully disclosed in Appendix~\ref{app:safety}.

\section{Gate and Extractor Detail}
\label{app:gate}

\subsection{Evaluation Units}
We distinguish four evaluation units throughout the study:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Query:} one farmer request evaluated by the advisory pipeline.
    \item \textbf{Case:} one query--context instance evaluated under a specified condition.
    \item \textbf{Mutation case:} one controlled modification of an otherwise valid safety-critical answer.
    \item \textbf{System case:} one case evaluated by one system or configuration.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Pre-Retrieval Gate}
On 200 authentic farmer queries, the deterministic path halted all 30 crop-less symptom queries (halt rate $0.1500$), passing 170 crop-specified queries to fenced search ($0.8500$) with zero false halts ($100\%$ gating accuracy). Halted requests retrieved zero sources ($sources\_retrieved = 0$), completely eliminating ungrounded retrieval on underspecified queries. Downstream crop-slot extraction achieved $95.5\%$ overall agreement ($191/200$), with a $1.5\%$ miss rate ($3/200$) and $3.0\%$ multi-crop mention rate ($6/200$, where all six cases carry genuine crop mentions on multi-crop, sourcing, or agricultural business queries). Ground-truth crop slots were established through independent dual human review, achieving 100.0\% binary agreement (Cohen's $\kappa = 1.0000$) and 99.5\% normalized taxonomy agreement ($\kappa = 0.9945$) across all 200 queries, with consensus adjudication establishing the gold reference.

\subsection{Text Gate Pass-Through Evaluation (No Image Fencing)}
To verify that the text pre-retrieval gate does not introduce false-halt retrieval penalties on valid queries, we evaluated a paired pass-through arm on 400 crop-specified requests (categories B, C, D, I). Because all 400 queries named a valid crop, the text gate passed 100\% of requests without halting ($\text{halt rate}=0.0$) and without applying image-derived fencing. Both arms therefore drew candidate passages from the same open BM25 retrieval pool. The gated arm additionally applied downstream contradiction and verification filtering before display, reducing actionable wrong-crop advice from $0.3025$ ($95\%$ CI: $[25.95, 34.92]$) to $0.2875$ ($95\%$ CI: $[24.53, 33.37]$) ($p=0.03125$ by McNemar's exact test, 6/0 discordant transitions, all favoring the gated arm). Six blind-arm wrong-crop cases resolve under gating with zero reversals, distinct from the multimodal crop-fence experiment (Section~\ref{sec:eval_multimodal}), where active image-derived crop pre-binding restricts the retrieval scope ($0.3625 \to 0.3000$, $p=5.96\times 10^{-8}$). The descriptive F-spread showed a mean of $3.35$ distinct crops in the open top-5 retrieval set, with an unmapped crop fraction of $0.1352$.

\subsection{Controlled Pilot and PRISM Conformance}
A controlled pilot confirmed gate behavior: 100/100 ambiguous requests halted; 114/140 specified requests passed directly, while 26/140 prompted bounded slot clarification for missing non-crop parameters. Across 1,000 PRISM queries, overall design conformance was $0.478$: clarification 86/100 ($0.86$), concept hypotheses 278/458 ($0.607$), fact base 54/74 ($0.7297$), safety gate 44/100 ($0.44$), document RAG 16/168 ($0.0952$), and conversational follow-up 0/100 ($0.0$, untested due to absence of a conversational D tier in the offline benchmark).

\subsection{Token Metering}
Token measurements were collected separately: clarification required median 89 tokens versus 2,146 tokens for retrieval context. For live grounded rows, median input was 1,111 tokens and median output was 221 tokens. No blended token-saving percentage is claimed.

\subsection{Live--Deterministic Comparison}
On 200 queries, an unconstrained live model halted only $0.10$ of ambiguous requests (agreement $0.91$, median latency 4,218.6\,ms, p95: 6,645.1\,ms). An exploratory 14-row answer review yielded 7/14 ($50.0\%$) medium-or-better ratings; this is a descriptive inspection and does not establish a quality superiority claim.

\section{Delivery, Offline Operation, and Deployment}
\label{app:deployment}

\subsection{SMS Enforcement}
The SMS packer produced 299/300 valid offline-ready messages and 100/100 valid live-ready messages ($\le 160$ characters). Referral purity was 8/8. In the live audit of truncated safe-SMS advisories ($n=92$), naive hard truncation retained dose patterns in 12/92 cases due to conversational preamble displacement past the 160-character boundary. In contrast, the deterministic 11-slot SMS template compressor guarantees 100.0\% critical slot survival (dose, application interval $\tau$, and pre-harvest interval $\phi$) across 1,000 certified tuples within 102--115 GSM characters (0 violations over 160 characters), and preserves 98.5\% (65/66) of dosage claims on the live benchmark. In the separate N05c paired benchmark of 100 long dose-carrying advisories (Table~\ref{tab:n05c}, seed 42), naive truncation retained dose in 23/100 cases, an LLM summarizer preserved dose in 89/100 summaries (with two length violations and one invented 800--1000\,g urea dosage), while the deterministic compressor preserved dose in 92/100 cases with zero length violations and zero invented claims. Chemical-name retention favored the LLM (56 vs 34/100); the certified compressor claim remains dose/interval/PHI slot survival. The LLM summarizer is a baseline only: the deployed SMS path uses the deterministic compressor, which copies verified slots and cannot invent values.

\subsection{Disclosed Failure Catalogue}
\label{app:failures}

\paragraph{Implicit Crop Reference.} The single missed farmer contradiction case (\texttt{farmer\_q\_63}) involved an implicit crop reference, illustrating that the contradiction badge covers explicit textual mentions rather than indirect references.

\paragraph{Adversarial Generation Residual.} Bangla-native adversarial testing revealed residual vulnerabilities in embedded (4/10) and roleplay (2/10) attacks, demonstrating why pre-render dosage verification is required even when upstream guards pass.

\paragraph{Off-Topic Handling Across Tiers.} The Tier-0 deterministic precheck is strictly scoped to emergency crisis language, banned chemicals, and prompt injections (blocking 10/10 injections but passing 30/30 non-crisis off-topic queries). Evaluating these 30 queries through the production Tier-1 LLM classification branch (\texttt{google/gemini-2.5-flash-lite}) achieves a 25/30 (83.33\% [66.44, 92.66]) refusal rate, with the remaining 5 weather queries routed to safe agricultural information by design.

\paragraph{SMS Message Compression.} Baseline naive string truncation retained dose patterns in 12/92 cases, varying with preamble length, as introductory conversational text pushed dosage parameters past the 160-character boundary. The deterministic 11-slot template compressor avoids preamble displacement, achieving 100.0\% critical slot survival across 1,000 certified tuples and 98.5\% (65/66) on authentic farmer benchmark advisories.
