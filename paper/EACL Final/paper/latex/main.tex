\begin{abstract}
Bengali agricultural advisory systems face a particular difficulty: treatment requests arrive without a crop name, photographs contradict the accompanying text, and generated dosage claims can exceed what the retrieved evidence supports. \textsc{KrishokTech} treats these uncertainties as explicit control states rather than leaving them to the language model. A deterministic gate halts crop-less treatment queries before retrieval runs. Crop identification fences the evidence set, making off-target passages unreachable, and a confirmation state surfaces when text and image disagree. Before rendering, every dosage-bearing claim in the generated draft is checked against retrieved passages and permitted dose ranges; unsupported claims are dropped. The demonstration follows one farmer interaction through clarification (\textbf{ASK}), multimodal conflict (\textbf{CONFIRM}), safety referral (\textbf{REFER}), and claim filtering (\textbf{DROP}). On 200 authentic farmer queries, the gate halted all 30 crop-less requests before retrieval. Crop fencing with contradiction gating reduced off-target retrieval from 36.25\% to 28.75\% on a 400-query stress set. On the deployed local model, guarded attack success fell from 52.5\% to 10.71\%; on the cloud benchmark, from 36.19\% to 0.95\%. The dosage verifier caught 114 of 118 inserted errors in live answers without rejecting any unmodified response.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

When yellow spots appear on potato leaves, a farmer may need advice before an extension visit is available. Phone-based and digital advisory services can extend agricultural information beyond face-to-face extension channels \citep{dae_extension_manual,dlec_bangladesh,aker-2011-dial-a,fabregas-etal-2019-digital,campenhout-etal-2021-ict}. But in a treatment setting, fluent conversation alone is not enough: an answer for the wrong crop or an unsupported dosage can turn an incomplete question into a harmful recommendation.

Several small uncertainties can cause this failure. A farmer may ask ``which pesticide should I spray?'' without naming the crop, leaving retrieval to search across unrelated agricultural guidance. Farmers may also write in colloquial, regional, or Romanized Bangla, while technical guidance uses more formal terminology \citep{bhuiyan-etal-2025-bhasabodh,hossain2026,our_bengali_retrieval}. The photograph and text can disagree as well: the image may indicate one crop while the message names another, and a system that silently chooses one modality leaves the conflict unresolved. Studies of vision-language models show that such conflicts can cause models to over-rely on textual cues, resolve modalities inconsistently, or hallucinate visual content \citep{Deng_2025_CVPR,zhu2024cross,zhang-etal-2025-modality,nooralahzadeh-etal-2026,li-etal-2023-object-halluc}, while Bangla multimodal datasets and benchmarks highlight the additional challenges of low-resource visual-language alignment \citep{Barua_2024,chitramiti2026}. Retrieved evidence also does not guarantee faithful generation: language models can produce claims that are unsupported by the supplied context \citep{Mulakkayala_2026,qin-etal-2025-premise,zhang-zhang-2025-review,brown-etal-2025-rag,xu-etal-2025-mega-rag}. For agricultural advice, this is particularly important for dosage, where a plausible numerical claim can be directly actionable \citep{our_agricultural_safety}.

\textsc{KrishokTech} addresses these failures by making uncertainty and safety decisions explicit rather than leaving them to the language model. The system asks for missing information before retrieval, surfaces disagreements between text and images instead of silently resolving them, refers blocked safety-critical requests to human assistance, and checks generated safety-critical claims before they reach the farmer. These decisions appear as four farmer-visible states: \textbf{ASK}, \textbf{CONFIRM}, \textbf{REFER}, and \textbf{DROP}.

Concretely, KrishokTech uses three control mechanisms. First, (\textbf{C1}) a deterministic gate halts underspecified treatment queries before retrieval and requests the missing crop or routes the case to the helpline. Second, (\textbf{C2}) crop identification constrains retrieval to evidence for the identified crop and raises a confirmation state when the farmer's text and image disagree. Third, (\textbf{C3}) dosage-bearing claims are treated as unverified drafts: each claim is checked against retrieved passages and reference dose ranges from BARI/BRRI guidance before rendering, and unsupported claims are dropped. Section~\ref{sec:evaluation} evaluates each mechanism against the failure it is designed to prevent.

The demonstration follows one continuous farmer session through the four states, from a colloquial crop-less query to crop-scoped evidence, multimodal conflict, safety referral, and verified answer delivery (Section~\ref{sec:demonstration}). Interactive demo: \url{https://krishoktech-one.vercel.app}; screencast (\textless{}2.5\,min): \url{https://krishoktech-one.vercel.app/screencast}. Source code and installable package are available under Apache-2.0 at \url{https://anonymous.4open.science/r/KrishokChat-Agricultural-Advisory-System-FCEE}.

\section{Related Work and Positioning}
\label{sec:related_work}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth,keepaspectratio]{figures/fig1_architecture.jpg}
\caption{KrishokTech inserts explicit control points before and after generation. Each of the five stages can end a turn in a farmer-visible state: REFER at T0, ASK at T1, CONFIRM at T2, or DROP at T4.}
\label{fig:architecture}
\end{figure*}

Digital agricultural advisors such as Farmer.Chat, KrishokBondhu, and Krishi Sathi have paired localized agricultural knowledge with conversational interfaces to improve access to timely advice \citep{farmerchat2024,krishokbondhu2026,vijayvargia2025}. Bengali agricultural RAG systems have further explored how farmer queries can be matched to more formal scientific knowledge \citep{hossain2026}. These systems establish retrieval grounding, multilingual or local-language interaction, and multi-turn information gathering as practical building blocks for agricultural advisory. KrishokTech focuses on a different question: \emph{when should the system be allowed to proceed?} A treatment request that omits the crop is not simply an incomplete conversational turn; it leaves a safety-critical retrieval condition unresolved. Rather than allowing the generator to infer the missing information, KrishokTech treats this uncertainty as an explicit control decision.

Clarification and evidence verification provide the foundations for this decision-oriented design. Work on conversational information seeking has long shown that asking a clarifying question can improve retrieval when a user's initial request is underspecified \citep{aliannejadi-etal-2019-clarifying}. More recently, LLM-based agents have been shown to guess or hedge under ambiguity instead of reliably asking for the missing information, while ClarQ-LLM shows that even strong models remain imperfect at information-gathering dialogue \citep{chen2024act,gan2024clarqllm}. On the generation side, MiniCheck demonstrates efficient claim-level checking of generated text against grounding documents, and MedRAGChecker extends atomic claim verification to safety-sensitive biomedical RAG \citep{tang2024minicheck,ji2026medragchecker}. Interactive systems such as My Climate CoPilot, FactSearch, and RAGVUE likewise emphasize grounded answers, inspectable evidence, and fine-grained diagnosis rather than treating the generated response as a single opaque output \citep{nguyen-etal-2025-climate,fang-mackenzie-2026-factsearch,murugaraj-etal-2026-ragvue}. KrishokTech brings these ideas together around explicit pipeline boundaries: \textbf{ASK} can stop retrieval, \textbf{CONFIRM} can stop an unresolved multimodal conflict, and \textbf{DROP} can stop an unsupported safety-critical claim from reaching the user.

The multimodal setting introduces a further failure mode. When text and visual evidence conflict, vision-language models do not always reconcile the two sources correctly; controlled studies have found strong tendencies to rely on misleading textual information and persistent cross-modal knowledge conflicts \citep{Deng_2025_CVPR,zhu2024cross}. This matters for Bengali deployment because dialectal and Romanized Bangla remain under-resourced, with recent work explicitly documenting the use of regional dialects and Latin-script Bangla in real-world language technology \citep{bhuiyan-etal-2025-bhasabodh,bangla_low_resource}. KrishokTech therefore does not silently select one modality when crop evidence disagrees. It surfaces the disagreement and asks for confirmation before crop-specific advice proceeds. The resulting contribution is not a claim of historical priority for any individual mechanism, but the integration of these controls into a single farmer-facing pipeline in which uncertainty and safety are explicit, observable states: \textbf{ASK}, \textbf{CONFIRM}, \textbf{REFER}, and \textbf{DROP}.

\section{System Design}
\label{sec:system}

KrishokTech is organized around the four intervention states introduced in Section~\ref{sec:introduction}, implemented as five pipeline stages rather than a single retrieve-and-generate step (Figure~\ref{fig:architecture}). Each stage narrows what the next stage is allowed to do: \textbf{T0} screens the raw request for content that should never reach retrieval or generation (\textbf{REFER}); \textbf{T1} decides whether the request carries enough information to search at all (\textbf{ASK} if not); \textbf{T2} constrains what evidence a search may return once it runs, and flags disagreement between modalities (\textbf{CONFIRM}); and \textbf{T3}--\textbf{T4} treat the generator's output as an unverified draft until it is checked (\textbf{DROP} on failure).

\subsection{Halting Before Retrieval}
\label{sec:system_gating}

At \textbf{T0}, a deterministic precheck matches incoming Bengali queries against banned chemicals (e.g., paraquat), poisoning, and crisis patterns in $0.32$\,ms and, on a match, \textbf{refers} the request to the national 16123 helpline with zero LLM invocation. Requests that clear T0 reach \textbf{T1}, where a deterministic extractor pulls crop, symptom, and treatment intent without invoking a language model. A treatment query with no resolvable crop is treated as missing a safety-critical slot: rather than search on an incomplete premise, the pipeline halts before BM25 runs ($sources\_retrieved = 0$) and \textbf{asks}, offering a non-chemical guidance card, interactive clarification with Bengali quick-reply crop chips, or helpline referral. A \textit{DialectSelector} covers six regional presets to narrow the colloquial-to-formal retrieval gap \citep{our_bengali_retrieval}.

\subsection{Crop-Fenced Retrieval and the Mismatch Badge}
\label{sec:system_retrieval}

Requests that clear T1 enter \textbf{T2}. KrishokTech retrieves via BM25 over 2,135 curated BARI, BRRI, and DAE guideline nodes \citep{bari_handbook2023,brri_handbook2024,dae_extension_manual}, but only once crop identity is bound; the current deployment covers six staple crops (rice, potato, brassica, chili, corn, wheat), surfaced unobtrusively in the interface. When a photograph is attached, an on-device ONNX vision model classifies the crop, and a tri-state router sends confident predictions straight to the matching species manual, sends ambiguous pairs to disambiguation chips, and sends out-of-scope images back for re-capture (exact thresholds in Appendix~\ref{app:architecture}). The predicted crop then acts as a fence on retrieval: passages for every other crop become unreachable, not merely unranked. When the farmer's text and the attached image name different crops, KrishokTech does not pick one silently; it raises a \textbf{Crop Mismatch} badge and \textbf{confirms} with the farmer before releasing any chemical advice. The same discipline applies within a single crop: if the retrieved evidence itself contains conflicting pathogen diagnoses, KrishokTech asks a discriminative question rather than blending the two treatments into one answer.

\subsection{Untrusted Generation, Verification, and Delivery}
\label{sec:system_verification}

At \textbf{T3}, \texttt{krishokchat-4b} (a 4-bit quantized Gemma base model fine-tuned with a LoRA adapter, served via \texttt{llama-server}) drafts a grounded answer, with \texttt{google/gemini-2.5-flash-lite} used as the cloud benchmark throughout evaluation; either way, the draft is treated as unverified text, not a final answer. At \textbf{T4}, the dosage verifier checks every numerical claim against a retrieved passage and a permitted BARI/BRRI dose band: a claim that is not bound to evidence, or that falls outside the permitted range, is annotated and \textbf{dropped} rather than shown. Surviving responses carry one of five provenance badges (fact lookup, grounded generation, guidance card, clarification, or referral; detailed in Appendix~\ref{app:architecture}), so a farmer can see, at a glance, why a given answer is on screen. The verified decision then reaches the farmer through one of four channels: an interactive web advisory, a 160-character SMS, an offline cached card served from local BM25, or sentence-synchronized Bengali neural read-aloud.

\section{Demonstration}
\label{sec:demonstration}

The demonstration is intended for researchers and practitioners who build multilingual, multimodal, or safety-critical conversational systems. Attendees follow one farmer session on the live system in five steps, S1 to S5, and the screencast follows the same order. The steps cover all four states from Section~\ref{sec:introduction}: \textbf{ASK} (S1), \textbf{CONFIRM} (S3), \textbf{REFER} (S4), and \textbf{DROP} (S5). S2 shows the crop fence that S3 depends on. Attendees can start from the sample queries on the start screen, which include regional-dialect and safety-test sets, or enter their own queries and photographs.

\textbf{S1: Missing crop (ASK).}
The farmer types a colloquial query that names no crop: \textit{``Patay holud dag hoyeche, ki bish dibo?''} (``Yellow spots have appeared on the leaves. Which pesticide should I use?''). A treatment request without a crop lacks a safety-critical detail, so the \textbf{T1} gate stops the request before retrieval ($sources\_retrieved = 0$) and asks which crop is affected. The reply shows crop chips (Figure~\ref{fig:demo_ask}). The farmer taps \textit{Alu} (Potato), which binds the crop, and the system answers from potato guidance with numbered citations.

\textbf{S2: Photograph scopes the evidence.}
The farmer then uploads a leaf photograph on the diagnosis page. The on-device classifier identifies the crop as rice and runs the rice disease model. The chat that opens from this page takes rice as its context, and the \textbf{T2} fence lets retrieval return only rice guidance. The photograph never leaves the device.

\textbf{S3: Photograph and text disagree (CONFIRM).}
With the rice photograph still attached, the farmer asks how to control potato late blight. The system does not pick a crop. It raises a \textbf{Crop Mismatch} badge, states that the photograph shows rice while the message names potato, and withholds chemical advice until the farmer confirms the crop (Figure~\ref{fig:demo_mismatch}).

\textbf{S4: Banned pesticide (REFER).}
The farmer next asks how to spray paraquat, a banned pesticide. The \textbf{T0} precheck matches the request before any retrieval or generation, so the system produces no advice. It shows a banned-chemical warning and a button to call the Krishi Call Centre (\textbf{16123}).

\textbf{S5: Verified answer (DROP).}
The farmer finally asks which fungicide to spray against potato late blight. The system retrieves evidence, drafts a Bengali answer, and checks each dosage sentence against a retrieved passage and a permitted dose range. In the demonstrated case, the \textbf{T4} verifier drops one sentence. The farmer sees the remaining answer with its citations and a provenance badge, and the Why panel shows the verification trace behind that decision (Figure~\ref{fig:demo_why}). The same verified answer can be read aloud in Bengali, formatted as a 160-character SMS, or opened as an offline card. Appendix~\ref{app:ui} shows the screens for each step.

\subsection{Pre-Retrieval Gate (C1)}
\label{sec:eval-gate}

A treatment request that names no crop cannot be answered safely, because open retrieval may return guidance for a different crop. We test the gate on 200 authentic Bengali farmer treatment queries, 30 of which name no crop. Two annotators labeled the crop slot independently and agreed on every crop-presence decision (Cohen's $\kappa = 1.00$). The gate halted all 30 crop-less queries before retrieval, so these turns retrieved no sources, and it passed the other 170 to fenced search without a false halt. An unconstrained LLM asked to make the same decision halted 10\% of the 200 queries, against 15\% for the gate, agreed with the gate on 91\% of decisions, and took a median of 4.2\,s per query. On this set, a deterministic slot check matched the annotators on every query without calling a model. Slot-extraction accuracy, the controlled pilot, and token metering are in Appendix~\ref{app:gate}.

\subsection{Crop Fence and Mismatch Badge (C2)}
\label{sec:eval-fence}

Retrieval that ignores the crop can return guidance for the wrong crop, and a photograph can contradict the farmer's text. The six on-device disease classifiers reach 0.94 to 0.99 top-1 accuracy on 4,294 held-out test images. To test the fence, we use a 400-query stress set of dialectal, ambiguous, and conflicting queries, chosen because open search drifts on them. A query yields wrong-crop advice when its top five sources include a document for another crop. Open retrieval does so for 36.25\% of the queries. Fencing retrieval to the crop predicted by the on-device vision model, at its empirical error rate of about 5\%, lowers this to 31.25\%. Adding contradiction gating, which halts conflicting queries before retrieval, lowers it further to 28.75\%.

The mismatch badge caught 453 of 454 explicit text-image contradictions (53 of 54 farmer cases and all 400 cases in PRISM, our synthetic multimodal-conflict suite) with no false halts. The one miss used an implicit crop reference. On a separate set of 300 conflict queries, a prompted LLM asked the farmer to clarify in only 20\% of cases, and naive multimodal RAG in 13\%. The fence and badge therefore reduce wrong-crop advice on a set where open search drifts, but they do not remove it. Per-crop accuracy, quantization, and the full fence comparison are in Appendix~\ref{app:vision}.

\subsection{Safety Verification and Constrained Delivery (C3)}
\label{sec:eval-safety}

We first measure attack success rate (ASR), the share of adversarial prompts that yield an unsafe response, with the deterministic controls on (guarded) and off (unguarded). Across 420 live calls to the cloud benchmark model (\texttt{gemini-2.5-flash-lite}), 210 per arm, the guarded pipeline had an ASR of 0.95\% against 36.19\% unguarded. On the Bengali-native subset of 100 attacks, the guarded ASR was 10.0\% against 83.0\%, with the residual failures concentrated in embedded (4 of 10 prompts) and roleplay (2 of 10) variants. On the deployed model, \texttt{krishokchat-4b} (280 calls per arm), ASR falls from 52.5\% to 10.71\% overall and from 77.0\% to 19.0\% on the Bengali-native subset, so the residual ASR is higher than on the cloud model. Latin-script Banglish and phonetic spellings such as \textit{parakwat} also evade keyword gates written for formal text. A gate limited to formal English and Bengali script intercepted 1 of 85 adversarial queries in this style, whereas our deterministic precheck intercepted all 85 and raised no false alarm on 15 benign farmer questions.

Next we look at the advice itself. In a blinded audit, three evaluators (two agronomy or botany undergraduates and one extension officer) rated 46 of 47 guarded outputs as safe and actionable, one as vague but harmless, and none as dangerous (Fleiss' $\kappa = 0.82$). All three unguarded control outputs were rated dangerous. For the dosage verifier, we inserted controlled errors into verified answers. On 38 live answers with 118 inserted errors, it caught 114 and rejected none of the unmodified answers. On a scaled benchmark of 528 cases from 88 BARI and BRRI passages, it caught 296 of 350 inserted errors (84.6\%): every overdose (76 of 76) and underdose (67 of 67), but only 85\% of chemical substitutions, 73\% of unit substitutions, and 65\% of invented pre-harvest intervals.

Constrained channels must also preserve the dose. The SMS packer kept 399 of 400 messages within 160 characters. On 100 long advisories that carry a dose, naive truncation preserved the dose in 23, an LLM summarizer in 89 (with one invented dosage), and our deterministic compressor in 92, without length violations or invented values. Local BM25 retrieval, which serves the offline card, had a 0.94 hit rate and was unaffected by simulated packet loss, while remote retrieval lost 13 to 30 percentage points. The non-LLM stages add a median of 33.6\,ms per request. Appendices~\ref{app:safety} and~\ref{app:delivery} give the attack families, audit protocol, mutation table, and delivery measurements.

\subsection{What This Evaluation Does Not Establish}
\label{sec:eval-scope}

The results hold only under the conditions in which they were measured. We ran no field study, so farmer adoption and yield effects are unmeasured. The crop-fence results come from a benchmark of paired queries under empirical vision noise, not from live farmer sessions, and most contradiction cases (400 of 454) are synthetic. The expert audit covers 47 guarded outputs, network resilience was tested only with simulated packet loss, and the dosage verifier checks numerical dosage statements, so other agronomic claims in an answer are not verified. Appendix~\ref{app:evidence} marks each result as measured, simulated, pilot, or modeled.

\section{Limitations, Availability, and Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

Four limitations matter most; Appendix~\ref{app:limitations_full} gives the full ledger.

\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}\setlength{\topsep}{2pt}\setlength{\parsep}{0pt}
\item Coverage is limited to six staple crops and Bengali input. Retrieval is lexical (BM25), and dense retrieval was not evaluated.
\item The mismatch badge detects explicit crop conflicts only, and it missed the one implicit reference in our test set.
\item The safety controls lower attack success but do not remove it, and the deployed local model is more exposed than the cloud model (10.71\% against 0.95\% guarded attack success).
\item SMS compression is lossy: on 100 long advisories it kept the dose in 92 but the chemical name in only 34. Generation with the local model on CPU takes about 24\,s per call.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Availability and Licensing}
\label{sec:availability}

KrishokTech is live at \url{https://krishoktech-one.vercel.app} (screencast: \url{https://krishoktech-one.vercel.app/screencast}). % BLIND REVIEW: restore HF mirror parenthetical for camera-ready Code, models, and container scripts are licensed under Apache-2.0 at \url{https://anonymous.4open.science/r/KrishokChat-Agricultural-Advisory-System-FCEE}, with models available at \url{https://huggingface.co/RaiyanKhaan/KrishokTech-Models}. % BLIND REVIEW: restore public repo URL for camera-ready The model \texttt{krishokchat-4b} and 2,135 extension nodes are on HuggingFace under CC-BY-4.0 (BARI, BRRI, and DAE guidelines). \subsection{Conclusion}
\label{sec:final_conclusion}

KrishokTech makes the boundary between model output and verified evidence visible to the farmer. Instead of leaving uncertainty and safety to the language model, it asks for a missing crop, confirms conflicts between text and photograph, refers unsafe requests to a human helpline, and drops dosage claims that the evidence does not support. Our evaluation shows that these controls reduce wrong-crop advice and unsafe output without eliminating either, so the system is meant to support extension officers and not replace them. A field study with farmers is the natural next step.

\section*{Ethics and Broader Impact Statement}
\label{sec:ethics}

Agricultural advice for smallholder farmers affects food security, livelihoods, and physical safety. Incorrect chemical or dosage guidance is the central risk this paper is designed around.

\paragraph{Farmer Query Data.} The 200 farmer queries in the gate evaluation (Section~\ref{sec:eval-gate}) come from extension-service interaction logs. Names, phone numbers, and plot locations were removed before analysis. The queries were collected in collaboration with regional extension officers under institutional review at North South University (Institutional Review Board / Research Ethics Committee), and informed consent was obtained for non-commercial research and safety evaluation. Two annotators labeled the crop slot independently, and consensus adjudication set the final labels. Agreement was 100.0\% for crop presence (Cohen's $\kappa = 1.0000$), 99.5\% for the normalized 37-crop taxonomy ($\kappa = 0.9945$), and 93.5\% for query intent ($\kappa = 0.8958$).

\paragraph{Agricultural Image Data.} Farmer leaf photographs are classified on the device (WebAssembly and ONNX) and are never uploaded or stored remotely, which is a deliberate privacy choice. The held-out images used to measure vision accuracy (Section~\ref{sec:eval-fence}) are separate from farmer photographs. They come from open-access agricultural datasets: PlantVillage, the Bangladesh Rice Knowledge Bank, and Kaggle agricultural datasets under CC-BY and open research licenses.

\paragraph{Pesticide and Dosage Guidance.} The chemical-advice pathway fails closed. Banned active ingredients and acute-poisoning language trigger a deterministic block and a helpline referral before any generation, and dosage claims in generated text are checked against source passages and permitted dose bands before display (Section~\ref{sec:system_verification}). These controls reduce risk but do not remove it. Guarded attack success is 10.71\% on the deployed model (19.0\% on Bengali-native attacks), and the dosage verifier missed 4 of 118 inserted errors (Section~\ref{sec:eval-safety}). We report these residuals and do not claim that the system prevents all unsafe output.

\paragraph{Helpline Referral.} Unsafe and crisis-pattern requests are referred to the national Krishi Call Centre (16123), a government agricultural advisory service. The centre is open from 9:00\,AM to 5:00\,PM, Saturday through Thursday, except on public holidays. The referral screen displays these hours and tells off-hours users to wait for the next shift or seek in-person extension help. The public demonstration runs in a sandbox that shows referral and safety blocking for toxic-chemical queries without giving any chemical advice.

\paragraph{Third-Party Model Use.} The cloud benchmark uses a third-party API (\texttt{google/gemini-2.5-flash-lite}) under commercial enterprise terms with a zero-data-retention agreement, so prompts and responses are not logged, retained, or used for training. Cloud calls were limited to offline benchmark evaluation and never received farmer images or direct personal identifiers. De-identified farmer text queries, with personal identifiers removed as described above, were also sent to this API for the live-model gate comparison in Section~\ref{sec:eval-gate}. Self-hosted deployment runs entirely on the local \texttt{krishokchat-4b} model through \texttt{llama-server} and makes no external network calls.

\paragraph{Human Oversight.} KrishokTech is meant to support extension officers and not to replace them. Unresolved or unsafe cases end in a referral to a human expert instead of a generated answer. All results are scoped to the conditions in which they were measured (Section~\ref{sec:eval-scope}), and simulated or bounded components are disclosed as such and not presented as field-validated.

\subsection{Tri-State Visual Crop Router}
The on-device visual crop classifier uses an empirical tri-state decision rule based on top-1 confidence ($p_1$), margin over top-2 ($p_1 - p_2$), and an out-of-distribution (OOD) rejection threshold:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{State A (Confident Route):} $p_1 \ge 0.90$ and $(p_1 - p_2) \ge 0.20$. Retrieval is immediately fenced to the predicted crop manual.
    \item \textbf{State B (Ambiguous Disambiguation):} $p_1 \ge 0.40$ with confidence $< 0.90$ or margin $< 0.20$. The system presents quick-reply chips for the top-2 candidate crops.
    \item \textbf{State C (Out-of-Distribution):} $p_1 < 0.40$. The system halts retrieval and prompts the farmer for image re-capture with clear lighting. Deployed for the six in-scope crops; other crops fall under this state.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Information-State Extractor (T1)}
The T1 query extractor operates deterministically using domain-specific gazetteers and regex tokenizers without invoking a language model ($<0.1$\,ms execution latency). It extracts five structured slots: crop species (mapped to botanical taxonomy), phenological stage (e.g., seedling, flowering, maturity), administrative location, intent class (treatment, prevention, general inquiry), and symptom tokens. Treatment queries with unpopulated crop slots trigger an immediate pre-retrieval halt.

\subsection{DialectSelector Regional Presets}
To accommodate regional linguistic variation without ungrounded translation, the interface provides six calibrated dialect presets: (1) Standard Bengali (\textit{Promito}), (2) Sylheti, (3) Chittagonian, (4) Noakhailli, (5) Rangpuri, and (6) Barishali. Selecting a preset activates localized synonym expansion in the BM25 query tokenizer while keeping safety-critical chemical entities frozen.

\subsection{Evaluation Units}
We distinguish four evaluation units throughout the study:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Query:} one farmer request evaluated by the advisory pipeline.
    \item \textbf{Case:} one query--context instance evaluated under a specified condition.
    \item \textbf{Mutation case:} one controlled modification of an otherwise valid safety-critical answer.
    \item \textbf{System case:} one case evaluated by one system or configuration.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Pre-Retrieval Gate}
On 200 authentic farmer queries, the deterministic path halted all 30 crop-less symptom queries (halt rate $0.1500$), passing 170 crop-specified queries to fenced search ($0.8500$) with zero false halts ($100\%$ gating accuracy). Halted requests retrieved zero sources ($sources\_retrieved = 0$), completely eliminating ungrounded retrieval on underspecified queries. Downstream crop-slot extraction achieved $95.5\%$ overall agreement ($191/200$), with a $1.5\%$ miss rate ($3/200$) and $3.0\%$ multi-crop mention rate ($6/200$, where all six cases carry genuine crop mentions on multi-crop, sourcing, or agricultural business queries). Ground-truth crop slots were established through independent dual human review, achieving 100.0\% binary agreement (Cohen's $\kappa = 1.0000$) and 99.5\% normalized taxonomy agreement ($\kappa = 0.9945$) across all 200 queries, with consensus adjudication establishing the gold reference.

\subsection{Text Gate Pass-Through Evaluation (No Image Fencing)}
To verify that the text pre-retrieval gate does not introduce false-halt retrieval penalties on valid queries, we evaluated a paired pass-through arm on 400 crop-specified requests (categories B, C, D, I). Because all 400 queries named a valid crop, the text gate passed 100\% of requests without halting ($\text{halt rate}=0.0$) and without applying image-derived fencing. Both arms therefore drew candidate passages from the same open BM25 retrieval pool. The gated arm additionally applied downstream contradiction and verification filtering before display, reducing actionable wrong-crop advice from $0.3025$ ($95\%$ CI: $[25.95, 34.92]$) to $0.2875$ ($95\%$ CI: $[24.53, 33.37]$) ($p=0.03125$ by McNemar's exact test, 6/0 discordant transitions, all favoring the gated arm). Six blind-arm wrong-crop cases resolve under gating with zero reversals, distinct from the multimodal crop-fence experiment (Section~\ref{sec:eval-fence}), where active image-derived crop pre-binding restricts the retrieval scope ($0.3625 \to 0.3000$, $p=5.96\times 10^{-8}$). The descriptive F-spread showed a mean of $3.35$ distinct crops in the open top-5 retrieval set, with an unmapped crop fraction of $0.1352$.

\subsection{Controlled Pilot and PRISM Conformance}
A controlled pilot confirmed gate behavior: 100/100 ambiguous requests halted; 114/140 specified requests passed directly, while 26/140 prompted bounded slot clarification for missing non-crop parameters. Across 1,000 PRISM queries, overall design conformance was $0.478$: clarification 86/100 ($0.86$), concept hypotheses 278/458 ($0.607$), fact base 54/74 ($0.7297$), safety gate 44/100 ($0.44$), document RAG 16/168 ($0.0952$), and conversational follow-up 0/100 ($0.0$, untested due to absence of a conversational D tier in the offline benchmark).

\subsection{Token Metering}
Token measurements were collected separately: clarification required median 89 tokens versus 2,146 tokens for retrieval context. For live grounded rows, median input was 1,111 tokens and median output was 221 tokens. No blended token-saving percentage is claimed.

\subsection{Live--Deterministic Comparison}
On the same 200 queries, an unconstrained live model halted 10\% of the queries, compared with 15\% for the deterministic gate (decision agreement 0.91; median latency 4,218.6\,ms, p95 6,645.1\,ms). An exploratory 14-row answer review yielded 7/14 ($50.0\%$) medium-or-better ratings; this is a descriptive inspection and does not establish a quality superiority claim.

\subsection{Zero-LLM Operation and Modeled Cost}
The evaluated tier mix yielded 7.9\% zero-LLM turns. Turns are bucketed by the pipeline stage at which they terminate: 5.7\% at T0 (deterministic guard), 0.0\% at T1 (guidance card), 2.2\% at T2 (fact lookup), and 92.1\% reaching LLM-backed generation paths. Modeled cost was USD~0.1682 per 1,000 turns versus USD~0.1950 for unconstrained generation with every turn reaching T3 (a $13.7\%$ modeled saving). Cost is modeled from the tier mix, not billed expenditure. Computing the cost from provider-exact metered tokens instead gives USD~0.1720 per 1,000 turns.
