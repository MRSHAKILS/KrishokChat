\begin{abstract}
A farmer who asks which pesticide to spray, without naming the crop, should get a question back before any answer. \textsc{KrishokTech} is a Bengali agricultural advisor that keeps such decisions away from the language model. Before generation, rule-based checks refer high-risk requests to a helpline, halt treatment requests that name no crop, scope retrieval to one crop, and ask for confirmation when a photograph and the text name different crops. After generation, a verifier withholds dosage claims unsupported by the retrieved BARI/BRRI evidence. The demonstration shows each decision as ASK, CONFIRM, REFER, or DROP, with its sources. On 200 real farmer queries, the gate halted all 30 crop-less requests before retrieval and passed the other 170. On 400 stress queries, binding the crop cut top-five lists naming another crop from 36.5\% to 30.25\%, and a crop filter removed the rest (0/400) while every query kept five passages. The mismatch check caught 53 of 54 farmer conflicts and all 400 synthetic ones, halting no matched control, and the dosage verifier caught 114 of 118 inserted errors and accepted all 38 original answers. On the deployed 4B model, the precheck and guarded prompt lowered attack success from 52.5\% to 4.64\%.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/fig1_architecture.jpg}
\caption{KrishokTech places five control stages (T0--T4) before delivery. T0 can end a request in \textbf{REFER}, T1 in \textbf{ASK}, and T2 in \textbf{CONFIRM}. T3 produces an unverified draft; T4 removes unsupported dosage claims before the remaining text is delivered.}
\label{fig:architecture}

Farmers often need treatment advice before an extension officer can visit. Their questions may be short, colloquial, or typed in Latin-script Bangla, and a request such as ``Which pesticide should I spray?'' may omit the crop entirely. In this setting, failure can begin before generation: retrieval may admit guidance for a different crop, while a fluent answer can make an unsupported dose appear actionable. Digital advisory services can narrow the access gap \citep{dae_extension_manual,aker-2011-dial-a,fabregas-etal-2019-digital}, but these unresolved inputs make pesticide advice safety-critical.

Photographs introduce another source of uncertainty. A farmer may attach a rice leaf while asking about potato disease, or the classifier may be unsure which crop it sees. Silently choosing the text or image hides that conflict \citep{Deng_2025_CVPR,zhu2024cross}. Farmer questions also contain colloquial and Romanized forms that differ from the terminology of agricultural manuals \citep{bhuiyan-etal-2025-bhasabodh,our_bengali_retrieval,parikh-solorio-2021-normalization}. Even after relevant evidence is retrieved, generated claims can drift from their context \citep{brown-etal-2025-rag,xu-etal-2025-mega-rag}, and an incorrect numerical dose can be acted on directly \citep{our_agricultural_safety}.

\textsc{KrishokTech} is a Bengali agricultural advisory system built around explicit authorization controls. The generating model drafts an answer; it does not decide whether the request may proceed or whether a dosage claim may be shown. Three control families act around it: (\textbf{C1}) \emph{admission} sends policy-covered high-risk requests to \textbf{REFER} and crop-less treatment requests to \textbf{ASK} before retrieval; (\textbf{C2}) \emph{evidence binding} scopes retrieval to one crop and sends explicit text--image crop conflicts to \textbf{CONFIRM}; and (\textbf{C3}) \emph{release} removes dosage-bearing claims that the retrieved passages and BARI/BRRI ranges do not support. The interface makes each decision visible to the farmer.

Bengali and Banglish normalization runs before admission and evidence binding, allowing colloquial and Latin-script spellings to reach the same controls as formal text. Agricultural guidance remains in an external evidence index, where maintainers can replace passages and rebuild retrieval without retraining the generator. Browser-side vision supports crop prediction on the device, while connected generation serves interactive use and the local generator supports queued SMS or offline kiosk workflows.

Our contribution is the ordered, visible control boundary rather than a new generator: admission, evidence, and release controls around a replaceable model, together with an interface that shows what stopped a request, which evidence was admitted, and why a claim was withheld. We measure each control on bounded test sets. We do not claim complete dialect coverage, general factual verification, or improved outcomes in the field.

The demonstration follows five steps, from a crop-less colloquial query to crop-bound evidence, a text--image conflict, a referral, and dosage-checked delivery (Section~\ref{sec:demonstration}). UI preview: \url{https://krishoktech.vercel.app}; screencast (\textless{}2.5\,min): \url{https://krishoktech.vercel.app/screencast}. Downloadable demonstration package: \url{https://github.com/RaiyaanReza/KrishokChat-Agricultural-Advisory-System}.

\section{Related Work and Positioning}
\label{sec:related_work}

Conversational agricultural systems combine local knowledge with text or voice interfaces to make advisory information easier to access \citep{farmerchat2024,krishokbondhu2026,vijayvargia2025}. Recent Bengali agricultural RAG work also addresses the gap between colloquial questions and formal source documents \citep{hossain2026}. These systems improve access and retrieval, but they generally treat generation as the next step once a query has been processed. KrishokTech instead defines explicit conditions under which the pipeline may continue. A crop-less treatment request, for example, stops before retrieval.

Clarifying questions can improve retrieval when a request is underspecified \citep{aliannejadi-etal-2019-clarifying}, yet models do not reliably decide when clarification is necessary \citep{chen2024act,gan2024clarqllm}. Claim-level checkers compare generated statements with supporting documents \citep{tang2024minicheck,ji2026medragchecker}, while evidence-inspection interfaces help users examine that support \citep{fang-mackenzie-2026-factsearch,murugaraj-etal-2026-ragvue}. Multimodal studies further show that conflicting text and images can produce unreliable model behavior \citep{Deng_2025_CVPR,zhu2024cross}. KrishokTech turns these concerns into visible state transitions: a missing crop produces \textbf{ASK}, an explicit text--image crop conflict produces \textbf{CONFIRM}, and an unsupported dosage claim produces \textbf{DROP}.

The system builds on two of our earlier studies. The KrishokChat benchmark provides Bengali agricultural data, safety tasks, and model resources used by the system \citep{our_agricultural_safety}; a separate study evaluates retrieval architectures over an agricultural corpus \citep{our_bengali_retrieval}. Neither study implements the ordered runtime controls evaluated here. The contribution of this demonstration is the admission, evidence-binding, and release boundary around generation, together with an interface that exposes each decision to the user.

\section{System Design}
\label{sec:system}

KrishokTech implements authorization as an ordered state transition rather than an instruction inside the generation prompt. Five stages, T0--T4, run before delivery. T0 and T1 can end a turn in \textbf{REFER} or \textbf{ASK}; T2 can enter \textbf{CONFIRM}; T3 produces a draft; and T4 can \textbf{DROP} unsupported dosage claims. Only T3 calls the answer generator. Learned components, including the crop classifier, may supply predictions to these stages, but they cannot override a halted state or release a claim rejected by T4.

\subsection{Halting Before Retrieval}
\label{sec:system_gating}

The first decision is whether the request may reach retrieval. At \textbf{T0}, normalization maps supported Bengali, Banglish, and regional variants to canonical terms. A rule-based precheck then detects the high-risk chemical, poisoning, and crisis patterns covered by the system policy. A match ends the turn before retrieval or generation. Agronomic chemical cases are directed to the Krishi Call Centre (\textbf{16123}); poisoning and self-harm cases are directed to the National Emergency Service (\textbf{999}). This precheck enforces a bounded policy and does not attempt to determine the regulatory status of every chemical.

Requests that pass T0 reach \textbf{T1}. A gazetteer-based extractor identifies the crop, symptoms, and request intent without calling a language model. If a treatment request contains no crop, retrieval does not run (\texttt{sources\_retrieved}\,=\,0), and the system enters \textbf{ASK}. The interface shows Bengali crop choices, a non-chemical guidance card, and the referral option. Selecting or typing a crop binds that value and allows the request to continue. Six regional synonym presets extend normalization for supported dialect forms; their current coverage is described in Appendix~\ref{app:architecture}.

\subsection{Crop-Scoped Retrieval and Conflict Handling}
\label{sec:system_retrieval}

At \textbf{T2}, the bound crop determines which evidence retrieval may admit. KrishokTech uses BM25 over 2,135 curated passages from BARI, BRRI, and DAE guidance \citep{bari_handbook2023,brri_handbook2024,dae_extension_manual}. BM25 keeps the retrieval path inspectable and is suitable for this small domain collection \citep{huly-etal-2024-old-ir}. The demonstrated deployment covers rice, potato, brassica, chili, corn, and wheat. Maintainers can replace passages and rebuild the external index without retraining the generator.

For a photographed crop, the browser-side classifier supplies its prediction to the router. A confident prediction binds the crop; an ambiguous prediction presents the top two candidates; and a low-confidence prediction asks the farmer to retake the photograph. The bound crop then conditions query construction and ranking. A final crop filter removes passages that mention only other crops while retaining crop-neutral guidance such as general soil recommendations.

When the crop named in the text differs from a confident image prediction, the router does not select either source silently. It enters \textbf{CONFIRM}, displays a \textbf{Crop Mismatch} warning, and withholds chemical advice until the farmer selects one crop. The selected crop becomes the binding constraint for retrieval.

\subsection{Drafting, Verification, and Delivery}
\label{sec:system_verification}

At \textbf{T3}, the selected generator receives the normalized request and crop-scoped passages. The package supports connected generation and an evaluated local \texttt{krishokchat-4b} deployment; operational measurements for the local path appear in Appendix~\ref{app:deployment}. Regardless of the generator, T3 returns an \emph{unverified draft} rather than a deliverable answer.

At \textbf{T4}, the verifier examines each dosage-bearing numerical claim against the retrieved passage and the permitted BARI/BRRI range. If the available evidence does not support a claim, or its value falls outside the permitted range, the corresponding sentence is removed. The remaining text may then be delivered. This check has a deliberately narrow scope: it verifies dosage-bearing numerical claims and does not establish the correctness of every agronomic statement.

Delivered responses show their source, decision state, and verification path through a provenance badge and a \textit{Why} panel. The same authorized content can be rendered for the web, SMS, an offline card, or read-aloud output. These displays make the system's decision inspectable; citation display alone does not prove that the generator causally relied on a passage \citep{wallat-etal-2025-faithfulness}. Together, the stages establish where a request stopped, which crop constrained its evidence, and which generated claims were permitted to reach the user.

The demonstration is intended for researchers and practitioners building multilingual, multimodal, or safety-sensitive advisory systems. It uses five steps to show when KrishokTech proceeds, pauses, or withholds content. The public website provides a preview of the interface, while the downloadable package contains the implementation. Attendees can follow the prepared sequence and, when a generator is configured, enter their own text and photographs.

\textbf{S1: A missing crop produces ASK.}
The attendee enters \textit{``Patay holud dag hoyeche, ki bish dibo?''} (``The leaves have yellow spots. Which pesticide should I use?''). Because the request asks for treatment without naming a crop, \textbf{T1} stops before retrieval. The interface displays \texttt{sources\_retrieved}\,=\,0, Bengali crop choices, and non-chemical guidance (Figure~\ref{fig:demo_ask}). Selecting \textit{Alu} (potato) binds the crop and resumes the request with potato-scoped retrieval. The step shows that missing information is resolved before evidence is selected.

\textbf{S2: A photograph binds retrieval to a crop.}
In a new consultation, the attendee uploads a rice-leaf photograph without naming the crop. The browser-side classifier predicts rice, and \textbf{T2} binds retrieval to rice guidance. The interface displays the predicted crop and the resulting evidence scope. This step shows how a visual prediction constrains which passages may reach generation.

\textbf{S3: Conflicting modalities produce CONFIRM.}
With the rice photograph attached, the attendee asks about potato late blight. The typed and predicted crops disagree, so \textbf{T2} enters \textbf{CONFIRM}. The interface displays a \textbf{Crop Mismatch} warning and asks the attendee to select rice or potato (Figure~\ref{fig:demo_mismatch}). Chemical advice remains unavailable until one crop is chosen. The conflict is therefore shown to the user rather than resolved silently.

\textbf{S4: A high-risk request produces REFER.}
The attendee asks how to apply paraquat, a request covered by the system's referral policy. The \textbf{T0} precheck ends the turn before retrieval or generation. The interface displays a safety notice and a one-tap link to the Krishi Call Centre (\textbf{16123}; Figure~\ref{fig:demo_refer}). This step makes referral a terminal system state rather than a warning appended to generated advice.

\textbf{S5: An unsupported dosage claim produces DROP.}
The attendee submits a fully specified potato-blight question. In the recorded test case, the draft contains a dosage value ten times above the permitted BARI range. \textbf{T4} removes that sentence and delivers the remaining text. The provenance badge and expandable \textit{Why} panel identify the removed claim and display the passage supporting the retained advice (Figure~\ref{fig:demo_why}). This final step shows that completing generation does not itself authorize every generated claim.

Across the five steps, attendees see the system at its decision boundaries: before retrieval, while evidence is being scoped, when text and images conflict, and after generation. The walkthrough therefore demonstrates the control path itself, not only successful advisory answers.

\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}

We evaluate whether the implementation follows the authorization boundary described in Section~\ref{sec:system}. The tests address three questions: (Q1) Do admission controls halt underspecified or policy-covered requests before retrieval? (Q2) Does crop binding restrict the evidence available to generation and expose text--image conflicts? (Q3) Do the prompt, precheck, and dosage verifier reduce the number of unsafe claims that can reach delivery? Table~\ref{tab:headline} reports the main results; Appendix~\ref{app:evidence} provides confidence intervals and supporting tests.

\subsection{Q1: Admission and Referral}
\label{sec:eval-gate}

We test the T1 crop gate on 200 collected Bengali farmer treatment questions. Two annotators independently labeled whether each question named a crop and agreed on all 200 cases (Cohen's $\kappa=1.00$). At T1, all 30 crop-less questions entered \textbf{ASK} before retrieval, while all 170 crop-specified questions were permitted to continue. This result measures the crop-presence decision at T1 rather than the outcome of later controls.

We separately test whether normalization helps T0 recognize colloquial and Latin-script forms. The constructed set contains 85 high-risk requests with Banglish, phonetic, or indirect wording and 15 benign farmer questions. A precheck limited to formal English and Bengali script intercepted 1 of the 85 high-risk requests. After normalization, T0 intercepted all 85 and flagged none of the 15 benign controls. The examples include forms such as \textit{parakwat} for paraquat; category-level results appear in Appendix~\ref{app:architecture}.

\subsection{Q2: Evidence Binding and Conflict Handling}
\label{sec:eval-fence}

We evaluate crop binding on 400 stress queries containing colloquial, dialectal, Banglish, or ambiguous disease descriptions. Each query has an adjudicated crop. A retrieved list counts as cross-crop when at least one of its top five passages names another crop but not the adjudicated crop. This is a property of the retrieved evidence, not a judgment of the final answer.

Open retrieval produced cross-crop lists for 146 of 400 queries (36.5\%). Binding the adjudicated crop, which simulates correct crop routing, reduced this to 121 of 400 (30.25\%): 25 queries improved and none became worse (exact McNemar $p=6\times10^{-8}$). The post-retrieval crop filter removed all remaining violations recognized by its alias list, while all 400 queries retained five passages. Because this zero is defined by the filter's own aliases, we also inspected 30 lists by title. Twenty-six contained no other crop, while four contained crop names outside the alias list: carrot, sweet potato, or forest trees. The independent audit therefore bounds what the 0/400 result establishes.

We test the \textbf{CONFIRM} transition with 54 farmer-derived text--image conflicts and 400 synthetic conflicts. Each case has a matched control containing the same query and an image of the named crop. The mismatch state fired on 53 of 54 farmer-derived cases and all 400 synthetic cases, while firing on none of the 454 matched controls. The single miss referred to the crop only implicitly.

\subsection{Q3: Release Checks on Generated Advice}
\label{sec:eval-safety}

We first test the generator boundary using 280 fixed attack requests. With T0 and T4 disabled, each request was run once through the evaluated local \texttt{krishokchat-4b} deployment under a neutral prompt and once under the guarded prompt. A fixed rule-based judge marked 147 of 280 neutral responses (52.5\%) and 30 of 280 guarded responses (10.71\%) as successful attacks. Replaying the requests through T0, without making new model calls, shows that 17 of those 30 requests would have terminated before generation. This leaves 13 successful attacks among the 280 requests (4.64\%) eligible to continue. The result demonstrates how the precheck complements the prompt for this model and test set; it does not establish complete generator independence.

We then test T4 using 38 previously verified answers. We inserted 118 controlled dosage errors while retaining the 38 unmodified answers as clean controls. The verifier removed 114 of the 118 inserted errors and rejected none of the clean originals. The four missed errors and results from the larger passage-derived benchmark are reported in Appendix~\ref{app:evidence}. This test concerns dosage-bearing numerical claims only; it does not evaluate every factual statement in an answer.

\subsection{Scope of the Evidence}
\label{sec:eval-scope}

These results are component and property-conformance tests on the stated samples. They show that the evaluated implementation usually follows its admission, evidence-binding, conflict, and dosage-release rules. They do not establish improved yield, correct farmer decisions, or complete safety in deployment. The crop-binding experiment assumes a correct crop label; the normalization attacks are constructed; 400 of the 454 conflict cases are synthetic; each generator prompt was run once; and the dosage errors were inserted into existing answers. These limits define the claims carried into the conclusion.

\section{Limitations, Availability, and Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\label{sec:limitations}
KrishokTech currently supports six crop groups and a bounded Bengali agricultural evidence collection. Adding a crop requires new evidence, aliases, routing rules, and a compatible vision classifier. The dialect presets expand known synonyms but have not been evaluated as general dialect-understanding models. Text--image conflict detection depends on an explicit crop mention or a confident image prediction, and the crop filter cannot reject crop names absent from its alias list.

The controls also have deliberately narrow scopes. T0 recognizes policy-covered patterns rather than every possible high-risk paraphrase, while T4 checks dosage-bearing numerical claims rather than all agronomic statements. Thirteen of 280 attacks remained eligible to continue after the guarded prompt and T0 replay, and the dosage verifier missed 4 of 118 inserted errors. These residual cases prevent a claim of complete safety.

The evaluation measures component behavior on bounded datasets. Several tests use constructed attacks or synthetic conflicts, and the crop-binding experiment assumes that the crop was identified correctly. We have not measured effects on farmer decisions, crop outcomes, or extension-service workload. Local generation is also slower than connected generation and is better suited to queued SMS or kiosk use. Browser-side classification and local retrieval reduce connectivity requirements, but they do not show that the complete pipeline runs interactively on every low-end phone.

\paragraph{Availability and Licensing.}
\label{sec:availability}
The public interface preview is available at \url{https://krishoktech.vercel.app}, and the demonstration video is at \url{https://krishoktech.vercel.app/screencast}. The downloadable implementation, installation instructions, deterministic controls, and tests are released at \url{https://github.com/RaiyaanReza/KrishokChat-Agricultural-Advisory-System}. The package can use a configured online generator or a compatible local runtime; continuous access to a paid hosted backend is not required to inspect the code or control logic.

Model artifacts, including the browser-side vision classifiers and the generator's LoRA adapter for its Gemma~4 E4B base, are released at \url{https://huggingface.co/RaiyanKhaan/KrishokTech-Models}. The MIT license in the GitHub repository applies to the application code. Model files remain subject to the licenses and base-model terms stated on their respective model cards. BARI, BRRI, and DAE passages retain their original source attribution and are not relicensed by the software license.

\paragraph{Conclusion.}
\label{sec:final_conclusion}
KrishokTech places an explicit authorization boundary around a replaceable answer generator: deterministic controls decide whether retrieval may begin, which crop constrains the evidence, whether conflicting modalities require confirmation, and whether a dosage-bearing claim may be delivered. On the evaluated test sets, these controls halted all crop-less treatment requests, exposed 453 of 454 text--image conflicts, and removed 114 of 118 inserted dosage errors. The demonstration makes these decisions visible while keeping the claims bounded to component behavior; KrishokTech is designed to support farmers and extension services, and field evaluation remains future work.

\section*{Ethics and Broader Impact Statement}
\label{sec:ethics}

Agricultural advice can affect health, livelihoods, and crop production. A fluent but incorrect chemical recommendation may be acted on immediately, especially when access to an extension officer is limited. KrishokTech therefore exposes its control decisions and routes selected requests to human services, but these measures do not remove the risks of automated advice.

\paragraph{Farmer Query Data.}
The T1 evaluation uses 200 Bengali questions: 100 collected from public Bangladeshi farming groups on Facebook, 60 submitted to KrishokChat, and 40 taken from the public question archive of \texttt{krishibangla.com}. We retained the question text needed for analysis and removed names, phone numbers, locations, and account information. No demographic attributes were inferred. Two annotators independently labeled crop presence and normalized crop identity; the paper reports aggregate results and agreement statistics. Publicly posted text may remain sensitive after direct identifiers are removed, so quotations and released records should be checked for indirect identification.

\paragraph{Agricultural Images and Privacy.}
The demonstrated browser classifier is designed to process crop photographs locally through ONNX and WebAssembly. The privacy claim applies to this browser-side path; any deployment that enables a server fallback, remote logging, or image telemetry must disclose that transmission and apply appropriate retention and access controls. Evaluation images come from previously released agricultural datasets. Their source attribution and license conditions are retained in the artifact metadata, and we do not claim ownership of those images.

\paragraph{Chemical and Dosage Guidance.}
T0 stops the high-risk chemical, poisoning, and crisis patterns covered by its policy before retrieval or generation. T4 checks dosage-bearing numerical claims against retrieved passages and permitted dose ranges. Both controls remain incomplete: unseen wording can pass T0, 13 of 280 tested attacks remained eligible to continue after the guarded prompt and T0 replay, and the verifier missed 4 of 118 inserted dosage errors. KrishokTech should therefore not be used as the sole authority for chemical application or medical decisions.

\paragraph{Referral Information.}
Agronomic chemical cases covered by the policy are directed to the Krishi Call Centre (\textbf{16123}). Poisoning, self-harm, and medical-crisis language is directed to the National Emergency Service (\textbf{999}) and appropriate medical care. The interface presents these routes immediately, but it cannot guarantee service availability or that a user will reach help. Helpline details and operating conditions may change and should be checked against current official information before deployment.

\paragraph{Third-Party Services.}
When connected generation is enabled, the text required for a request is transmitted to the configured model provider and is governed by that provider's retention and processing terms. Deployers must disclose the provider and avoid sending unnecessary personal information. The public website is an interface preview and need not keep a paid generation service active. The downloadable package can instead be configured with a compatible local runtime, subject to the local model's license and hardware requirements.

\paragraph{Human Oversight and Broader Impact.}
KrishokTech is intended to help farmers and extension workers inspect an advisory path, not to replace agronomists, medical professionals, or emergency services. \textbf{ASK} and \textbf{CONFIRM} return unresolved decisions to the user, while \textbf{REFER} directs policy-covered cases to a human service. Visible evidence and provenance may improve scrutiny, but they may also create false confidence if users interpret a citation as proof that every statement is correct. The present evaluation measures system behavior rather than effects on farmers, and deployment should be preceded by participatory evaluation with intended users and agricultural experts.

\section{Architecture, Decision Logic, and Gate Evaluation}
\label{app:architecture}
\label{app:gate}

This section specifies the state transitions behind T0--T4 and provides supporting results for the pre-retrieval controls. The released implementation includes the T0 rules and chemical registry in \url{backend/app/domain/safety_policy.py} and \url{backend/app/domain/chemical_registry.py}.

\paragraph{State transitions.}
\textbf{REFER} is terminal for the current turn: T0 returns the appropriate referral without retrieval or generation, and a later message begins again at T0. \textbf{ASK} ends the current turn with a request for the missing crop; selecting a chip or typing a crop binds that value and allows the request to continue to T2. \textbf{CONFIRM} holds chemical advice when the typed and predicted crops conflict; the farmer's selection becomes the bound crop. \textbf{DROP} applies at claim level after generation: T4 removes an unsupported dosage-bearing sentence and delivers any remaining authorized content.

\paragraph{Information-state extractor (T1).}
The extractor uses gazetteers and pattern rules, without a language-model call, to fill five fields: crop, growth stage, location, intent, and symptom terms. Its internal taxonomy contains 45 normalized crop labels. Thirty-seven of these labels occurred in the 200-query evaluation set, while the demonstrated crop-specific retrieval and vision path covers six crop groups.

For the 200 farmer questions, the extractor matched the adjudicated normalized crop label on 95.5\% of cases. The remaining errors concerned crop identity rather than crop presence: the T1 presence decision still separated all 30 crop-less questions from the 170 crop-specified questions. Annotator agreement was 100\% for crop presence ($\kappa=1.00$), 99.5\% for normalized crop identity ($\kappa=0.99$), and 93.5\% for intent ($\kappa=0.90$).

A separate constructed pilot contained 100 crop-less and 140 crop-specified treatment requests. T1 halted all 100 crop-less requests. All 140 crop-specified requests avoided the missing-crop halt; 114 continued directly, while 26 requested another bounded field such as growth stage or location.

\paragraph{Vision crop router (T2).}
Let $p_1$ and $p_2$ be the highest and second-highest crop probabilities. If $p_1\geq0.90$ and $p_1-p_2\geq0.20$, the router binds the top prediction. If $p_1\geq0.40$ but either confidence condition fails, the interface presents the top two crops for confirmation. If $p_1<0.40$, retrieval remains halted and the interface asks for another photograph. These thresholds govern the demonstrated router and are not calibrated estimates of correctness for crops outside the evaluated data.

\paragraph{Dialect presets.}
The \textit{DialectSelector} offers Standard Bengali, Sylheti, Chittagonian, Noakhali, Rangpuri, and Barishali presets. A preset adds known regional synonyms during normalization while leaving recognized chemical names unchanged. These are lookup-based interface features; we have not measured general understanding or accuracy for any dialect.

\paragraph{Provenance badges.}
Each rendered result carries one interface badge: \emph{Fact lookup} for an exact match in the nine-fact base without generation; \emph{Grounded generation} for a generated draft processed by T4; \emph{Guidance card} for non-chemical information shown during ASK; \emph{Clarification} for crop selection or a mismatch; or \emph{Referral} for T0 routing. The \emph{Grounded generation} badge records that the dosage check ran. It does not mean that every factual statement was independently verified.

\section{Delivery, Offline Operation, and Deployment}
\label{app:deployment}
\label{app:delivery}

This section reports implementation measurements that clarify how an authorized result is rendered and operated. These measurements are supplementary: they do not establish usability on farmers' devices or performance under real rural network conditions.

\subsection{SMS Rendering}
Bengali-script SMS uses UCS-2 encoding, which permits 70 characters in one message and 67 characters per part in a concatenated message. A 160-character Bengali advisory therefore occupies two or three SMS segments rather than one. Across separate offline and connected test sets, the deterministic packer kept 399 of 400 outputs within its configured 160-character limit. All eight referral cases contained only the referral message.

Table~\ref{tab:n05c} compares four rendering methods on the same 100 long, dosage-bearing advisories. The current compressor copies detected values from the verified answer and does not generate new quantities. It improved preservation of both the dose and chemical name from 14/100 under naive truncation to 61/100. This remains insufficient for treating the compact SMS as semantically equivalent to the full answer. The SMS view is therefore a constrained rendering whose source and full verified response should remain available when exact chemical details matter.

\subsection{Offline Retrieval}
The local BM25 path obtained a hit rate of 0.94 on the 400-query retrieval benchmark. With service-worker precaching enabled, the test recorded no cache miss in 400 requests. Packet loss was simulated rather than measured in a field deployment. This result applies to retrieval; offline generation additionally requires a compatible local model and sufficient hardware.

\subsection{Updating the Evidence Index}
Agricultural passages are stored outside the generator. As an implementation check, we removed and reinserted 10 guideline passages selected from the top-ranked results of the stress queries. Rebuilding the index took a median of 0.2\,s on the evaluation laptop, and the rebuilt full index reproduced the deployed top five for all 400 queries. After reinsertion, each of the 10 selected passages again appeared at rank 1 for its original query.

This check confirms that passages can be replaced and the index rebuilt without loading or modifying the generator. It does not measure whether newly added guidance is correct, sufficiently complete, or retrieved well for a new topic. Adding a crop still requires supporting evidence, aliases, routing rules, and any corresponding vision component.
