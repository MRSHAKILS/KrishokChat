\begin{abstract}
Safety-critical agricultural advice creates a deployment tension: farmers need useful answers in low-resource settings, but a fluent language model should not decide whether uncertain or actionable advice is safe to release. \textsc{KrishokTech} therefore treats the language model as a bounded generator rather than an authority. Bengali and Banglish normalization, pre-retrieval safety controls, crop-fenced evidence, multimodal confirmation, unverified draft generation, and dosage release checks form an ordered boundary around generation. On 200 authentic farmer queries, deterministic controls halted all 30 crop-less requests before retrieval. Crop fencing with contradiction gating reduced off-target retrieval from 36.25\% to 28.75\% on a 400-query stress set, while the mismatch detector caught 453 of 454 explicit contradictions. On the deployed local model, guarded attack success fell from 52.5\% to 10.71\%. The live dosage verifier caught 114 of 118 inserted errors without rejecting any unmodified response.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/fig1_architecture.jpg}
\caption{KrishokTech uses five control stages (T0--T4), followed by delivery. T0 can end a request in \textbf{REFER}, T1 in \textbf{ASK}, T2 in \textbf{CONFIRM}, and T4 in \textbf{DROP}; T3 produces an unverified draft that must pass T4 before delivery.}
\label{fig:architecture}
\end{figure*}

When yellow spots appear on potato leaves, a farmer often needs advice before an extension officer can visit. Digital advisory services can narrow this access gap \citep{dae_extension_manual,dlec_bangladesh,aker-2011-dial-a,fabregas-etal-2019-digital,campenhout-etal-2021-ict}, but agricultural treatment advice is safety-critical: an answer for the wrong crop or an unsupported dosage can turn an incomplete question into a harmful recommendation.

A farmer may ask ``which pesticide should I spray?'' without naming the crop. The uncertainty therefore begins before generation: retrieval may search unrelated guidance, and a plausible dosage may be drawn from the wrong evidence. Farmer queries may use colloquial or Romanized Bangla, creating a mismatch with the formal terminology used in agricultural resources \citep{bhuiyan-etal-2025-bhasabodh,our_bengali_retrieval,parikh-solorio-2021-normalization}. Photographs and text can disagree, and silently choosing one modality leaves the conflict unresolved \citep{Deng_2025_CVPR,zhu2024cross}. Even with relevant evidence, generated claims may not remain supported by their context \citep{brown-etal-2025-rag,xu-etal-2025-mega-rag}; a plausible numerical dosage is directly actionable \citep{our_agricultural_safety}.

\textsc{KrishokTech} makes these decisions explicit as four farmer-visible states (Figure~\ref{fig:architecture}): \textbf{ASK}, \textbf{CONFIRM}, \textbf{REFER}, and \textbf{DROP}. The system asks how much authority the model needs, rather than assuming that a stronger model removes the need for an independent boundary. This question is especially important at the edge, where offline operation and local privacy motivate a small generator, while changing regional guidance requires an update surface outside the model. The system therefore combines three controls: (\textbf{C1}) a deterministic gate halts underspecified treatment queries before retrieval; (\textbf{C2}) crop identification fences evidence and raises confirmation when text and image disagree; and (\textbf{C3}) dosage-bearing claims are checked against retrieved passages and BARI/BRRI dose ranges before rendering.

The model is deliberately not the authority for access, evidence, or release. A general-purpose model cannot be assumed to know which chemicals and dose ranges current BARI, BRRI, and DAE guidance permits for a crop in Bangladesh. KrishokTech instead provides an independently updateable evidence layer; adding curated guidance changes the retrieval surface without inherently retraining the generator, although retrieval quality still limits coverage. Bengali and Banglish normalization is part of this safety boundary: it makes colloquial and Latin-script variants available to the same deterministic checks rather than leaving their interpretation entirely to the model.

Our contributions are threefold: (1) deterministic-first safety control that makes asking, confirming, referring, and dropping explicit pipeline states; (2) knowledge-centric edge deployment that separates a local bounded generator from independently updateable agricultural evidence; and (3) Bengali/Banglish-aware control that places normalization before retrieval and safety decisions. The paper reports measured behavior for these controls without claiming that dialect understanding is solved or that the system has established field effectiveness.

The demonstration traces one continuous farmer session through these states, from a colloquial crop-less query to crop-scoped evidence, multimodal conflict, safety referral, and answer delivery after dosage verification (Section~\ref{sec:demonstration}). Interactive demo: \url{https://krishoktech-one.vercel.app}; screencast (\textless{}2.5\,min): \url{https://krishoktech-one.vercel.app/screencast}. Source code and installable package are available under Apache-2.0 at \url{https://github.com/RaiyaanReza/KrishokChat-Agricultural-Advisory-System}.

\section{Related Work and Positioning}
\label{sec:related_work}



Digital agricultural advisors such as Farmer.Chat, KrishokBondhu, and Krishi Sathi have paired localized agricultural knowledge with conversational interfaces to improve access to timely advice \citep{farmerchat2024,krishokbondhu2026,vijayvargia2025}. Bengali agricultural RAG systems have also explored matching farmer queries to more formal scientific knowledge \citep{hossain2026}. This work establishes localized retrieval and multilingual interaction as practical building blocks. KrishokTech instead focuses on when the system should be allowed to proceed: a crop-less treatment request is treated as an unresolved safety condition rather than an incomplete conversational turn.

Classic information-seeking research shows that asking a clarifying question improves retrieval when a request is underspecified \citep{aliannejadi-etal-2019-clarifying}. LLM-based agents can instead guess or hedge under ambiguity \citep{chen2024act,gan2024clarqllm}. Claim-level checking and inspectable evidence provide complementary safeguards for grounded generation \citep{tang2024minicheck,ji2026medragchecker,nguyen-etal-2025-climate,fang-mackenzie-2026-factsearch,murugaraj-etal-2026-ragvue}. These findings motivate KrishokTech's separation of evidence access and claim release from the generator.

The multimodal setting adds a second boundary problem: vision-language models may rely on misleading text when sources conflict \citep{Deng_2025_CVPR,zhu2024cross}. Bengali deployment adds regional dialect and Latin-script Banglish variation, which can make formal-text gates brittle \citep{bhuiyan-etal-2025-bhasabodh,bangla_low_resource}. KrishokTech keeps these uncertainties observable through crop confirmation and script-agnostic deterministic checks; it does not claim to resolve dialect variation in general.

\section{System Design}
\label{sec:system}

KrishokTech implements these four states through five ordered stages, T0--T4, followed by delivery. Each stage constrains the next: T0/T1 can end the turn in \textbf{REFER}/\textbf{ASK}, T2 in \textbf{CONFIRM}, and T4 filters the T3 draft before delivery. Only T3 invokes the generating language model; T0, T1, T2, and T4 make their decisions independently of it.

The central design principle is a separation between generation and authorization. T3 may propose language, but it cannot authorize retrieval, resolve conflicting crop evidence, or release a dosage claim. Those permissions belong to deterministic stages that operate before and after generation, so a more capable generator changes the draft's quality rather than the system's authority boundary.

\subsection{Halting Before Retrieval}
\label{sec:system_gating}

The first decision is whether a request should reach retrieval at all.

At \textbf{T0}, a deterministic precheck examines incoming Bengali and Banglish text for high-risk chemical requests, poisoning-related language, and crisis patterns covered by the system's safety policy. A matched request is stopped before retrieval or generation: agronomic chemical inquiries route to the Krishi Call Centre (\textbf{16123}), while acute poisoning and self-harm language escalate directly to the 24/7 National Emergency Service (\textbf{999}), without invoking a language model. The exact matching patterns are withheld to prevent evasion; measured safety results are in Appendix~\ref{app:evidence}. The boundary follows the system’s safety policy; it does not attempt to reproduce the full regulatory status of every chemical.

Requests that pass T0 reach \textbf{T1}. Here, a deterministic information-state extractor identifies the crop, symptom, and treatment intent without invoking a language model. When a treatment request contains no resolvable crop, the crop is treated as a safety-critical missing slot. The pipeline halts before BM25 retrieval runs ($sources\_retrieved = 0$) and enters \textbf{ASK}. The interface can then request the crop through Bengali quick-reply chips, provide a non-chemical guidance card, or offer referral to the helpline. A \textit{DialectSelector} can apply one of six regional synonym presets during query normalization to accommodate colloquial variation \citep{our_bengali_retrieval}.

\subsection{Crop-Fenced Retrieval and the Mismatch Badge}
\label{sec:system_retrieval}

Requests that pass the information gate enter \textbf{T2}, where crop identity determines what evidence retrieval is allowed to access. KrishokTech uses BM25 over a curated index of BARI, BRRI, and DAE agricultural guidelines \citep{bari_handbook2023,brri_handbook2024,dae_extension_manual}. We use BM25 as a transparent lexical baseline over a small, curated, domain-specific collection; sparse retrieval remains a defensible RAG choice even when dense retrievers are available \citep{huly-etal-2024-old-ir}. The current deployment covers six staple crops: rice, potato, brassica, chili, corn, and wheat.

When a farmer attaches a photograph, an on-device vision model identifies the crop before retrieval proceeds. The router has three outcomes: a confident prediction routes the request to the corresponding crop-specific evidence, an ambiguous prediction presents the top candidates for disambiguation, and an out-of-scope image asks the farmer to recapture the photograph. The exact confidence and margin thresholds are given in Appendix~\ref{app:architecture}.

Once a crop is bound, it becomes a hard retrieval fence. Passages associated with other crops are excluded from the candidate evidence set rather than simply receiving a lower ranking. This prevents evidence for an unrelated crop from reaching generation. If the farmer's text names a different crop from the attached photograph, the system does not silently choose between them. Instead, it raises a \textbf{Crop Mismatch} warning under the \textbf{Clarification} badge and enters \textbf{CONFIRM}, withholding chemical advice until the farmer confirms the intended crop. For conflicting diagnoses within a crop, the system asks a discriminative question rather than combining incompatible treatments into a single response.

\subsection{Untrusted Generation, Verification, and Delivery}
\label{sec:system_verification}

Once the request has passed T0--T2, \textbf{T3} generates a response from the crop-scoped evidence. The deployed edge system uses \texttt{krishokchat-4b}, a 4-bit quantized Gemma-based model fine-tuned with a LoRA adapter and served locally through \texttt{llama-server} on commodity CPU for offline operation. Four-bit quantization is an established way to reduce the memory burden of on-device LLM inference \citep{lin-etal-2024-awq}. A 4B model requires $\sim$24\,s per call on an unaccelerated consumer CPU, so the deployment targets asynchronous SMS dispatch and offline queuing rather than instantaneous interaction. For connected live web sessions, the cloud model (\texttt{gemini-2.5-flash-lite}) generates answers in 1.8\,s.

In both cases, the generated response is treated as an \emph{unverified draft}, not as the final answer. Because T4 checks the draft against evidence regardless of its source, this check runs unconditionally whether T3 uses the local or the cloud model, even though residual risk still differs by model (Section~\ref{sec:eval-safety}).

At \textbf{T4}, KrishokTech checks numerical claims that carry dosage information against the retrieved evidence and the permitted BARI/BRRI dose range. A claim is withheld when it cannot be grounded in the retrieved passage or falls outside the permitted range; the unsupported claim is \textbf{dropped} rather than rendered. This verifier is intentionally narrow: it checks dosage-bearing numerical claims and does not establish the factual correctness of every agronomic statement in the response. The verification breakdown is summarized in the evidence ledger (Appendix~\ref{app:evidence}).

Delivered outputs carry a provenance badge and Why-panel trace and can be rendered as web, SMS, offline card, or read-aloud. The badge exposes the retrieved source and verification path; it does not by itself establish that every citation was causally used by the generator \citep{wallat-etal-2025-faithfulness}.

The demonstration is intended for researchers and practitioners building multilingual, multimodal, or safety-critical conversational systems. Attendees follow one continuous farmer session on the live system in five steps, S1--S5, in the same order as the screencast. Together, the steps expose the four control states introduced in Section~\ref{sec:introduction}: \textbf{ASK} (S1), \textbf{CONFIRM} (S3), \textbf{REFER} (S4), and \textbf{DROP} (S5). S2 establishes the visual crop context used by S3. Attendees can begin with pre-loaded regional-dialect and safety-test queries or enter their own text and photographs.

\textbf{S1: Missing crop (ASK).}
The farmer submits a colloquial treatment query without naming the crop: \textit{``Patay holud dag hoyeche, ki bish dibo?''} (``Yellow spots on leaves. What pesticide to use?''). Because the query lacks a crop, the \textbf{T1} gate halts retrieval ($sources\_retrieved = 0$) and renders quick-reply crop chips (Figure~\ref{fig:demo_ask}). Selecting \textit{Alu} (potato) binds the crop and initiates potato-scoped retrieval with numbered citations.

\textbf{S2: Photograph scopes evidence.}
The farmer uploads a leaf photo. An on-device vision classifier identifies rice, scoping the session to rice guidance. The image remains local and restricts candidate evidence at the \textbf{T2} fence.

\textbf{S3: Text--image conflict (CONFIRM).}
With the rice image attached, the farmer queries potato late blight. Rather than guessing, the system raises a \textbf{Clarification} badge and withholds chemical advice until confirmed (Figure~\ref{fig:demo_mismatch}).

\textbf{S4: High-risk chemical request (REFER).}
The farmer asks how to apply paraquat, which is blocked by KrishokTech's safety policy. The \textbf{T0} precheck blocks the turn before retrieval or generation, displaying a safety alert and a 1-click route to the Krishi Call Centre (\textbf{16123}).

\textbf{S5: Dosage verification (DROP).}
The farmer requests fungicide for potato blight. At \textbf{T4}, claims are checked against retrieved BARI/BRRI evidence and dose bands. One unsupported dosage statement is \textbf{dropped}, and the verified residue is rendered with citations, provenance badge, and an expandable \textit{Why} audit panel (Figure~\ref{fig:demo_why}; see Appendix~\ref{app:ui} for alternate delivery channels).

\subsection{Q1: Halting and Redirecting Unsafe or Underspecified Requests}
\label{sec:eval-gate}

We test the gate on 200 authentic Bengali farmer treatment queries, 30 of which name no crop. Two annotators labeled the crop slot independently and agreed on every crop-presence decision (Cohen's $\kappa = 1.00$). The gate halted all 30 crop-less queries before retrieval and passed the other 170 to fenced search without a false halt.

An unconstrained LLM asked to make the same decision halted 10\% of the 200 queries, compared with 15\% for the deterministic gate, agreed with the gate on 91\% of decisions, and took a median of 4.2\,s per query. On this set, the deterministic slot check matched the annotators on every query without calling a model. Slot-extraction accuracy, the controlled pilot, and token metering are in Appendix~\ref{app:gate}.

\enlargethispage{2\baselineskip}
\subsection{Q2: Preventing Wrong Evidence from Reaching Generation}
\label{sec:eval-fence}

The six on-device disease classifiers reach 0.94 to 0.99 top-1 accuracy on 4,294 held-out test images. To test the fence, we use a 400-query stress set of dialectal, ambiguous, and conflicting queries selected to stress retrieval under these conditions. A query is counted as yielding wrong-crop advice when its top five sources include a document for another crop. Open retrieval does so for 36.25\% of the queries. Fencing retrieval to the crop predicted by the on-device vision model, at its empirical error rate of about 5\%, lowers this to 31.25\%. Adding contradiction gating, which halts conflicting queries before retrieval, lowers it further to 28.75\% (a text-only ablation without image fencing, reported in Appendix~\ref{app:evidence}, reaches a similar 28.75\% endpoint through a distinct mechanism).

The mismatch badge caught 453 of 454 explicit text-image contradictions (53 of 54 farmer cases and all 400 cases in PRISM, our synthetic multimodal-conflict suite) with no false halts. The one miss used an implicit crop reference. On a separate set of 300 conflict queries, a prompted LLM asked the farmer to clarify in only 20\% of cases, and naive multimodal RAG in 13\%. Per-crop accuracy, quantization, and the full fence comparison are in Appendix~\ref{app:evidence}.

\subsection{Q3: Independently Checking Dosage Release}
\label{sec:eval-safety}

We evaluate attack success rate (ASR), the share of adversarial prompts that yield an unsafe response, across both guarded and unguarded pipelines. On the locally deployed edge model (\texttt{krishokchat-4b}, 280 calls per arm), which serves offline and privacy-sensitive deployment, the guarded pipeline reduces ASR from 52.5\% to 10.71\% overall, and from 77.0\% to 19.0\% on the Bengali-native subset of 100 attacks. Residual failures are concentrated in embedded (4 of 10) and roleplay (2 of 10) variants, reflecting the operational bounds of a 4-bit local model. For comparison, on the cloud benchmark model (\texttt{gemini-2.5-flash-lite}, 420 calls), the guarded pipeline lowers ASR to 0.95\% against 36.19\% unguarded (10.0\% vs.\ 83.0\% on Bengali-native). Latin-script Banglish and phonetic spellings such as \textit{parakwat} also evade keyword gates written for formal text: a formal-only gate intercepted 1 of 85 adversarial queries in this style, whereas our deterministic precheck intercepted all 85 with zero false alarms on 15 benign farmer questions.

We then assess the generated advice itself. In a blinded audit, three evaluators (two agronomy or botany undergraduates and one extension officer) rated 46 of 47 guarded outputs as safe and actionable, one as vague but harmless, and none as dangerous (Fleiss' $\kappa = 0.82$). All three unguarded control outputs were rated dangerous. For the dosage verifier, we inserted controlled errors into verified answers. On 38 live answers with 118 inserted errors, it caught 114 and rejected none of the unmodified answers. On a scaled benchmark of 528 cases from 88 BARI and BRRI passages, it caught 296 of 350 inserted errors (84.6\%): every overdose (76 of 76) and underdose (67 of 67), but only 85\% of chemical substitutions, 73\% of unit substitutions, and 65\% of invented pre-harvest intervals.

Constrained delivery preserves the dose: the SMS packer kept 399 of 400 messages within 160 characters and preserved the dose in 92 of 100 long advisories without invention; full SMS, offline, and latency detail is in Appendix~\ref{app:delivery} and the evidence ledger (Appendix~\ref{app:evidence}).

\subsection{What This Evaluation Does Not Establish}
\label{sec:eval-scope}

Results hold only under measured benchmark conditions without longitudinal field trials on yield. Crop-fence tests use paired queries under empirical vision noise; 400 of 454 contradiction cases are synthetic; multi-turn evaluations reflect constructed state-transition stress suites rather than longitudinal farmer conversations. Expert audits cover 47 guarded outputs, network resilience reflects simulated packet loss, and dosage checks target numerical bounds rather than general agronomy. Appendix~\ref{app:evidence} ledgers every result by evidence type.

\section{Limitations, Availability, and Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\label{sec:limitations}
Four limitations qualify the three questions (see Appendix~\ref{app:evidence} for the ledger): (1)~coverage is limited to six staple crops and Bengali input, with lexical BM25 retrieval; extending coverage is primarily a knowledge-curation and retrieval-maintenance task and does not inherently require retraining the generator, but retrieval quality and coverage remain limits; (2)~the mismatch badge detects explicit crop conflicts only, and it missed the one implicit test reference; (3)~safety controls reduce but do not eliminate attack success (10.71\% guarded deployed vs.\ 0.95\% cloud); and (4)~SMS compression is lossy, while the 4B model takes $\sim$24\,s per call on commodity CPU without GPU acceleration, targeting asynchronous SMS dispatch and offline village kiosks rather than instantaneous live web chat.

\paragraph{Availability and Licensing.}
\label{sec:availability}
Code, models, and container scripts are licensed under Apache-2.0 at \url{https://github.com/RaiyaanReza/KrishokChat-Agricultural-Advisory-System}, with models available at \url{https://huggingface.co/RaiyanKhaan/KrishokTech-Models}. The model \texttt{krishokchat-4b} and 2,135 extension nodes are on HuggingFace under CC-BY-4.0 (BARI, BRRI, and DAE guidelines). While exact T0 regex dictionaries are withheld from the public release to prevent dual-use evasion, they are available to qualified academic researchers and safety auditors under a non-commercial research agreement upon request.

\paragraph{Conclusion.}
\label{sec:final_conclusion}
KrishokTech assigns authority outside the generator. The model drafts, while deterministic controls decide access, confirmation, referral, and dosage release. Bengali/Banglish normalization makes those controls applicable before retrieval; the external BARI/BRRI/DAE knowledge layer can be updated independently of the generator, although its retrieval quality bounds the result. The measured controls reduce but do not eliminate residual failures, and the system is intended to support rather than replace extension officers. No claim of field effectiveness is made; evaluation with farmers remains to be done.

\section*{Ethics and Broader Impact Statement}
\label{sec:ethics}

Agricultural advice for smallholder farmers affects food security, livelihoods, and physical safety. Incorrect chemical or dosage guidance is the central risk this paper is designed around.

\paragraph{Farmer Query Data.} The 200 farmer queries in the gate evaluation (Section~\ref{sec:eval-gate}) come from extension-service interaction logs. Names, phone numbers, and plot locations were removed before analysis. The queries were collected in collaboration with regional extension officers under institutional review at North South University (Institutional Review Board / Research Ethics Committee), and informed consent was obtained for non-commercial research and safety evaluation. Two annotators labeled the crop slot independently, and consensus adjudication set the final labels. Agreement was 100.0\% for crop presence (Cohen's $\kappa = 1.0000$), 99.5\% for the normalized 37-crop taxonomy ($\kappa = 0.9945$), and 93.5\% for query intent ($\kappa = 0.8958$).

\paragraph{Agricultural Image Data.} Farmer leaf photographs are classified on the device (WebAssembly and ONNX) and are never uploaded or stored remotely, which is a deliberate privacy choice. The held-out images used to measure vision accuracy (Section~\ref{sec:eval-fence}) are separate from farmer photographs. They come from open-access agricultural datasets: PlantVillage, the Bangladesh Rice Knowledge Bank, and Kaggle agricultural datasets under CC-BY and open research licenses.

\paragraph{Pesticide and Dosage Guidance.} The chemical-advice pathway fails closed. High-risk chemical requests and acute-poisoning language covered by the system's safety policy trigger a deterministic block and a helpline referral before any generation, and dosage claims in generated text are checked against source passages and permitted dose bands before display (Section~\ref{sec:system_verification}). These controls reduce risk but do not remove it. Guarded attack success is 10.71\% on the deployed model (19.0\% on Bengali-native attacks), and the dosage verifier missed 4 of 118 inserted errors (Section~\ref{sec:eval-safety}). We report these residuals and do not claim that the system prevents all unsafe output.

\paragraph{Helpline Referral.} Agronomic chemical inquiries are referred to the national Krishi Call Centre (16123), a government agricultural advisory service open 9:00\,AM--5:00\,PM, Saturday through Thursday. For acute chemical poisoning, self-harm, or medical crises, the referral interface escalates immediately to the 24/7 National Emergency Service (999) and local healthcare facilities, ensuring life-safety emergencies are never delayed by agricultural office hours. The public demonstration runs in a sandbox that shows referral and safety blocking for toxic-chemical queries without giving any chemical advice.

\paragraph{Third-Party Model Use.} The cloud benchmark uses a third-party API (\texttt{google/gemini-2.5-flash-lite}) under commercial enterprise terms with a zero-data-retention agreement, so prompts and responses are not logged, retained, or used for training. Cloud calls were limited to offline benchmark evaluation and never received farmer images or direct personal identifiers. De-identified farmer text queries, with personal identifiers removed as described above, were also sent to this API for the live-model gate comparison in Section~\ref{sec:eval-gate}. Self-hosted deployment runs entirely on the local \texttt{krishokchat-4b} model through \texttt{llama-server} and makes no external network calls.

\paragraph{Human Oversight.} KrishokTech is meant to support extension officers and not to replace them. Unresolved or unsafe cases end in a referral to a human expert instead of a generated answer. All results are scoped to the conditions in which they were measured (Section~\ref{sec:eval-scope}), and simulated or bounded components are disclosed as such and not presented as field-validated.
