## (b) Voice / accessibility norms for low-literacy users

**Voice-first is now the accepted norm for reaching low-literacy smallholders — and it is still rare:**

- Only **12% of 47** digital agri platforms offered voice interaction (systematic review, cited in FarmSaarthi study); but **78% of farmers use advisory services more** when voice input in their native language is supported ([FarmSaarthi paper](https://www.jetir.org/papers/JETIR2604936.pdf)).
- FarmSaarthi field study (120 farmers): vernacular **voice interface = 87.3% decision accuracy vs 62.1% for English text**, 43% faster task completion, SUS 82.4 — direct quantitative evidence that voice beats text for this user group (same paper).
- Farmer.Chat's own research: audio replies are essential for non-literate users; they translate query → English → LLM → back-translate for quality ([paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.08916v1)).

**Building blocks available today (all with Bengali support):**
- **Google Cloud Speech-to-Text** — supports **Bengali (Bangladesh) `bn-BD`** incl. `chirp_2`/`chirp_3` and long/short models ([STT docs](https://docs.cloud.google.com/speech-to-text/docs/v1/speech-to-text-supported-languages)).
- **Google Cloud Text-to-Speech** — Bengali voices exist but only as **`bn-IN` (India) locale** — no `bn-BD` TTS locale; Wavenet/Chirp3-HD premium voices ([TTS voices](https://cloud.google.com/text-to-speech/docs/voices)).
- **ElevenLabs** — Bengali TTS supported in Eleven v3 (74 languages incl. BEN); regional-accent adaptability claimed ([ElevenLabs Bengali](https://elevenlabs.io/text-to-speech/bengali), [languages help](https://help.elevenlabs.io/hc/en-us/articles/13313366263441-What-languages-do-you-support)).
- **Whisper / IndicWhisper** — multilingual ASR incl. Bengali; fine-tuned on 450h of agri speech: 8.1% WER in field conditions (Bengali 9.3%) ([FarmSaarthi](https://www.jetir.org/papers/JETIR2604936.pdf)).
- **Bhashini (India DPI)** — ASR for 22 languages (incl. a **Bengali ASR benchmark dataset**), NMT, TTS, and **VoicERA** for serving AI over phone networks incl. landlines; powers MahaVISTAAR (30 lakh+ farmers) and Farmer.Chat in India ([Bhashini](https://bhashini.gov.in/), [AIKosh use case](https://aikosh.indiaai.gov.in/home/use-cases/details/ai_powered_voice_assistant_for_farmers.html), [Science.org](https://www.science.org/content/article/india-s-national-ai-platform-tackles-country-s-many-tongues)). India-only; Bangladesh has no equivalent DPI.
- **Phone/IVR channel** remains the highest-reach low-literacy interface: 16123 call center (BD), Kisan Call Centre 1800-180-1551 (India), AgriPredict USSD *404# (Zambia), KrishokBondhu call-in RAG (BD). "Voice-first" here often literally means **phone-first, not app-first**.
- **Dialects are the frontier:** Maharashtra had to add tribal Bhili after user complaints (Science.org); Krishi Call Center agents admit they can only answer Dhaka-dialect calls (Dhaka Tribune); Agrinofy markets Chittagonian support. Bangla dialects (Chittagonian, Sylheti, Noakhali) are an untapped wedge.

**Implication:** voice in + voice out (and eventually phone/IVR) is table stakes for a credible low-literacy claim; Bangla-dialect tolerance is a differentiator no global player has.

## (c) Monetization & marketplace patterns (South Asia)

**Farmers themselves do not pay for advisory.** Consistent evidence:
- PxD (7.8M users, 9 countries): "farmers are typically unwilling to pay for advisory services alone; cost of collecting from individual farmers is prohibitive" — their commission-based B2B partnership pilots did **not** offset costs ([PxD/Swiss Re report](https://precisiondev.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/PxD-Swiss-Re-Foundation-Report-September-2022.pdf)).
- mKisan paid SMS/IVR subscription: ARPU only $0.89; 30% of trial users left over price ([GSMA](https://www.gsma.com/solutions-and-impact/connectivity-for-good/mobile-for-development/wp-content/uploads/2015/02/GSMA_HandygoCase_FinalProof02.pdf)).
- SE/South Asia: ~5% of 71M farmers use digital platforms, avg spend <$50/yr; direct-to-farmer CAC runs 3–5× higher than B2B/intermediary models ([Better Bioeconomy, Apr 2026](https://www.betterbioeconomy.com/p/agritech-didnt-fail-in-southeast)).
- India: SaaS subscription works with larger horticulture farms and with **corporate subscribers (insurers, seed/crop-protection companies)**, not smallholders ([Sathguru](https://www.sathguru.com/report/whither-agtech-in-india/Operational_Viability_of_Farm_Service_Providers_and_Input_Aggregators.html)).

**Models that actually generate revenue in this space:**
1. **Freemium app + B2B data/API** — Plantix: free consumer app monetized via Plantix Intelligence API and retailer ecosystem (Syngenta, Digital Green, ITC; 60k+ retailers) ([Plantix](https://plantix.net/en/plantix-intelligence/)); Agrio: free basic + paid satellite/alerts packages + API ([Agrio](https://agrio.app/Agriculture-API/)).
2. **Fintech-embedded (commission/interest)** — iFarmer: collateral-free cycle loans via partner FIs (4% vs 25% informal rates), input supply, 130+ collection centers, retail-investor marketplace (~$1k tickets); advisory is the acquisition hook, credit/inputs/market are the revenue ([UNESCAP](https://unescap.org/story/ifarmer-revolution-empowering-women-farmers-bangladesh), [MIT Solve](https://solve.mit.edu/solutions/96058)).
3. **B2B2C through intermediaries** — iFarmer "Sofol" field-agent model; AgriPredict agro-dealer listings + buy/sell matching; Digital Green's 54k government extension agents as distribution; Krishi Batayon's retailer/haats database.
4. **Government / donor funded (B2G)** — PxD (donors), Bhashini-backed state platforms (MahaVISTAAR), DAE/BARC apps, Krishi Call Center 16123 (gov budget, 25 paisa/min); blended finance (IFC/FMO/Rabo cornerstone LPs) is the capital layer ([Better Bioeconomy](https://www.betterbioeconomy.com/p/agritech-didnt-fail-in-southeast)).
5. **B2B sales leads inside apps** — ACI's IDSS/Fosholi (Bangladesh) charges ACI business units for sales leads, messaging, content-subscription management, and GIS analytics (€3.5M 2025 revenue target) while exploring freemium for farmers ([IDSS](https://g4aw.spaceoffice.nl/en/g4aw-projects/g4aw-projects/3/idss.html)).

**Implication for KrishokChat:** premium "saved history" is a thin wedge; the durable paths are (i) freemium knowledge + B2B (agro-dealers, extension officers, NGOs), (ii) government partnership (DAE/BARC/a2i — 16123 adjacency), (iii) commissions on input/market links at scale. The safety/audit-log engine is the least-commoditized asset for B2B/B2G trust stories.

## (d) Top 7 feature / UX opportunities (what competitors miss or do poorly)

1. **Voice-first Bengali interface (ASR + TTS, and eventually phone/IVR)**
   Rationale: voice measurably outperforms text for low-literacy farmers (87.3% vs 62.1% decision accuracy; 78% greater use with native voice input), and no Bangladesh product has it — gov apps (Khamari, Krishoker Janala, Krishi Batayon) are all text/picture-only, and Farmer.Chat/KissanAI don't serve Bangladesh.
   Sources: [FarmSaarthi](https://www.jetir.org/papers/JETIR2604936.pdf); [Google STT bn-BD](https://docs.cloud.google.com/speech-to-text/docs/v1/speech-to-text-supported-languages); [ElevenLabs Bengali](https://elevenlabs.io/text-to-speech/bengali); [KrishokBondhu proof-of-concept](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.18355v2).

2. **Grounded photo diagnosis + dosage verifier (safety layer as product)**
   Rationale: Plantix's documented weaknesses — single-disease assumption, non-local crops, unverified recommendations, no explanation of recommendation logic — are exactly what KrishokChat's verifier agent + audit trail solves; DAE's official pesticide-dosage guidelines (Balaynashok Nirdeshika) are the ground-truth corpus no competitor cites.
   Sources: [Plantix limitations (Nigeria study)](https://www.ajol.info/index.php/ijs/article/view/247309/233912), [functional evaluation](https://doi.org/10.56975/ijcrt.v13i12.298178); [DAE pesticide guidelines](https://dae.lakshmipur.gov.bd/en).

3. **Position as the AI front-end to the 16123 Krishi Call Center (triage + escalation, not competition)**
   Rationale: the government helpline is capacity-starved (180–200 calls/day, no 24/7, manpower shortage, dialect limits) — an AI layer that answers routine queries and escalates to 16123 human experts gives an instantly defensible B2G story and a demo-ready "AI + national helpline" integration.
   Sources: [Dhaka Tribune](https://www.dhakatribune.com/bangladesh/agriculture/345509/farmers%E2%80%99-friend-krishi-call-centre-struggling-to), [BSS](https://www.bssnews.net/special-stories/401845).

4. **Agent-trace UI + safety-metrics panel as the trust differentiator and B2B/B2G asset**
   Rationale: no competitor (Plantix, KissanAI, Farmer.Chat, Agrio) exposes pipeline decisions, claim grounding, or safety metrics — KrishokChat's stepper trace and audit log are both the poster story and the transparency the Plantix literature explicitly calls for; extension officers/NGOs can buy this as a compliance/audit product.
   Sources: [Farmer.Chat pipeline](https://github.com/digitalgreenorg/monorepo/blob/main/farmer-chat/README.md); [Plantix transparency critique](https://doi.org/10.56975/ijcrt.v13i12.298178).

5. **Dialect-aware input (Chittagonian / Sylheti / Noakhali) with a genuine dialect map**
   Rationale: dialect gaps are the documented #1 accessibility failure in both India (Bhili added on demand at MahaVISTAAR) and Bangladesh (16123 agents can't serve non-Dhaka dialects) — a real dialect map (KrishokChat already has a 110-word one) is defensible and unmatched.
   Sources: [Science.org](https://www.science.org/content/article/india-s-national-ai-platform-tackles-country-s-many-tongues), [Dhaka Tribune](https://www.dhakatribune.com/bangladesh/agriculture/345509/farmers%E2%80%99-friend-krishi-call-centre-struggling-to), [Agrinofy (Chittagonian)](https://agrinofy.com/ai-chatbot-for-farmers-by-agrinofy/).

6. **Offline-first PWA with cached advisories + image diagnosis queue**
   Rationale: Plantix's internet dependency and image-quality fragility are documented weaknesses; the proven Bangladesh pattern (Krishoker Janala offline pictorial, Folon offline modules) plus a PWA install path (OSS "Krishok AI" already shows Next.js 16 PWA viability) fits rural connectivity realities.
   Sources: [Plantix functional evaluation](https://doi.org/10.56975/ijcrt.v13i12.298178), [Krishoker Janala](https://www.krishokerjanala.gov.bd/about-app), [krishi-ai OSS](https://github.com/moniruzjaman/krishi-ai).

7. **B2B layer for agro-dealers and SAAOs (extension officers) — the missing middle**
   Rationale: monetization evidence says smallholders won't pay but intermediaries/corporates will (PxD, Sathguru, IDSS/Fosholi sales-lead model, Plantix Partner's 60k retailers); Bangladeshi SAAOs are already the primary app intermediaries (Krishoker Janala study: 64.2% "moderately effective" as an SAAO tool) — a dealer/SAAO dashboard with lead routing and compliance reports is the credible marketplace path.
   Sources: [Sathguru](https://www.sathguru.com/report/whither-agtech-in-india/Operational_Viability_of_Farm_Service_Providers_and_Input_Aggregators.html), [PxD](https://precisiondev.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/PxD-Swiss-Re-Foundation-Report-September-2022.pdf), [IDSS/Fosholi](https://g4aw.spaceoffice.nl/en/g4aw-projects/g4aw-projects/3/idss.html), [SAAO study](https://www.scirp.net/pdf/jss_2019031414520450.pdf).

### Quick takeaways for the investor demo
- **Nobody in Bangladesh does conversational AI advisory with safety verification.** The field is: gov reference apps (no AI), fintech-first (iFarmer), or research prototypes (KrishokBondhu).
- **Voice + dialect + offline** are the three accessibility claims investors will test; all three are buildable from existing APIs today.
- **The safety/audit layer is unique globally** (no Plantix/Farmer.Chat/KissanAI equivalent) — it is simultaneously the demo centerpiece, the trust story, and the future B2B/B2G monetization asset.
